## What

- **Instances (Keel step 8)** in `loft-gpu/src/lib.rs`:
  - `Loft::instance(device, queue) -> Result<Instance>`. An `Instance` has its own time, params and inputs, its own
    frame uniform, and a copy of every resource a `Frame` kernel writes. Each copy starts as the loft's resource is
    at that moment. Load state, `Every` histories and pipelines are shared.
  - `instance()` refuses, as `Usage` with the reason, three cases a copy cannot own: a live `Keep::Latest` stepper, an
    offscreen or shadow pass, or a view-sized `Frame` write. The fix it names is `encode_into` per instance.
  - `prepare(queue, enc, &[Instance])` runs every instance's `Frame` kernels in one compute pass, with timestamps 0/1.
  - `draw_subject(&mut RenderPass, canvas, &[Instance])` records the subject draws into the caller's pass. Each
    pipeline is set once, then each instance's group 0 and group 1 are bound and it is drawn.
  - `Instance::{set_param, set_input}(&loft, ...)` share the name lookup (`param_slot`, `input_slot`) with `Loft`'s
    setters. Bind groups go through one `group(device, layout, list, resolver)`, used by the loft, the steppers and
    the instances.
- **Trusted library modules (§4.3).** `shader_module` matches on `Module.origin`:
  - `Author` keeps every check;
  - `Library` uses `create_shader_module_trusted` with `bounds_checks` and `force_loop_bounding` off. Integer-division
    checks stay on. It carries a `SAFETY:` comment.
- **A safe `PipelineCache::open`.** It takes an exclusive `try_lock` on `dir/.lock` and holds it until drop. A second
  open is refused as `Environment` naming the lock. There is now no `pub unsafe` in loft-gpu.
- **`tools/check_pins.sh [KEEL_LOCK]`** compares `wgpu` and `naga` in `rust/Cargo.lock` with Keel's lock (default
  `~/git/room-runners-arena-lib/keel/Cargo.lock`). It fails on a mismatch or on a crate that resolves twice. A
  missing lock exits 2 and names the fix.
- **`docs/keel.md`** covers the contract, pins, steps 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8, and safety.
- **Tests** in `tests/it/instances.rs`:
  - a functional test: two instances in a game pass, each at its own input, the game's pixels between them kept,
    and the live-steps refusal;
  - an ignored bench.

Lines (scc code): loft-gpu src 1,125 → 1,322 (+197), instances.rs 282, check_pins.sh 22, docs/keel.md about 70 prose
lines. That is about 570 in total, under the ≤ 800 ceiling.

**Why the lamp boss itself was not run.** No V1 bundle of `wreckers_lamp` can exist yet: there is no program driver,
and the decoder refuses Draw and Pass decls. The check therefore ran on a stand-in with the same structure: a `Frame`
pose kernel over a 24-sphere table, and a subject quad sphere-traced through emit's clamped `Fetch` (48 steps × 24
fetches). The setup was five instances at 1280×800, over the game's frame. The timing is the GPU span from the first
begin to the last end, median of 60 frames, three runs:

| | Five instances, one pass | Five loads, five passes |
|---|---|---|
| GPU ms | 12.53, 12.74, 12.76 | 14.07, 14.40, 14.49 |

Instances save 1.5–1.7 ms (11–12%). I had predicted 0.4–0.8 ms, from four fewer attachment load/store round trips. The
miss is in the right direction, but the passes per load evidently cost more than the bandwidth alone.

How to rerun:

```
LOFT_BENCH_FRAMES=60 tools/dev.sh test loft-gpu --fast --run-ignored only -- five_instances --no-capture
```

Two timing lessons came out of the bench:

- Metal timestamps must be resolved after `poll` has waited for the frame. An earlier resolve read garbage, including
  negative spans.
- Summing pass durations double-counts on a tiled GPU, because passes overlap. Use the span instead.

**§4.3 A/B.** The arms were the same binary and the same artefacts, with every module checked against every module
trusted (bounds and loop checks off). It ran on today's `loft-loader` through `loft-draw still --frames 16 --wait` at
1280×800, t = 1.37, with arms alternated. The toggle was a temporary environment switch in loft-loader, which was **not
committed**: loft-loader is not this lane's file, and the cutover deletes it. Dawn's figure is `loft profile`'s least
of 16 at the same settings. All figures are GPU ms.

| Scene | Dawn | wgpu checked, least | wgpu trusted, least | Medians, checked → trusted | Gap to Dawn, checked → trusted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| wreckers_lamp | 4.52 | 4.955, 4.957 | 4.690, 4.692 | 4.99 → 4.72 (−5.4%) | +9.6% → **+3.8%** |
| dust_veil | 21.82 | 22.06, 22.08 | 21.80, 21.89 | 22.49 → 22.36 | +1.1% → **+0.2%** |
| volume_dust | 62.91 | 62.30, 62.45 | 62.00, 62.04 | 63.5 → 63.6 (noise) | −0.8% → −1.4% |

On the synthetic boss, trusted against checked is 0.70–0.71 (three runs). That shader is almost entirely fetches in
nested loops, which is where the checks cost.

**Verdict.**

- The runtime checks are most of the gap. Where a gap exists (the lamp, 9.6%), trusted modules close about 60% of it.
- dust_veil lands within ±3%.
- The lamp is 0.8 points outside ±3%.
- At this size, volume_dust has no gap.
- Next candidate, per the plan, for the lamp's remaining 3.8%: Metal's `mathMode`.

## Left

- **Run the real five lamp bosses** through `loft-draw` once the driver writes V1 bundles and loft-draw moves to
  loft-gpu at the cutover. The bench's two arms are the ones to copy: `draw_subject` against five `encode_into`.
- **The trusted path rests on two promises.** The bundle writer (the driver and 3.1) must mark any module that
  contains author WGSL, including `Op::Raw` text spliced into a draw module, as `Origin::Author`. Library templates
  index constant arrays by the host's own vertex counts: `corners[k]`, where k is below 6 or 36.
- **Instances do not cover:**
  - offscreen and shadow passes (a lamp's shadow map is refused today; a per-instance shadow would need per-instance
    targets);
  - `Latest` steppers;
  - draw order between instances for alpha-blended subject draws, which is per item and then per instance.
- **Not tested:** the cache lock, because Metal has no `PIPELINE_CACHE`, so `open` refuses before it reaches the lock.
  It needs a Vulkan run (lane 4.2's Linux runner).
