# Motion side by side: hand keys or gait vs `solve` (19 Nov 2026)

The owner asked whether hand-written motion or `solve` gives more natural motion. After the scope change, this round
compares only the two existing methods. `spring` and the effort preference were not built, pending a design audit.
Each subject is shown with the same camera (`square`), the same 512² size and the same clock, as two panels side by
side: left (a) hand, right (b) solve.

Scenes: `examples/motion/heron_hop_keys.ts`, `heron_hop_solve.ts`, `crab_walk_gait.ts`, `crab_walk_solve.ts`, and
`lib/crab.ts`. The heron's set (deck, rail, light, cameras) is `stage()` in `examples/second/heron_leap.ts`, now

## What each method is

**Hand (keys or gait).** Every curve is written directly as a function of time. For the heron, `keys` places a few
values per curve at the leap's beats: crouch at 0.35 s, takeoff at 0.6, the arc's top at 0.9, contact at 1.2, the
absorb at 1.4 and still at 1.8. `keys` joins them with a monotone cubic, so nothing overshoots a key. Body x, y and
pitch and the feet (x, y) each get 5 to 7 keys. Nothing checks that the flight is a parabola, that the legs are within
reach or that the feet clear the rail; the keys were placed so they do. For the crab (islandback's method), `gait`
plants the feet and swings them on arcs. The body rides the course frame at a fixed height, with a keyed bob
`0.09 cos(4πt/P)` and roll `0.04 sin(2πt/P)`, P = 2 s.

**Solve (least jerk).** Each unknown is a uniform cubic B-spline. The numbers minimise ∫ Σ (d³u/dt³)² dt subject to
the holds (augmented Lagrangian with Newton steps; `agent_notes/2026-11-18_solve/NOTE.md`). For the heron this is
heron_leap.ts unchanged. Its holds: standing still at 0, feet planted until 0.6, `accel = (0, -g)` from 0.6 to 1.2,
feet on the deck from 1.2, still at 1.8, legs within reach, and every part clear of the rail. **The crab had no solve
version; I wrote one** (`crab_walk_solve.ts`) with the existing API. The feet are the gait's, the same as the hand
panel. The body's carriage (x, height y, z, yaw) is 4 × spline(14) over 4 s, with these holds:
- the course's pose and speed at 0 s and at 4 s;
- every leg's root-to-foot distance between 0.5 and 0.95 of its span at all times.

## How each reads

**Heron.**
- **Hand:** reads as a cartoon jump. The crouch is where the animator put it (0.6 m down at 0.35 s). The flight is a
  monotone ease, not a parabola, so it hangs at the top and drops onto the deck. The body stays level and readable
  throughout.
- **Solve:**
  - The crouch is earned, not keyed: 0.77 m down at 0.40 s (CPU readout).
  - The flight is a true parabola, and the landing dips and recovers.
  - Over the rail, though, the bird pitches **67° nose-down at 0.925 s**, a dive that reads as wrong for a heron.
    Pitch is free and only clearance and jerk constrain it, so tipping the torso, neck and bill round the rail is the
    least-jerk way past.
  - The solve does what its holds say, physically right in the vertical and unnatural in attitude. A hold on pitch
    (e.g. `le(abs(c.pitch), 0.3)`) is the fix, at a cost of more authoring.

**Crab.**
- **Hand:** the keyed bob and roll give the body a small, regular pulse on the step.
- **Solve:** the solved carriage *is* the course. Measured on the CPU, the body is at most 3.7 mm off the arc, yaw
  within 5e-6 rad, and height constant at 2.50. No reach bound is active; the solve took 0.2 s.
- So the solve panel is the hand panel without the bob and roll: smoother and, because of that, deader. The feet step,
  but the body does not answer them.
- Least jerk over a fixed footing has no reason to move the body off a constant-speed path. Nothing in it carries
  weight, so the bob that loading would cause cannot appear.

## Owner feedback, and the diagnosis of (b) (measured on the CPU from the solved curves, `out/motion/heron_diag.ts`)

The owner on the heron: (a) is smooth but a little too linear; (b) has far too much recoil, is wildly springy and
exaggerated, and the head jolts violently toward the ground at about 0.9 s.

**Free unknowns.** Five B-splines (15 numbers each over 1.8 s, knot gap 0.15 s): body x, y and pitch, and the feet
fx, fy (both feet share them). There is **no neck or head unknown**. `lib/heron.ts` places the neck, bill and plume
`rigid(body)`, so the head goes wherever the body's pitch swings it. The bill base sits 1.70 m from the body origin.
At the solved pitch of -67° (0.925 s) it is **0.39 m below the hips**; standing, it is 1.38 m above them. That is the
1.77 m "jolt toward the ground".

**Which hold drives the pitch.** It is not the bill. Over the flight (0.6 to 1.2 s) the tightest bound is hold 24,
the clearance of the **lower leg** (ankle to foot) from the rail: margin 2.0e-4 at 0.965 s, which is active. Next are
hold 7, legs within 0.97 of their reach (3.8e-3 at 0.74 s), and hold 25, the toe bar (2.0e-2 at 0.965 s). Every
torso, neck and bill clearance has more than 0.76 m to spare.

The leg is IK from a hip fixed in the body frame, with its bend along -body.x. Pitching nose-down swings the hips
forward and up and turns the ankle's bend, which folds the lower leg up and over the rail. With jerk as the only cost,
that pitch is cheaper than moving the feet and hips further. The head, rigid to the body, is the casualty.

**Why least jerk gives a deep crouch and the recoil.** The family is C2 (uniform cubic B-spline), so acceleration is
continuous through the knots at 0.6 and 1.2 where the flight hold `accel = (0, -g)` starts and ends. That has three
consequences:
1. **Takeoff.** At 0.6 s the body must already be decelerating at exactly -g while its feet are still planted. The
   whole push must happen earlier and harder. Clearing the legs over the rail within reach of the feet makes the
   solve fly high (apex 2.93 m), so it leaves at vy = 4.74 m/s from y = 1.79 (below standing). To build 4.74 m/s from
   rest in 0.6 s, ending at -g, it crouches **0.77 m (to 1.135 at 0.40 s)** and pushes at **46.8 m/s² (4.8 g) at
   0.45 s**.
2. **Landing.** At 1.2 s it is still accelerating at -g (falling at 1.15 m/s, 0.26 m above standing). It cannot
   brake at once, so it sinks to 2.255 at 1.515 s, **0.345 m below standing on the deck**, and springs back up to
   2.60. That is the recoil: the dip is the C2 continuation of free fall, not a choice to absorb.
3. **The end.** The holds at 1.8 s fix position and velocity but not acceleration. The curve ends with
   ay = -18.4 and ax = -9.4 m/s², and the spline then holds still: a snap at the end of the hop.

## Panel (c): the hand keys as the prior (existing `solve` API only)

The `solve` API is unchanged. The bird gains a head: `heron()` takes an optional frame for the neck, bill and plume
(`headFrame(body, angle)` in `lib/heron.ts`, a turn about the neck's base). That is a rig change, not a solve feature.

Unknowns: x, y, pitch, fx, fy and `head` (the neck's turn from the body). Each is spline(27): a knot every 0.075 s,
so 0.6 and 1.2 are still knots and acceleration can ramp within 0.075 s instead of 0.15.
- **Prefer:** `near` the hand keys (head near -keyed pitch, i.e. level), weight 1, plus jerk 1e-7. The jerk/near ratio
  sets a crossover of (1/1e-7)^(1/6) = 14.7 rad/s: detail slower than about 0.4 s comes from the keys, faster detail
  is smoothed.
- **Holds:** heron_leap's holds (planted, the gravity flight, on the deck, still at both ends, reach, feet above
  ground, every clearance with the neck and bill in the head frame), plus:
  - |pitch| <= 0.35 rad (20°);
  - head level, `equal(pitch + head, 0, 0.03)`, i.e. within 1.7° of level in the world;
  - acceleration 0 at 0 s and at 1.8 s (no end snap).

**Predicted, before rendering:**
- crouch 0.55 to 0.70 m, at 0.33 to 0.40 s (the keys say 0.6 m at 0.35);
- takeoff vy 3.6 to 4.2 m/s; push peak 2 to 3 g;
- max |pitch| 12 to 16° (the keys' 12.6° crouch; the 20° bound not active);
- head within 1.7° of level throughout (the hold), and its least height above the hips at least 1.25 m (b: -0.39);
- landing dip 0.35 to 0.5 m below standing at 1.35 to 1.45 s, with less than 0.05 m of rebound above standing;
- solve time 10 to 60 s.

**How (c) reads.** It keeps the hand version's character and staging (upright body, the crouch where the animator
put it) and gains what the holds add: a real parabola (no hang at the top), a push that eases into takeoff, and an
absorb that settles without bouncing. The head floats level on a smooth arc while the body crouches and pitches under
it, which is the heron's head stabilisation. The "too linear" feel of (a) is gone in the flight, because the parabola
is forced; on the ground (c) is still close to the keys, as asked.

Two things carry the fix:
- **Giving the head its own unknown.** It is a rig change, and it removed the jolt.
- **Making the keys the prior instead of jerk.** That removed the dive and the recoil: the solve no longer goes high
  to fold the legs over the rail, because the keyed flight already clears it.

The finer knot gap (0.075 s) lets the acceleration ramp quickly at takeoff and landing, so the C2 continuity costs
little.

## Verdict for this round (a and b)

Neither method is natural on its own. Hand keys give the attitude and character but no physics. Least-jerk solve
gives correct ballistics and an earned anticipation where holds force motion (the heron's takeoff and landing). It
gives nothing where they do not (the crab's carriage), and it gives odd attitudes wherever a degree of freedom has no
hold.

The gap in both is the same: no mass and no load. That is what the deferred effort preference (load-dependent cost
would make the crab's body sink and shift onto its planted feet) and secondary springs were for. These two videos are
the baseline to judge them against.

Not measured: GPU frame time beyond the renderer's per-frame readout (about 2 to 3 ms at 512² for every panel; in both
methods the motion is a few per-frame scalars).

**After (c):** the useful form of `solve` for this hop is the hand keys as the prior plus the holds, not least jerk
alone. The animator keeps authorship of the look, and the solve guarantees the physics the holds state.

## Stepping from weight: the crab scramble (Spore-style feet)

The owner wants the crab's legs driven by weight distribution, not a fixed gait clock. The body moves right and then
suddenly hard left; the legs take the acceleration and step from wherever they are, never resetting to a gait phase.

**Built with what exists.** It uses `recur` (a list recurrence stepped on the GPU, baked once, read as a function of
time) and adds no public name. Scenes: `examples/motion/crab_scramble_gait.ts` (a) and `crab_scramble_steps.ts` (b),
with the body path in `lib/scramble.ts`; `crab()` in `lib/crab.ts` now takes the feet as an argument.

**Body (both panels).** Hand keys as closed forms of time (smootherstep S5, C2):
- path: z(t) = 1.8 S5((t - 0.2) / 1.7) - 3.2 S5((t - 1.7) / 1.8). Right to +1.8, reversing before it stops, and
  ending at -1.4.
- v and a are `derivative`s of z. Roll (lean into the acceleration) = atan(a / g). The body lags its ground point by
  0.08 a (the centre of mass shifting).
- No yaw. The legs on the side the body rolls toward compress through the existing three-segment IK.

**(a) Gait.** Today's `gait`, paced by distance: travelled = z(t), stride 2.1, duty 0.62, alternating tetrapods. When
the body reverses, the count runs backward, so the feet replay their steps in reverse.

**(b) Stepper.** `recur` over 8 feet at 120 steps/s. Lanes: (p, swinging), (swing start, s), (seconds planted, steps).
Per foot i, with h_i(t) its home under the body's ground point:
- strain_i = |(p_i - (h_i + v Tc)).xz|, with Tc = sqrt(H / g) = 0.505 s (H = 2.5, the body's height). This is the
  capture point of an inverted pendulum, carried to the foot's home: the faster the body moves, the earlier the foot
  is "behind".
- wants_i: the foot is planted, strain_i > R = 1.3, and it has been planted at least 0.12 s.
- lifts_i: it wants to, no neighbour (same side i±1, and the opposite foot) is swinging, and no neighbour wants to
  with more strain (ties go to the lower index). So at most alternating neighbours swing at once, and the 8 feet fall
  into two independent sets of four only when the strain says so.
- target_i = h_i + v Tl, Tl = 0.25 s (home ahead by about half a step), re-aimed every step of the swing, so a hard
  reversal redirects a foot in the air.
- swing: s += dt / 0.24 s; p = mix(start, target, smoothstep(s)) + up 1.0 sin(πs). The foot plants at the target
  when s reaches 1.
- A planted foot's position is copied from step to step.

Not built: the "centre of mass outside the support polygon" trigger. The capture-point term plays its part (it
fires on speed before the body leaves the feet).

**Predictions, before rendering** (`crab_scramble_steps.ts` checks measure the baked state):
- max lean **30.2° at 2.08 s**, read off the equation (the reversal's peak acceleration is 5.70 m/s²);
- first lift 0.6 to 0.9 s, in a set of up to four (not neighbours), with the other set within about 0.3 s;
- after the reversal, the feet lift within 0.2 s of v changing sign (about 1.9 to 2.1 s);
- steps in all 20 to 36: about 1.5 per foot in the right move and 2 in the left; per foot 2 to 5;
- planted slide **exactly 0**; neighbour pairs swinging together **0**;
- the gait panel takes about 2.4 cycles of steps (5.0 of path over a 2.1 stride), and in the reversal its feet visibly
  run their steps backward (a moonwalk);
- GPU cost: the bake is 480 steps × 8 entries, once at load (well under 5 ms). Per frame it is 8 × 2 fetches and a
  mix: below the GPU clock's 0.065 ms tick, and cheaper per vertex than the gait's closed form (floor, sin, two course
  frames per foot);
- scrubs: kept every step (8 entries × 3 lanes × 16 B × 481 states = 185 KB), so any time reads directly without a
  replay; no checkpoints are needed at this size;
- deterministic: gather-only steps in fixed order, no atomics. Exact on the GPU that baked; another GPU's f32 could
  flip a threshold comparison and shift a step by one tick.

**Measured.** From the baked state (`loft check examples/motion/crab_scramble_steps.ts`) and the profiler. Video:
`2026-11-19_motion/crab_scramble.mp4` ((a) | (b), game camera, 1024x512, 4 s). Sheet: `2026-11-19_motion/crab_scramble.webp`
(0 to 4 s).

| max lean | 30.2° at 2.08 s | **30.2° at 2.08 s** (into the reversal, to the crab's left) |
| first lifts | 0.6 to 0.9 s, the other set within about 0.3 s | **0.82 s**; the 8 first lifts at 0.82 to 1.20 s (0.38 s spread; missed, a little slow) |

On the first measurement the slide read 1.5e-6. That was `at`'s linear read between a swing's last step and the
landing step (a mixed flag counted as planted), not motion. A planted foot's lanes are copied bit for bit.

**Scrubbing and determinism.** Every state is kept (481 states, 0.21 MB), so any time reads directly, backward or
forward, with no checkpoint replay. At this size `recur` chooses no checkpoints; a 64 MB state would make it keep
every K-th state and replay at most K - 1. The step is gather-only, in fixed order, with no atomics, so the result is
exact on the GPU that baked it. A different GPU's f32 rounding near R = 1.3 could move a lift by one 1/120 s tick and
change what follows. It is a function of the scene's numbers and time only: a game cannot push it at run time (a
host input in the step is refused by `recur`).

**How they read.**
- (a) The gait's footfalls are a function of distance, so when the body reverses at 1.8 s every foot runs its own
  step backward: the moonwalk. Until the left move has covered a stride, the feet are where they were a stride ago.
- (b) The feet stand still until the body's speed loads them, then lift in non-adjacent sets of up to four, land a
  quarter-second ahead of the motion, and stay put. The reversal shows the method's character:
  - the feet that just landed to the right are now behind, so they lift and are re-aimed in the air;
  - the body rolls 30° into the turn, and the legs on the low side fold through the IK while the high side
    stretches;
  - it ends standing where it stopped, feet unevenly placed, not reset to a pose.
- (b) is livelier and reads as weight. Its weakness is uniformity: the lifts come in waves of the same 0.24 s swing
  and 1.0 lift whatever the speed. The next knobs are a swing time and lift that scale with strain, and a small
  per-foot seed in R.

Not done: the centre-of-mass support-polygon trigger, a yaw in the body path, and a step on the end standing pose (a
settle step when strain is between 0.5 R and R once the body is still).

**Verdict.** For the grab's legs, the stepper (b) is the right base: weight-driven, scrubbable, deterministic on one
GPU, cheaper per frame than the gait, and built from `recur` with no new public name. Two predictions (the lift
timings) were slow by about 0.1 to 0.2 s. The rest held to the number.

## Round 4: heron (d), gaze lock and neck IK; crab (c), swings shaped by need

### Heron (d) (`examples/motion/heron_hop_gaze.ts`)

**Body.** Panel (c)'s solve (`hop()` in heron_hop_near.ts, now parametrised by `Head`: `solved` for c, `placed` for
d): near the hand keys plus jerk 1e-7, the same holds, with no head unknown, |pitch| ≤ 0.6 rad (34°), and only the
torso and legs held clear of the rail (the neck and bill are placed afterwards).

**Head, placed per frame.** Position: a `recur` (1 entry, 120 steps/s) running a critically damped follower,
p'' = ω²(target − p) − 2ω p', ω = 2π·2 Hz. The target is shoulder + rest offset (0.5, 1.18) + shoulder velocity × 2/ω;
the velocity feed-forward cancels the follower's steady lag of 2v/ω. In the run-up (0 to 0.6 s) the target's x
holds where the shoulder will be at the end of each 0.2 s window, then jumps: hold, then thrust (the follower turns
each jump into a smooth thrust). Attitude: no roll, the bill pitched toward the look point (the landing spot until
0.9 s, blending to straight ahead by 1.4 s), clamped to −20..+12°.

**Neck.** A fixed length L = 1.55 between the shoulder and the head, drawn as an S of two circular arcs, each of
chord d/2 and length L/2, bulging opposite ways. The bend θ solves sin θ / θ = d / L, from θ₀ = √(6(1 − d/L)) by four
Newton steps, per frame and in closed form. A chord longer than L clamps at d/L = 0.999 and would show as length
error.

**Predicted, before running** (b and c are measured by the same finite differences on the CPU, d on the baked
follower):
- body pitch range: (c)'s, within ±2°. The 20° bound was inactive in (c), so relaxing it adds nothing; the keys prior
  holds the pitch. "The body may pitch more" needs a lower near weight or new keys, not a looser bound;
- head jerk over 0.6 to 1.75 s (flight and landing):
  - (b), with its 67° dive: above 5× (c);
  - (c): 150 to 400 m/s³ (the knots' acceleration steps);
  - (d): at most half of (c), since the follower filters the body's acceleration steps;
- head jerk in the run-up (d): large by design, above 1000 m/s³ at each thrust (a target jump Δx gives an
  acceleration step of ω²Δx);
- neck length error at most 0.1% (1.6 mm), mostly the 128-chord sampling; chord/L at most 0.92 (no clamp);
- bill clear of the rail axis by at least 1 m;
- per frame: (d) minus (c) below one 0.065 ms tick; the follower's bake at most 3 ms GPU once.

### Crab (c), swings shaped by need (`examples/motion/crab_scramble_varied.ts`; the stepper is now `lib/stepper.ts`)

Each swing's duration and lift are set at lift-off from its length l (foot to target) and its urgency u. u is the
capture point's distance from the planted feet's centroid, past 0.5 m, over 2 m, clamped to [0, 1]:
- seconds = clamp(0.30 + 0.03 l − 0.30 u, 0.12, 0.40);
- lift = clamp(0.7 + 0.25 l − 1.2 u, 0.25, 1.6).

So urgent steps are short and low, and relaxed ones longer and higher. (b) is the same stepper with fixed 0.24 s and
1.0.

**Predicted:**
- (b) after the refactor: the same 24 steps, lean, first lifts and slide (the rounding of dt/0.24 might move a lift
  by a tick);
- (c):
  - first lift still 0.82 s (the trigger is unchanged);
  - swing seconds spread 0.18 to 0.34 and lift 0.5 to 1.2, with the urgent (short, low) ones in the reversal;
  - steps 20 to 28;
  - slide 0, neighbours together 0.

Why (d) missed. The velocity feed-forward makes the follower's transfer ω²(1 + τs)/(s + ω)²: with τ = 2/ω its
high-frequency gain is 2ω/s, one order of attenuation instead of two. The landing's acceleration step (the C2
spline's jerk spike at 1.2 s) passes through as a velocity step, so (d) only matched (c).

τ = 1/ω halves that gain. It brought the landing down to 0.61× (c), at the cost of a steady lag of v/ω (about 0.3 m
in flight, chord/L to 0.961). Two weights were tried, as allowed; 1/ω is shown.

### Heron (d), gaze fixed (owner: the head started facing down and looked up at the end)

**Cause.** The gaze target was the landing spot, which sits below the head, until 0.9 s, blending to "ahead" only by
1.4 s. So the head pitched down at the start and came up late.

**Fix** (`heron_hop_gaze.ts`):
- The default gaze is level: a point ahead at the head's own height.
- In the run-up the head glances toward the landing spot, weighted by smoothstep(0.05, 0.25) × (1 − smoothstep(0.45,
  0.6)) and clamped to 15°. So it is level again at takeoff and stays level through the flight and the landing.
- The 0.6 s hitch: over the run-up's last window (0.4 to 0.6 s), the held target now blends into tracking by a
  smoothstep. Position and speed are equal at both ends, so the hand-off has no velocity step.
