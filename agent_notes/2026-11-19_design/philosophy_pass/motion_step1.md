# Who gets to move the foot?

At 0.9 seconds in Loft’s heron comparison, look at the bill. In the hand-keyed row it points across the rail. In the least-jerk row it points into the ground. The clearance that drove this difference was at the **lower leg**. At its closest approach the lower leg had 0.2 mm to spare. Every torso, neck and bill station checked during flight had over 0.76 m to spare. The hip and knee bend with the torso, so pitching the torso bought leg clearance. The bill went with it because the rig gave the head no independent motion. An animator asking to keep the bird’s attention across the rail would have found little help in a better collision tolerance. [Three-way comparison sheet](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/heron_hop_3.webp) · [Motion-branch measurements at `88e2fc5`](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

That edit is my test of a motion engine. Can the consumer give the bird a different intention, keep the rail and landing time, and see what else that change costs? In Loft’s terms the tiny core runs programs. The motion sub meta engine supplies ways to express, compose, schedule and examine motion. The heron’s choice of where to look belongs in a program an animator or model can rewrite. A two-page solver used by one bird can be a good bargain. A one-word switch that requires changing every bird to help this one is expensive. Access to an engine repository helps. It matters just as much whether an edited law can still use the same rig, compiler, diagnostics and playback path. [Philosophy §§0–3](../../../docs/PHILOSOPHY.md) · [Motion goals](../../../docs/design/motion.md) · [Project priorities](../../../CORE.md)

Two elementary problems recur. First, two crabs can arrive at the same position, speed and gait phase with different feet on the floor. One stepped on a rock that the other passed. Once the rock is behind them, no calculation on their common current inputs can recover which foot landed where. Something must remember. Second, two birds can satisfy exactly the same landing conditions while one studies a fish and the other watches the horizon. More accurate integration of gravity cannot supply the missing intention. In both cases a framework appears to save work by silently answering a question the author still needs to answer. The examples below distinguish reported failures from consequences one can derive for a Loft shot.

## Bodies invented after the engine ships

**Spore.** Its important invention was letting an animator state which relationship survives a change of anatomy. Taking fruit to a mouth has meaning on a creature whose grasper and mouth share no joint indices with the source. The animator could preview that relationship across creatures. For locomotion, Spore clustered legs of similar length, related their cycle rates by small rational ratios and mapped speed to duty and step timing. It used authored mappings for groups of one through six feet and generated styles for seven or more. Different groups could run different gaits. This bought extraordinary coverage for a game where the next customer could invent the next skeleton. The authors report about 0.2 ms per frame for a 25-body creature on a 1.7 GHz Pentium M, roughly 35% in IK. That is historical CPU time for their character. [Hecker et al., §§4–6](https://www.cmlab.csie.ntu.edu.tw/~ming/images/new_reference_papers_ICG/Proj_10_Sporeanim-siggraph08_Real-time%20Motion%20Retargeting.pdf)

The authors say the foot-flight editor and general retargeting machinery developed separately for historical reasons. Consider the cost when a crab presses its large claw against a ledge to haul itself up, then uses that claw to display the fruit. The contact must persist while the claw changes jobs. A movement mode can preserve the fruit’s relation to the claw. A gait can produce a plausible swing. Neither description settles when the claw can unload without dropping the torso. Spore’s IK also solves the spine before finishing limbs against it. A last-stage limb correction has consequently lost a way to move the torso. Keep semantic relationships and the preview across anatomies. Discard a fixed assignment of locomotion, manipulation and torso authority to separate machinery. That historical division earns no place in the definition of a limb.

**Overgrowth.** David Rosen’s pass and reach poses, stride wheel, tilt and crouch spring give a small team considerable range from a few drawings of the body. Using the crouch spring again on landing is good art direction as well as economy. A high fall and a short hop can share a leg response while the player retains immediate control. Wolfire even documents a script hook for final adjustments to a character’s bones. For the original team, writing the specific animal was precisely the advantage. The limit appears when a consumer inherits this as a general recipe. The ordering of movement, pose construction and final adjustment becomes part of the meaning of every edit. [Rosen’s GDC presentation](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/Rosen_David_Animation_Bootcamp_An.pdf) · [Wolfire’s character scripting interface](https://wiki.wolfire.com/wiki/Character_Scripting)

Take a simple transplant. A heron’s toe is planted while the artist asks for a five-degree anticipatory pitch. If the whole posed hierarchy rotates about a hip 1 m from the toe, the toe moves along a chord of length 2 sin(2.5°) m, about 8.7 cm. Correct the toe afterward and the knee has to absorb the displacement, assuming the leg can reach. Both versions use the same poses, angle and IK. The visible difference comes from who can edit the order and choose what yields. This is a geometric example, independent of Overgrowth’s measured behaviour. Keep the small repertoire of authored poses and explicit springs. Discard a reusable character stack whose last transform implicitly wins every dispute. An agent can write the bird’s crouch directly and leave the rabbit’s landing alone.

**Rain World.** The slugcat can tumble because its gameplay body has two spherical chunks joined at a fixed distance. Limbs can seek purchase and the visible animal can scramble through a space with much finer detail than those two colliders encode. Joar Jakobsson describes the extra work of scripting individual creatures as a price of the game’s biodiversity. Loft should take that seriously. An agent being able to write the next animal makes this choice more attractive. Forcing a lizard, slugcat and bird into a single configurable locomotion template would discard the achievement. [Developer account of the body and creature scripts](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world) · [Jakobsson and Therrien’s animation presentation](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023475/Animation-Bootcamp-Rainworld-Animation)

The error would be to promote the two-chunk bargain to a general contact model. In three dimensions two marked centres determine a line. Rotating a rigid body about that line leaves both centres stationary. A claw tip 20 cm off the line can move 28.3 cm in a quarter turn while a check on both centres reports exactly zero displacement. The screen silhouette, surface contact and transmission of weight can therefore demand information absent from the gameplay proxy. A decorative foot can find a convincing grip without exerting a force on that proxy. Keep the economy of a coarse game body and independently scripted appendages. Discard any inference from “the proxy is supported” to “this visible claw is bearing the load.” Give that claw physical authority only in the performance that needs it.

## A performed movement and the price of editing it

**Motion matching.** Given a large collection of performances, search for a frame that fits the current body and the desired future path, advance the take, then search again. This removed much of the hand work of wiring transitions in games such as *For Honor*. It also preserved acting that would be expensive to describe analytically. In Holden and colleagues’ example, a 27-component query contains future planar positions and headings at 20, 40 and 60 frames, local positions and velocities of two feet, and hip velocity. At 60 Hz the furthest query is a second ahead. That future is a steering prediction. A player can contradict it on the next frame. [Clavet’s production account](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023280/) · [Holden et al., §2.2](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)

Suppose a director asks for the same turn with the large claw still shading the crab’s eyes. Two frames could agree on all 27 coordinates and differ maximally on the claw. No choice of weights on those coordinates distinguishes them. Adding a claw feature is easy. Producing a take that contains the gesture, the entering support and the exit is the bill. Even storing just 27 uncompressed f32 features at 60 Hz costs 23.3 MB per hour of source, before pose data and search structures. Compression or a learned matcher can change that bill and make source-frame attribution harder. Keep retrieval wherever the recorded performance earns its cost. Discard the assumption that a nearer frame can answer an absent request. The consumer needs the selected take, features, splice and contact correction available individually. A scene with a single excellent turn should be able to play it directly.

**DeepMimic.** An example clip can teach a simulated body a flip with an intelligible takeoff, flight and landing. The policy sees the state and reference phase and issues joint targets for proportional-derivative drives. Reference-state initialization lets training visit the landing long before an inexperienced policy can get there from takeoff. Early termination discourages spending training time flailing after a fall. These are substantive decisions about which experience the controller gets. The humanoid in the paper has 34 degrees of freedom, 197 state features and 36 action parameters. The dragon has 79, 418 and 94 respectively. The work demonstrates several anatomies. The dimensions explain why a trained policy is a particular body’s asset. [Peng et al., §§5–7 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02717)

Now direct the dragon to finish the same landing with its neck yielding for two extra frames while its feet stay fixed. Editing the source clip changes the reference. Changing drive gains changes the physical response. Either may work, but neither says which joints will compensate after impact. That decision was learned jointly from the body, examples, rewards and training distribution. There is no corresponding pair of weights one can identify as “the extra two frames.” A correction applied to the rendered neck can make the next simulated state disagree with the picture. Keep imitation where the reference and disturbance recovery are the product. Discard a trained policy as the compulsory owner of all joints, including the right to determine when an authored beat ends.

**AMP.** Adversarial Motion Priors exchanges exact reference timing for a learned score of how much a simulated transition resembles example motion. A separate task reward lets the character reach a goal in an acquired style. That is valuable when ten acceptable ways to approach an object occur in the data. The paper also contains a sharper warning than a general objection to machine learning. In its ablation, removing explicit velocity features could produce a character holding a fixed pose on the ground during a roll. Consecutive poses carried some information about velocity, yet this particular discriminator failed to demand the motion the viewer cared about. The representation was an artistic decision. [Peng et al., §8 and velocity ablation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02180)

For Loft, a high style score could coexist with the one wrong toe during an otherwise beautiful landing. Raising the task reward for the whole landing couples a small local correction to every way the policy earns its return. Keep AMP’s ability to borrow style from unlabelled sequences and to discover ways of performing a task. Discard distributional resemblance as the authority on an exact hold. The consumer who asks for two tenths of a second of contact needs to see the contact point and interval even when AMP supplies every other channel.

**ASE.** Reusing a repertoire across tasks addresses a real shortcoming of training anew for each goal. ASE’s low-level policy takes a latent skill, and a higher-level controller chooses skills to accomplish a task. The authors trained the low-level policy with 4,096 environments in parallel on a V100, over ten billion samples, about ten simulated years in roughly ten days of wall time. The expenditure bought a repertoire for subsequent tasks. The paper also notes that a sequence of individually admissible latent codes can produce unnatural movement. The transition between skills is itself a motion decision. [Peng et al., training details and limitations](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/ASE/ASE_2022.pdf)

The consumer who wants to keep the first half of a guard and replace only its release must find a trajectory through that learned space or edit the resulting pose. A code’s numerical distance has no declared conversion to degrees of wrist rotation or centimetres of foot travel. An agent can cheaply write a release law in those units. Discovering the correct latent sequence and revalidating the rest of the body is a different expense. Keep amortized skill learning where many tasks use that repertoire. Discard a latent coordinate as the public definition of a skill. An external model’s performance should still be editable on the posed skeleton, with the altered support checked there.

**SuperTrack.** Fussell, Bergamin and Holden attack the expense of teaching a simulated character to follow a kinematic performance. They train a differentiable approximation to the simulator and backpropagate tracking loss through it to learn a control policy. The policy supplies offsets to joint-drive targets and runs with the physical simulator at use time. Ubisoft reports that after 40 hours on LaFAN data its policy could track 80% of motions beyond 30 seconds, against 5% for its PPO comparison with the same training time. That result measures survival under a tracking criterion. It gives no upper bound on the error of the hand in the last frame the director cares about. [Fussell et al.](https://staticctf.ubisoft.com/J3yJr34U2pZ2Ieem48Dwy9uqj5PNUQTn/OXYoOEqVwwtxSMiPzMIva/302e9a76da0b1f95ab62d6c766179868/SuperTrack.pdf) · [La Forge’s experiment report](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/7fMzaMaDgnd0gqPsCaJZYb/supertrack-motion-tracking-for-physically-simulated-characters-using-supervised-learning)

A tracker makes a reference physically playable. Someone else still has to decide which foot should step when a heron loses sight of its fish. If a consumer changes the beak mass or contact compliance, the policy now acts on dynamics different from those it experienced. How much validation it needs depends on the edit. Charging the training world model to each rendered frame would misprice the method. Charging zero for revalidating the changed body would also misprice it. Keep supervised tracking for bringing performances into physics. Discard the idea that possessing a fast tracker gives the consumer an editable stepping policy or an explanation for a missed beat.

**MotionBricks and other learned generators.** MotionBricks gives an object interaction an inspectable part. The author can bind intent keyframes to an object and distinguish a hard contact pose from softer guidance during approach. The generator supplies the movement between them. Its large training set lists 700 hours on a 27-joint representation. Both the learned movement and the authored binding do work. Moving the object can reuse the binding. Giving a heron the task of pulling the object with its bill changes which endpoint can reach it, which feet must brace, and the allowable neck rotation. Rewriting the binding is cheap compared with acquiring representative performances for a different kind of body. [Wang et al., §§6–7 and Table 2](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/motionbricks/pdfs/motionbricks_siggraph_2026.pdf)

There is a temporal problem even on the same body. A hand can meet a handle at both authored keys and pass through it between them. Over a 0.4 s interval, an additional displacement of 2 cm times sin²(πt/0.4) is zero with zero velocity at both ends. It slips by the full 2 cm halfway through. This constructed example explains why a hard key, a predicted contact label and a held grip establish different facts. NVIDIA’s Kimodo documentation makes the issue practical. It describes raw foot skating and inexact constraints and recommends post-processing when contact matters. Tevet et al.’s Human Motion Diffusion Model similarly makes joint positions, velocities and foot contact terms available during training by predicting the clean sample. A loss discourages a defect across examples. A director still has to examine the delivered shot. [Kimodo limitations](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/key_concepts/limitations.html) · [Tevet et al., Human Motion Diffusion Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14916)

Keep these generators as sources of motion. Consumers have a good reason to run them elsewhere and bring back joint tracks, timing and contacts. Discard the arrangement in which changing one planted interval means editing a prompt and resampling a whole performance. After retargeting and correction, the pose sent out to another tool should be the same one that Loft draws. Giving a consumer only the original generated clip would lose precisely the edits they asked to keep.

## The mechanics an engine usually owns

**Euphoria.** A character already falling can try to regain balance or protect itself when another collision arrives. A hand-authored reaction clip would require an entering pose, direction, velocity and contact configuration for every plausible interruption. Simulating an actively driven character let NaturalMotion carry these across events. In its developer interview the team describes directing behaviours through parameters, supplemental animation, extra forces and stronger constraints. Those are genuine controls. They also identify the cost of making production behaviour a middleware service. A director may end up pushing against a controller whose own decision about yielding is the thing they wanted to change. The public account describes the integration experience. It cannot establish what a particular licensee could change internally. [NaturalMotion developer interview](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)

For a boss struck twice during a recovery, record the points of application. A 10 N·s sideways impulse with a perpendicular lever arm of 0.4 m contributes 4 N·m·s of angular momentum. The same impulse through the centre contributes zero. An interface carrying only a direction and strength cannot distinguish the two. Even with the correct input, the boss can answer by yielding a shoulder, taking a step or stiffening for a directed pose. Keep history-dependent active response and its ability to survive interruption. Discard behaviour selection plus corrective forces as the only way to author that choice. The local drive, the condition that releases the foot and the state after the first hit are the pieces an agent needs to change separately.

**Unreal Control Rig.** Epic exposes a great deal more than pose playback. A graph can execute a forward solve, manipulate controls and use modular rigs. Full-Body IK offers a position-based method with effectors, preferred angles, bone limits, stiffness, root behaviour and an iteration budget. A rigger can build a different graph, a custom unit or a specialized rig. The burden appears at the boundary of a useful node. Asking the foot to plant until a particular shoulder reaches a limit joins a contact event to an internal solver decision. Effectors and stiffness inputs conveniently specify a destination. Discovering why that destination yielded, then changing just that priority, can require rebuilding the unit or restructuring the graph. The editor serves animators who need direct manipulation, previews and familiar reusable controls. Those are good reasons for its existence. [Epic’s Full-Body IK reference](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/control-rig-full-body-ik-in-unreal-engine) · [Modular Control Rigs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/modular-control-rigs-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)

Consider conflicting effectors on a fully extended limb. With a fixed hip and a 0.9 m leg, a target 1.0 m away leaves at least 10 cm of endpoint error. Increasing iterations can spend more time establishing the same geometric fact. Moving the hip, releasing the plant and allowing stretch are three different performances. Keep visual tooling and editable graphs. Discard a public interface that offers only tuning knobs at the point where the rigger needs to name which promise gives way. Editing that rule should cost an ordinary function and a new picture, with the same diagnostics that a shipped node enjoys.

**MuJoCo.** Articulated inertia, actuator models, contact Jacobians and forward and inverse dynamics are worth borrowing for a creature that needs force-consistent interaction. MuJoCo is open source, exposes state and intermediate quantities, and supports control and actuator callbacks. Its documentation is unusually explicit about the trade in soft contact. There can be slow slip in a nominal stance. A NoSlip post-process can suppress it, at the cost of giving up properties of the original model, including its inverse-dynamics treatment. Timestep, solver and friction representation matter. A contact-rich robotics simulator has good reasons to make these decisions together. [MuJoCo modelling guide, contact slip](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html) · [Computation and simulation pipeline](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.1.1/computation/index.html)

The lock-in would be requiring every Loft motion to enter that world and advance its clock. A keyed blink evaluated at 1.2 s has no integration history. A braced foot in a physically simulated shove has one, including the impulses that brought it there. An illustrative 1 kHz simulation needs 1,200 ticks to seek from rest to that time without a checkpoint. A finite spline needs a span lookup. Conversely, reading a spline at the time of an unannounced shove cannot reveal the impulse. Keep MuJoCo where its dynamics answer the question. Discard a simulator-owned world, timestep and contact law as the entry fee for making anything move. A rendered foot on a hero shot may warrant exact geometric placement while another actor is allowed to slip.

**Position-based dynamics.** Predict positions, project violated constraints and recover velocities from the corrected motion. This makes direct position editing and collision correction practical. It also puts much of the apparent material in the numerical schedule. For an isolated scalar error, if each projection removes half the remainder, one sweep leaves half the error and eight leave 1/256. Real coupled joints reintroduce errors in each other. XPBD introduces compliance scaled by the squared timestep and an accumulated multiplier to give stiffness a physical interpretation. Scheduling still changes the picture. In *Small Steps*, one substep with 30 XPBD iterations stretched visibly in a cloth example at 12.4 ms/frame. Thirty substeps with one iteration stretched less at 13.5 ms/frame. Those are cloth results on the authors’ setup. [Müller et al., PBD](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/posBasedDyn.pdf) · [Macklin et al., XPBD](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf) · [Small Steps, Figure 1](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/smallsteps.pdf)

For a bird holding a perch, a final neck correction can disturb a previous contact correction if both move the trunk. Grouping independent rows for parallel execution, choosing row order and deciding which mass may move all have visible consequences. A checkbox marked rigid or an iteration count cannot define the outcome. Keep projections, compliance and multipliers as readable numerical methods. Discard the practice of treating a completed sweep as proof of a plant. Check the residual at the drawn endpoint after the last writer. Where a two-link analytic construction exactly supplies the required shape, the consumer should be able to use it without paying for a general island solve.

**Spacetime constraints.** Witkin and Kass solved for motion and actuation over a whole interval. A jumping lamp can anticipate its landing because the landing is part of the problem before takeoff. This is a powerful way to make sparse direction do mechanical work. It gives expensive decisions a natural home at build time. The price is that the objective, feasible family, contact assumptions and initial guess decide what happens in all the time the animator left unspecified. Start a local search with a leg on the wrong side of an obstacle and a small descent step cannot move it through the obstacle to a different feasible family. Smoothness and effort also disagree about which motion looks heavy. [Witkin and Kass, *Spacetime Constraints*](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/pub_files/pub1/witkin_andrew_1988_1/witkin_andrew_1988_1.pdf)

There is a sharper limit for live play. A clip optimized with foreknowledge of a collision can coil just before it. Played as a reaction to an unforeseen game event, the same anticipation tells a lie about cause and effect. Keep the whole-clip solve for known beats, its objective exposed alongside its constraints. Discard evaluation of every action as a complete future trajectory and the belief that feasibility plus a low objective constitutes an artistic verdict. An edited landing deadline can justifiably change the entire approach. An unannounced hit cannot change frames the player has already seen.

## Direction, perception and the evidence inside Loft

**Disney principles.** Anticipation lets the viewer read intention before displacement. Overlap lets the eye infer that two connected parts carry different weight. Staging can require one part to go absolutely still while the rest settles. Lasseter’s account of translating these practices to 3D emphasizes timing, spacing and what the audience perceives. These are questions an animator asks of a picture. Packaging each answer as a global effect makes the judgment inaccessible. At 24 fps a three-frame hold is 125 ms. Applying a plausible damped follow-through to the eyes during those three frames can erase the stare that makes the following recoil funny. The particular damping ratio is beside the point. [Lasseter, *Principles of Traditional Animation Applied to 3D Computer Animation*](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~animation/Lasseter_1987.pdf)

Loft already has a better piece of practice here. Its struck-pose function gives the face an exact unit weight through a hold, while an ornament can use time since arrival to keep swinging. The score order is visible, and the helper that examines stillness describes its sample rate. The Titan’s brow uses 14 stations per antenna with delays and a separate response at impact. Loose parts already move in Loft. The difficult edit is to change the parent’s performance and choose which parts should inherit the new timing, which have their own cue, and which should stay still. Keep the principles as a vocabulary for reviewing a shot. Throw out a uniform “add life” pass. A deliberate dead stop has as much claim on the library as a spring. [Struck pose and sampled check](../../../src/geom/struck.ts) · [Titan brow](../../../examples/bosses/titan/face/brow.ts) · [Motion audit](../../2026-11-19_audits/motion_audit_astra.md)

**Bird gaze stabilisation.** This is observational evidence for what to animate. The error belongs to the engine that reduces it to a head-bob oscillator. Troje and Frost found that in walking pigeons the head stays remarkably stable during the hold phase in measured translations and rotations, with a small systematic slip. The body advances under it, then the head thrusts forward. The reported frequency varies with walking speed, around 3–8 Hz. A separate moving-perch experiment perturbed standing birds in three translational and three rotational directions, in light and darkness. A pigeon walking or balancing on a perch supplies evidence about distinct tasks. Applying its timing constants to a hopping heron is an artistic hypothesis. [Troje and Frost](https://www.biomotionlab.ca/Text/TrojeFrost00.pdf) · [Moving-perch experiment](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00551/full)

A head can stay level while its sightline misses a fish. An eye can aim exactly at the fish while the skull rolls around the sightline. The neck can also run out of length. For a fish initially 0.5 m straight ahead of the eye, moving the eye 5 cm sideways requires a bearing correction of about 5.7°. Holding the old angle cannot fixate it. Loft’s current look-at returns clamped yaw, pitch and direction. It has no memory of the previous hold and no authority to ask the torso to turn when the neck limit binds. The gaze look round made the separate artistic decision visible. A 1.55 m neck could bend while the head tracked the landing spot, yet the owner saw a bird looking down for too long. Changing the *target over time* gave a 15° glance at 0.250 s and level pitch from takeoff at 0.6 s. Smoothing the handoff reduced measured peak head jerk over 0.6–1.75 s from 670 to 428 m/s³. Keep fixation, skull orientation, reach and release independently editable. Discard a single stabilize strength. [Current look-at](../../../src/geom/motion.ts) · [Gaze comparison sheet](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/heron_hop_gaze.webp) · [Round 4 measurements](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

**Loft’s walk and scramble.** On main at `80b2d8e`, a foot’s integer cycle identifies a world-space plant sampled from the course at mid-stance. This is an elegant way to walk a known curve. The outside feet naturally travel further around a bend. But horizontal smoothstep, half-sine lift and fixed phases embody choices about the foot’s arrival. At 0.12 m lift, period 0.8 s and duty 0.66, swing lasts 0.272 s. On level ground the half-sine arrives with a downward velocity of 1.39 m/s and stance immediately sets it to zero. This sells a stamp. A stalking bird would have to change the law. The stance blend mixes foot positions while switching the planted flag at a threshold. The leg’s analytic IK can also clamp an unreachable target. These are three possible writers of a foot. A report about the first cannot certify the third. [Gait](../../../src/geom/gait.ts) · [Stance](../../../src/geom/stance.ts) · [Leg construction](../../../src/geom/limb.ts)

The branch comparison held the crab’s feet to the same gait on a 6 m radius turn and solved the carriage. It stayed within 3.7 mm of the arc at constant height 2.50. Every leg-reach constraint had slack. The solved body lost the hand version’s small roll and bob, and the author judged it deader. There was no load transfer in the objective. In the separate lateral scramble, feeding signed displacement into the gait simply ran the phases backward on reversal. The alternative remembered each of eight feet at 120 Hz and chose liftoff from displacement relative to a velocity-led target and neighbouring feet. This change was ordinary scene code. Its specific weaknesses became editable too. Velocity reversed at 1.792 s and the first new lift came at 2.150 s, missing the predicted 0.2 s limit. Fixed swings gave exactly three steps per foot. Letting urgency change the swing produced 23 total steps and durations from 0.120 to 0.348 s. [Turn and scramble measurements](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md) · [Scramble sheet](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/crab_scramble_3.webp)

What did that cost? The fixed-swing bake kept 0.21 MB and took 3.1 ms GPU for 480 steps, plus a reported 309 ms of pipeline setup. At 512² and one selected pose the least of 60 frames was 0.59 ms versus 0.66 ms for the gait, a difference of about one timestamp tick. It establishes cheap playback for this known trajectory. The input path was baked, the body lean prescribed, zero slide measured on stored foot positions across fully planted samples, and the support-polygon trigger was never built. Calling it live balance would hide four distinctions the author paid to uncover. Throw out compulsory phase and the claim that zero sampled target motion establishes load-bearing contact. Keep both the cheap course and the scene’s ability to replace its foot law. [Baked recurrence and refusals](../../../src/map/recur.ts) · [Scramble timing and test conditions](../../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

Read the actual varied-scramble call. An author selects `{ kind: "varied" }` and passes the stored foot positions to the drawing. The interesting work is a few lines lower down in the example library. The stepper stores three vec4s per foot, knows about two rows of four, and finds the opposite neighbour by adding four modulo eight. Its diagnostic independently encodes the same numbering. Give this crab six feet and changing the list of homes alone leaves both the release decision and the check wrong. That is a specific cost of treating this example as the motion sub meta engine. It is also an argument for leaving this excellent eight-foot implementation available to copy. The state needs 384 bytes per tick for the eight feet before the clock and layout. The topology assumption occupies a few expressions. Rewriting those expressions is cheaper than requiring every possible animal to configure the same gait. A body description earns its place where it lets the controller and its checker agree on which contacts actually exist.
