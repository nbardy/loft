# What a Loft author can change about a picture

Render and light, Step 1 · 19 November 2026

Put a brass ring on a table, shine a lamp through its hole, and photograph the patch of light on the wall. An author replaces the ring's triangle mesh with an analytic torus. Three people can give three different answers about whether the edit worked. The modeller sees a round silhouette at close range. The lighting artist sees a polygonal hole in the shadow. The person picking the ring clicks through the brass at a grazing angle. Each subsystem can be working as designed. The defect is that the author changed what the ring *is*, while the engine allowed them to change only what one camera sees.

That is a useful reading of Loft's claim to be a meta engine. The small stable part gives programs a mathematical representation, compiles them and runs their GPU work. The render and light sub meta engines give authors ways to compose visibility, shading and transport. A particular answer to the ring problem belongs in an engine written with those pieces. A game may deliberately keep a coarser shadow. The question is who can make that decision, what else they have to edit, and how they discover which shape actually blocked the light. Source availability helps. A source fork maintained across renderer updates is still an expensive way to change one intersection. [Loft philosophy, §§0–3](../../../docs/PHILOSOPHY.md) · [Render goals](../../../docs/design/render.md)

The relative costs have changed. Writing a ray equation, sampling a light or sorting a work queue is within reach of the consumer. Finding every place that assumes a triangle ID, a particular light list or yesterday's camera depth remains difficult, even for an excellent programmer. In the comparisons below, published measurements belong to their authors and workloads. Calculations and hypothetical scenes illustrate consequences. Loft's figures come from existing runs. The priority order matters here. A frame that misses the light through the ring has already lost on picture, regardless of its GPU time. [Loft's scoring order](../../../CORE.md)

## Engines that own the frame

**Unreal 5, Nanite.** The original Nanite visibility buffer let Epic rasterize geometry once per view, identify the winning triangle and evaluate its material afterwards. Streaming and selecting clusters at screen-appropriate detail addresses a genuine production cost. An artist can use a dense scan in many different shots without authoring a separate mesh for every viewing distance. Epic even changed the rasterizer for small triangles. The data prepared for that fast path, including the identity needed to recover attributes, is the price of admission. A mathematical surface can be tessellated to enter it. That gives a cluster selector discretion over triangles but gives the author no corresponding place in the triangle test to supply the exact torus root. Epic also documents overdraw when closely stacked surfaces defeat occlusion and when aggregate geometry fails to simplify like a continuous surface. The cost depends on the asset's structure even at the same screen coverage. Discard the presumption that the geometry which earns this machinery on a cliff also earns it on the ring beside the cliff. Keep the cluster builder, visibility buffer and small-triangle rasterizer available to the engine that wants them. [Karis, Nanite deep dive](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf) · [Epic's content and overdraw guidance](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content?lang=en-US)

**Unreal 5, Lumen.** Epic budgets about 8 ms for GI and reflections together at 1080p internal resolution at its Epic console setting. Screen traces, alternate scene representations, and cached lighting make dynamic diffuse bounce affordable at that budget. The Surface Cache initially uses 12 capture cards per static mesh. Epic shows uncovered areas in pink in the diagnostic view. In the picture they give no bounce and appear black in reflections. One documented remedy is to split a complex mesh. An asset author fixing an integral over incoming light ends up changing the partition of the asset. Screen traces can conceal a difference between the visible mesh and what rays intersect until the relevant geometry leaves the image. Increasing the card count can fix coverage. It cannot express “for this hit inside the ring, evaluate this material and trace to the lamp.” The cache, screen trace, fallback geometry and material evaluation each answer a different physical question. Discard the coupling that makes changing the answer for one surface an asset rebuild or an engine modification. Epic's diagnostic view is worth inheriting precisely because it exposes which representation gave the bad answer. [Epic's Lumen technical details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)

**Unity HDRP.** A Custom Pass can draw real geometry and access real buffers. Its position in HDRP determines what that geometry can influence. In HDRP 17, content drawn at BeforePreRefraction can enter the colour pyramid used for rough refraction. At BeforeTransparent the author can sample that pyramid, but objects drawn there are absent from it. AfterPostProcess, Unity documents jitter on objects tested against depth. These are intelligible consequences of building the pyramid once and applying temporal processing at a particular moment. Consider two panes, one etched and one clear, through which the other should be visible. Moving both into a different injection point changes which previously drawn image they read. It never creates the missing second view or the second refracted segment. A custom Scriptable Render Pipeline can choose a different frame, but the rewrite has then crossed from authoring an effect into owning the pipeline. Discard the list of insertion points as the limit of composition. The reusable idea is being explicit about which version of a resource a draw reads. That gives an author enough information to choose an approximation for the two panes. [HDRP 17 injection points and buffer access](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Custom-Pass-Injection-Points.html)

**Godot.** A Godot compositor effect can issue GPU work through RenderingDevice on the render thread. The compositor is available on Mobile and Forward+, and its request for a normal/roughness prepass buffer applies only to Forward+. Consider a woodblock print that inks creases in a smooth face. Depth alone cannot recover the artist's perturbed shading normal. A shader copied to Mobile can therefore require a new source of data even when the device could calculate the normal cheaply from the author's face equation. These distinctions make sense for Godot's renderer choices, particularly the expense of producing and storing additional attachments. For Loft the cost is that the decision to compute a normal for this face has grown to a choice about the renderer for the viewport. Discard that scope of decision. Keep the ability to request a resolved texture and pay for it only where it is consumed. A missing input should identify exactly which effect or object needs another calculation. [Godot's compositor guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/compositor.html) · [CompositorEffect resource requests](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_compositoreffect.html)

**Bevy.** Bevy is unusually candid about how to put a new draw into an existing opaque phase. Its example extracts a component into the render world, queues a phase item, specializes a pipeline, associates visibility and issues a render command. A lower-level node is also available. Each mechanism solves a real problem when thousands of entities change independently and many similar draws can be batched. Now take one triangle whose custom fragment test locates a curved surface. The fragment equation is the small edit. Joining the opaque phase requires agreeing with the batch keys, depth state, resources and view selection. Success there still says nothing about a shadow pass dispatching the same test. An author bringing their own device and a list of parts pays in concepts before they can find out if the new surface looks good. Discard the requirement that the render world's entity lifecycle mediate an independent draw. Keep phase sorting and batching as inspectable algorithms, particularly where the author can show that a different binning rule preserves the picture and lowers frame time. [Bevy's complete custom phase item](https://bevy.org/examples/shaders/custom-phase-item/)

**three.js.** In the WebGL renderer, patching a built-in material in `onBeforeCompile` lets a few lines change a highlight. Those lines depend on generated shader text, and a change that alters the program can require the matching custom program cache key. The callback also does not survive normal material cloning or serialization. The WebGPU renderer uses node materials and TSL to generate for multiple backends. Its migration guide requires porting ShaderMaterial, RawShaderMaterial and these patches, as well as replacing EffectComposer passes. Both designs have a reason. One keeps the existing shader infrastructure cheap to customize, the other makes shader construction portable. Together they show how fragile a “few lines” metric is. A Fresnel function whose mathematics survived the backend migration can still require a new authoring representation, cache identity and post stack. Discard textual surgery on engine internals as the durable unit of reuse. Keep three.js's ease of placing an object next to an imported mesh and TSL's ability to share expressions. The cost to watch is whether the author's equation still means the same thing on the next renderer. [WebGL material hooks](https://archive.threejs.org/docs/api/en/materials/Material.html) · [three.js renderer migration guide](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/webgpurenderer)

**Babylon.js.** Babylon's manual shows a distorted cube casting the wrong shadow. Its main material displaced the vertices. The default shadow shader drew the old shape. Babylon exposes a real repair, ShadowDepthWrapper, which can derive a shadow shader from the material. It also shows a lighter dedicated material for fragment rejection. This is a sensible optimization. A shadow map needs the deformation and coverage calculation, and can usually omit the expensive colour calculation. The cost of two separate programs is semantic bookkeeping. Edit the cutout rule in just the lighter shader and the silhouette and shadow diverge again. Further independent consumers, such as a picking pass, need the same decision. Discard the rule that each observing pass is responsible for reconstructing the object's shape from its material. Keep the option to run less work for depth, with the omitted work and the source of the coverage decision visible. Babylon's side-by-side images make a better acceptance test for a new primitive than a screenshot of the primitive alone. [Babylon's incorrect and corrected shadow examples](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/lights/shadows)

**Filament.** Filament grants more control than a menu of PBR presets would suggest. In a lit material, `surfaceShading` can compute a custom response for each relevant directional, spot or point light. The inputs include processed roughness and light visibility. Alpha handling happens elsewhere. That boundary is excellent for drawing a toon band that tracks all the renderer's lights. Try drawing one sample from a distribution over those lights instead. By the time the hook receives a light, the renderer has already decided how to enumerate lights and supply visibility. Returning zero for nineteen out of twenty lights still invokes the hook under the existing enumeration and does not supply a sampling probability. The original bargain buys a coherent mobile material library, predictable lighting and shared preprocessing. Discard a per-light callback as the outermost unit an author can replace. Keep the documented material maths and conventions. A new scattering lobe, a new way to choose lights and a new shadow test are three independent edits with different costs. [Filament's custom surface shading contract](https://google.github.io/filament/main/materials.html)

**Frostbite.** Two Frostbite contributions show where to draw the boundary. FrameGraph describes resource use and pass dependencies, which allows lifetime analysis and reuse of temporary storage. Hillaire's volumetric work brought particles and large-scale fog under common extinction and lighting, with volume grids and volumetric shadows. A graph can correctly schedule every pass while the chosen storage still loses the picture. Imagine a narrow Gaussian ribbon crossing a sunbeam. Projecting it onto a coarse volume grid spends resolution on empty space and can smear extinction across the beam edge. Doubling resolution on all three axes multiplies the cell count by eight. The continuous Gaussian has a ray integral regardless of the grid spacing. Particle voxelization earns its cost when many different effects share a volume whose lighting is expensive to evaluate. Discard compulsory conversion into that storage in exchange for common lighting. The common quantity worth inheriting is the light transmitted along a segment. Frostbite's frame graph can schedule an analytic evaluation just as legitimately as a grid integration if both are presented as passes. [O'Donnell, Frostbite FrameGraph](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024612/FrameGraph-Extensible-Rendering-Architecture-in) · [Hillaire, unified volumetric lighting](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/)

**id Tech.** Doom 3's released stencil-shadow code makes the dependency inspectable. Given triangles and their silhouette edges for a light, it constructs shadow-volume geometry. Stencil tests give sharp dynamic occlusion on the hardware of the time. A ray equation for a deforming ring supplies neither a mesh edge list nor the extruded faces. A broad area light supplies many point-light visibility questions and a penumbra that one such volume cannot encode. The shape and light are simple, but getting them into that lighting path requires surrogates or a different algorithm. A later generation made a different strong bet. The published minimum PC specification for the id Tech 8 game Doom: The Dark Ages requires hardware ray tracing. A team owning the title can trade hardware reach for a lighting budget and image it can count on. A Loft example engine deserves that freedom too. Discard transferring a title's chosen visibility mechanism or device floor to every consumer of the library. The inheritance is the willingness to make a decisive choice for a particular game and actually ship it. [Doom 3 shadow-volume implementation](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3/blob/master/neo/renderer/tr_stencilshadow.cpp) · [The Dark Ages PC requirements](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24191398/prepare-for-doom-the-dark-ages-with-our-pc-specs)

## Engines written for one picture

**The demoscene.** Mercury's hg_sdf publishes the actual functions used in its 64k intros. A fold, a bevel and a primitive compose as GLSL. The author can change the field on either side of a join and read the resulting code. Mercury even records an operator revision that preserved the surface while changing the field inside. That distinction matters if a second author uses the interior to place particles or bound a step from inside the solid. Sphere tracing relies on a conservative distance bound. An unconstrained warp can make the next step cross a thin feature. Reducing all steps until one camera stops missing it increases every affected ray's work and establishes little about another angle. A hand-tuned intro can afford to settle the question for its camera path. A reusable renderer incurs a new verification cost with every use. Discard the assumption that a function which renders correctly in the demo carries all the geometric guarantees a new consumer needs. Inherit the plain functions, folds and author ownership. Even the distinction between distance and a bound is already present in the original library. [Mercury's library and revision history](https://mercury.sexy/hg_sdf/) · [Hart's sphere-tracing derivation](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348b-20-spring-content/uploads/hart.pdf)

**Shadertoy.** A fragment author can own everything from camera ray to film response in a page of code. Image buffers and feedback can carry state between frames. The difficulty appears when somebody wants *part* of the result. Imagine borrowing an ocean from a shader whose Image output includes fog, exposure and sky. Alpha may already hold yesterday's accumulation count in an intermediate buffer. The host needs a depth in its own camera convention to put a boat on the ocean, and optical depth out to that boat to put spray in front of it. Neither can be recovered from the final RGB. The author can return those values, but doing so requires reading the whole shader to discover its units, channel packing and frame order. Natron's Shadertoy port documents a concrete failure of transplantation. Wiring nodes to reproduce same-buffer previous-frame feedback would form an illegal cycle in its ordinary compositing graph. Discard the idea that an editable string and a few texture channels provide a sufficient interface between two independently authored effects. Keep the radical ease of copying an entire renderer, and the freedom to write its innermost loop. [Natron's Shadertoy adapter and feedback example](https://github.com/NatronGitHub/openfx-misc/blob/master/Shadertoy/Shadertoy.cpp)

**Dreams.** Media Molecule's experiments put the picture first. Its brick renderer chose a level through a tree of 8×8×8 blocks, rasterized each block's bounds, then traced the distance field inside. Artists used the result, but the hard depth edge lost the soft, painted intersections the team wanted. Slicing a block 8–16 ways burned fill rate. Recording partial-opacity samples during the march left tens of millions to composite in depth order in an experiment. Evans went back to the sculpt evaluator and tried point clouds and splats. The crucial freedom belonged to the people who could go back to the evaluator. An author working solely downstream of a resolved depth image could never recover the soft overlap by improving its edge filter. The lesson concerns the production history of Dreams, rather than a universal performance claim about splats. Discard any authoring boundary that makes access to the pre-render representation a privilege of the engine team. Keep the team's criterion for discarding an algorithm. Even years of working artist tools did not make the hard edge right for that picture. [Evans, *Learning from Failure*, especially slides 67–75 and the point-cloud experiments](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)

**Teardown.** A dense voxel grid per destructible shape earns its place. Breakage edits the same occupancy that defines the visible object. The original renderer's shadow volume also illustrates the cost of a second representation of the world. Dennis Gustafsson describes trying to make trees sway and failing to get the lighting to follow properly. His later prototype retained voxels, used custom hardware intersection shaders and dispensed with the shadow volume. He attributes sharper shadows and removal of light leakage and world-size restrictions to that change. Those are claims for the prototype, distinct from the shipping game's capabilities. The diagnosis for Loft is specific. A regular grid is appropriate for editable masonry. A gently bending branch and its time-dependent shadow were constrained by how another pass stored occupancy. Doubling a dense shape's linear resolution costs eight times as many voxels before compression, even if the surface discrepancy is confined to the branch tip. Discard global voxel occupancy as a prerequisite for casting a shadow. Keep Teardown's much more interesting decision to let the thing people actually do to the world determine its native representation. [Gustafsson's account of both renderers](https://blog.voxagon.se/2024/12/29/year-summary.html)

## What a lighting algorithm commits the author to

**ReSTIR.** The original direct-lighting paper repeatedly resamples candidate lights, retaining a weighted representative and combining information across space and time. It reports 6–60× faster at equal error for its unbiased estimator against its comparisons. Its biased variant reached 35–65× with some energy loss. The example workloads went up to 3.4 million moving emissive triangles and used at most eight rays per pixel. Those are reasons to expose this estimator in full. They are poor grounds for selecting it whenever something emits light. Under one directional sun with a hard shadow, the light identity is already known with probability one. A reservoir cannot improve that choice. At a newly uncovered pixel, a sample taken for another surface requires a valid reuse rule and weights. Copying yesterday's bright result is a particularly bad approximation the instant a door shuts. The paper gives the reader access to these decisions. A renderer that exposes them as a single quality slider seals the proposal, normalization, visibility and bias trade together. Discard that slider as authority over energy conservation. Keep the estimators, including the biased one when its error is acceptable for the shot. A converged reference can distinguish a systematic loss of energy from variance that falls with more samples. [Bitterli et al., original ReSTIR results](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2020-07_spatiotemporal-reservoir-resampling-real-time-ray-tracing-dynamic-direct)

**RTXDI.** NVIDIA's implementation already lets the application define surface and light types, sample lights, evaluate densities and supply visibility through its bridge. The expensive assumption can live in the bridge between frames. NVIDIA distinguishes conservative visibility now from conservative visibility at the previous surface and light. Its documentation says that using current data when the previous acceleration structures are unavailable introduces temporary bias and can increase noise. For a cut from one camera to another, reusing the wrong history cheaply is a worse answer than starting a fresh sample. Retaining previous geometry, identities and material state has a memory and update cost even if the reservoir arithmetic takes only a few instructions. The SDK pays that price to serve difficult dynamic scenes. A Loft engine for one lamp, or one that can evaluate the exact past pose on demand, has a different trade. Discard the assumption that adopting one resampling function entails adopting a particular frame history. Keep the explicit probability and visibility functions. They reveal the information a caller owes an estimator, including cases where it cannot supply it. [RTXDI application bridge, particularly temporal conservative visibility](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI/blob/main/Doc/RtxdiApplicationBridge.md)

**DDGI.** An irradiance probe amortizes ray tracing over many diffuse receivers. Majercik and colleagues interpolate probes using visibility information derived from distance moments. They demonstrate a separate glossy term. The distinction is fundamental. Diffuse irradiance has already integrated incoming directions with a cosine weight. One can distribute light continuously across the hemisphere or concentrate it in a bright patch and obtain the same irradiance for a chosen normal. A mirror can distinguish those cases immediately. More rays can reduce noise in the stored quantity without recovering the angular information lost in that integral. Probe spacing also matters across a thin wall. Approximate visibility weights reduce leaking, while doubling probe density along all axes costs eight times as many probes in a fixed volume. Discard diffuse probes as the definition of what a point knows about light. Keep visibility-aware interpolation for the diffuse term, including for an engine that spends its saved rays on the brass ring's reflection. Blurring a failed glossy reflection until it resembles diffuse shading changes the material. [Majercik et al., *Dynamic Diffuse Global Illumination with Ray-Traced Irradiance Fields*](https://research.nvidia.com/index.php/publication/2019-05_dynamic-diffuse-global-illumination-ray-traced-irradiance-fields)

## Which parts of an open renderer are actually local to replace?

**PBRT.** PBRT makes the hard algorithms legible down to their derivations. It is a strong model for a reference engine because a reader can trace a sampling weight all the way to the film. Its fourth edition also documents the distance between an editable algorithm and a performant GPU implementation. The GPU path offers one volumetric path-tracing integrator, organized as wavefront queues. The book's example at 2,048 samples per pixel takes 318.6 seconds on its 2020-era high-end GPU against 11,983 seconds on the eight-core CPU. The result explains why it was worth reorganizing the work. It does not estimate the first-frame latency of a Loft preview. A new CPU integrator whose control flow diverges per bounce does not acquire suitable GPU queues through the act of translating its equations. A shadow-only experiment can inherit queue traffic and material sorting intended for many-bounce paths if that is its only accelerated entry. Discard a privileged GPU schedule that each new estimator must grow into. Keep the inspectable reference and the option to pay for wavefront scheduling where divergent rays justify it. [PBRT v4, chapter 15](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Wavefront_Rendering_on_GPUs)

**Mitsuba.** A researcher can register a BSDF or integrator written in Python and obtain compiled execution through Dr.Jit. The public example even changes the tint of reflection on a dielectric. This is close to Loft's ambition. The instructive boundary is what happens when the researcher wants to optimize that same object's shape or index of refraction. The ordinary path replay backpropagation integrator uses detached sampling. Mitsuba documents restrictions on ideal specular IOR derivatives, and offers projective sampling integrators for discontinuous visibility such as moving silhouettes. The extra work represents real missing terms. Swapping a forward shader cannot manufacture those terms. Likewise, a JIT path integrator that renders the ring need not yield a raster depth attachment suitable for another application's viewport. Discard the assumption that registering the material makes every downstream analysis and execution mode valid. Inherit Mitsuba's honesty about which derivatives an integrator supports, and the ability to replace the integrator in ordinary user code when the property of interest changes. [Mitsuba's Python plugin](https://mitsuba.readthedocs.io/en/latest/src/others/custom_plugin.html) · [Integrator capabilities and limits](https://mitsuba.readthedocs.io/en/stable/src/generated/plugins_integrators.html)

**Falcor.** NVIDIA's research framework lets the author replace a pass and inspect its shader. Its documented path tracer needs a primary visibility buffer encoding a hit primitive and coordinates so that the scene can reconstruct its material data. Two optional inputs show how much semantics can hide in an apparently clean graph. RTXDI temporal reuse needs motion vectors. Correct depth of field needs the sampled view direction, since a ray reconstructed toward the camera origin goes through the middle of the lens. Two producers can agree on texture size and format while giving different pictures through the lens. An analytic volume can go further and return an interval with extinction and no surface normal at all. Connecting its colour to the path tracer cannot turn that colour into a recoverable surface. These dependencies help Falcor compare interchangeable research passes on an established scene. Discard the assumption that texture-port compatibility establishes physical compatibility, or that a mesh visibility record is a universal input. Keep the runnable pass graphs and the explicit evidence about what each connection means. [Falcor path-tracer inputs and outputs](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Falcor/blob/master/docs/usage/path-tracer.md)

**Slang.** Interfaces, generics and link-time specialization address a real compiler defect in macro-driven shader libraries. A module can be type-checked before the caller selects an implementation, then optimized after linking. That is leverage for an author replacing a BSDF. It does not decide how much to specialize. Six independent binary choices emitted eagerly as whole pipelines permit 64 combinations. A colour supplied as an instance parameter need incur just one program. Conversely, turning a sharp, expensive branch into data can cost registers and SIMD efficiency. The language makes both choices possible. A renderer can still seal the choice by shipping only preselected entry points or keying a pipeline on every material constant. Discard using the number of possible type combinations as the compiler's work list. Retain separate compilation and specialization at measured sites. An engine embedding Slang or WGSL still owns the consequences for resource layouts, pipeline creation, editable source locations and the time to the first useful frame. [Slang's link-time specialization and precompilation guide](https://docs.shader-slang.org/en/latest/external/slang/docs/user-guide/10-link-time-specialization.html)

**Hardware ray tracing.** DXR explicitly allows an intersection shader to receive a candidate bounding box, test a procedural primitive and report a hit with custom attributes. A hardware path therefore need not triangulate the ring. The sealed part is the accelerator's internal representation and traversal. Build flags express preferences. The application cannot replace the traversal of one internal subtree with its own grid walk while continuing to use those same opaque nodes. It can put the grid inside a procedural leaf or traverse a separate structure, each with its own occupancy, ordering and update costs. A large box around sparse contents can invite many expensive procedural tests. For an animated object, compare total build or update cost plus ray costs. If an update costs B and acceleration saves s per ray, break-even is B/s rays for that update, assuming equal hits and positive s. A shadow ray that can stop on the first opaque blocker and a reflection ray seeking the closest hit can have quite different s. Discard hardware traversal as a mandatory scene representation, and the equally wasteful assumption that a software intersector must do its own broad-phase search. [Microsoft's intersection-shader contract](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3d12/intersection-shader) · [DXR functional specification](https://microsoft.github.io/DirectX-Specs/d3d/Raytracing.html)

**WebGPU and wgpu.** WebGPU standardizes validated GPU work across devices and a browser security boundary. Its portable render and compute interfaces give a consumer the means to write a software traversal. Portable WebGPU supplies no standard hardware ray query. Rust wgpu is a separate implementation and native API. In Loft's pinned 30.0.1, the feature documentation lists experimental ray query as native-only on Vulkan. The presence of a ray unit on the host's GPU alone therefore establishes nothing about this program's access to it. There is also a real price for wasting portable bindings. Loft's earlier one-buffer-per-table layout left only 26 of 232 built corpus entries loading under the tested default limits. Packing the tables into at most five buffers let all 232 load. That is a binding-layout failure imposed by Loft on itself within a legitimate device limit. Discard treating either the browser baseline or the most capable native adapter as the definition of what an object is. Keep validation, capability reporting and the host's ownership of the device. Report which traversal actually executed on it. [WebGPU specification](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/) · [wgpu 30.0.1 features](https://docs.rs/wgpu/30.0.1/wgpu/struct.Features.html#associatedconstant.EXPERIMENTAL_RAY_QUERY) · [Loft binding and load results](../../2026-11-19_lean_rust_draw.md)

## Loft under the same test

**The hard part is sometimes already public.** On the TypeScript path an author can supply a Fresnel function to the PBR style. A furnace with uniform sky radiance 0.5 returned a mean of 127.51 in eight-bit output, with every measured sphere pixel at 127 or 128. A different experiment integrated the specular lobe in isolation. At unit Fresnel, maximum roughness and normal incidence its directional albedo was 0.307, agreeing with the analytic value 1 − ln 2. The authors had predicted 0.70–0.88. The missing multiple scattering is visible in the latter experiment. The assembled dielectric furnace can pass because the diffuse and specular environment weights add back up. If the desired object is a rough white conductor, that dielectric result gives the wrong reassurance. Keep the small replaceable terms, the numerical integration and the record of a falsified prediction. Discard calling a style physically complete because two executions of its own approximation agree. A path-traced reference earns its keep only if its material and light assumptions are also open to inspection. [PBR measurements and derivation](../../2026-11-18_pbr_style/NOTE.md) · [Implemented shading terms](../../../src/style/pbr_parts.ts)

**Visibility changes with the drawing method.** The TS field AO code made a groove 23.2% darker in display luma at a measured patch while leaving the flat control alone. In alternating warm runs the median extra GPU cost was 0.066 ms at 512², about one clock tick. A meshed field or custom tracer receives the unoccluded default. Replacing the drawing method to save time therefore changes the material's environment visibility. The traced field's water shadow has a different dependency. Its CPU path samples a 22³ grid and fits a four-joint capsule chain. A GPU-only field can get a capsule fitted to its bound, with the reason recorded. Neither fit follows a time-varying field. A ring with a moving hole can have a convincing camera silhouette and a stationary solid shadow. This compromise originally paid for cheap localized shadows on water. Discard choosing it as a side effect of how the author expressed the field. The approximation is valuable to a consumer who actually wants that shadow. [AO controls and timing method](../../2026-11-18_pbrsky/NOTE.md) · [Water-shadow fit](../../../src/draw/field_shadow.ts) · [Projected capsule patches](../../../src/draw/shadows.ts)

**A cheaper representation can change the object.** Loft modelled a folded capsule tree at 360×300. Marching used 8,212,834 capsule-distance evaluations. A coarse pass per 8×8 tile reduced that to 2,701,405 and changed 17 pixels. Expanding the tree into 5,461 branches required only 183,842 analytic capsule tests, but changed 172 of 108,000 pixels. Folded copies were clipped at a mirror plane and the listed copies continued through it. The 44.7× ratio counts different operations on a CPU model. It establishes neither a GPU speedup of that size nor exact shape equivalence. The important discovery is the cut at the mirror plane. More timing rounds cannot explain it away. Discard N-way unions evaluated at every step where bounds or spatial lookup suffice, and discard automatic substitutions whose justification stops at the cost model. The author's cell and clipping rule is part of the geometry. [Loft's model, counts and differing pixels](../../../docs/tracing_sota.md)

**The port removed duplication and also moved policy into the compiler.** The TS audit found five ray-hit drawables wiring their own fragment, outline and depth behavior. A raw TS draw gives its author WGSL entry points but records empty geometry forms, so geometric checks lose sight of it. Rust's one ray template is an advance. Colour and depth call the same hit function.
