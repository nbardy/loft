# Where renderers lose the authored picture

Step 1 critique · 19 November 2026 · Loft revision `901e48d`

A ray sent straight down the bore of Loft’s drilled bead hits empty air. The example puts a unit sphere at (0, 1, 0) and removes a vertical cylinder of radius 0.35. I asked its public hit function about a ray from (0, 4, 0) in direction (0, −1, 0), over distances [0, 10]. The entire ray is inside the hole. Loft’s CPU twin returned distance 2, gap 0 and normal (0, 1, 0), exactly what a solid ball would return. For an axial ray the cylinder equation loses its quadratic term. Clamping the divisor to 10⁻⁹ produces two zero roots and the sphere entry wins. This is an author-level bug in a particularly good example. The example makes the geometric law public enough to question directly. A beautiful oblique view, a closed-form label and agreement between two executions of the same formula could all miss the defect. [Bead source](../../examples/bead.ts) · [Ray and hit conventions](../../src/draw/traced.ts)

That observation sets the standard for this critique. A viewer, a lamp looking through the hole, a mirror and a pick at its centre each ask about the same missing material. Their sampling footprints can differ. Changing the viewpoint or the drawing method cannot put the material back. The hardest failures below occur when a renderer throws away information while answering one question and silently uses the result to answer another. The next hardest occur when it preserves the information but makes the author express the same fact twice.

## What counts as a loss

Loft puts picture first, GPU frame time second, then author tokens, truth and economy of implementation. Its training budget is 16 frames at 512² and at most 50 ms GPU per frame. That is 4,194,304 primary pixel locations and at most 800 ms of GPU time across a clip if every frame meets budget. A cache that needs 32 frames to settle cannot spend the second half of that history on this clip unless someone pays for preroll. An offline reference can spend much more. Under ordinary independent finite-variance Monte Carlo sampling, halving RMS noise takes roughly four times the samples. Those are budget calculations, not timings of any engine. [Core priorities](../../CORE.md) · [Render goal](../../docs/design/render.md) · [Philosophy](../../docs/PHILOSOPHY.md)

“Exact” needs an observable. A sphere equation specifies a boundary. Rasterizing its triangulation specifies another boundary. Integrating pixel coverage, choosing a point on a lens and estimating radiance introduce separate errors even with the right boundary. Likewise, two exact methods for finding the nearest opaque surface can disagree about a stylised outline drawn on a near miss. A useful cost comparison holds the relevant observable fixed, including its tolerance. An opaque depth comparison earns no credit for a mist shadow. A visually indistinguishable approximation may win the picture, but it still needs an accurate description.

“Load time does not grow with content” also has a physical limit. Reading B new bytes through a channel of bandwidth W takes at least B/W time. The achievable demand is that adding copies of an existing kind adds instance data and necessary transfers, with bounded *distinct program and pipeline work* for fixed kinds, formats and pass state. A new algorithm or a new target format can legitimately need a pipeline. The colour of crab number five should not. These distinctions let us judge engines beyond the conditions that produced them without inventing a common benchmark where none exists.

## Production engines and the worlds their passes see

**Unreal Engine 5.** Nanite’s achievement is that the artist can hand over a densely triangulated asset and the renderer chooses clusters at a useful screen scale. Its winning premise was a huge supply of authored triangles. A surface given by two radii and a transform pays a different price. Converting that surface first loses the equation that could answer a ray at arbitrary magnification. Adding triangles reduces the discrepancy and increases the data. Neither the hierarchy nor the streaming machinery can infer that all those triangles once described one torus. Keep hierarchical selection and measured screen coverage. Discard mandatory conversion to the representation that happens to stream best for scanned assets. [Epic’s Nanite technical details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)

Lumen makes the loss visible. Its screen trace can see the detailed foreground while its hardware ray follows a fallback mesh in the documented fallback configuration. Its Surface Cache separately captures material properties, with 12 cards per mesh by default. Epic documents uncovered areas that bounce no light and appear black in reflections. Splitting an interior into several meshes can repair coverage, which means the artist’s file partition has become a lighting parameter. Epic gives about 8 ms for GI and reflections at 1080p internal resolution on its Epic console target. That buys a remarkable amount of light, yet the milliseconds measure an approximation with specific blind spots. Turn a mirror until a detailed object leaves the screen and the screen-space repair loses its input. Discard the assumption that an apparently continuous transition between representations establishes geometric agreement. Retain the ability to inspect each representation separately. [Epic’s Lumen technical details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)

**Unity HDRP.** Consider a consumer who can already draw the drilled bead and wants it visible through rough glass. HDRP 17.0 documents seven custom-pass injection points. Depth, normals, roughness, colour and their pyramids have different availability at those points. The colour pyramid for refraction is a snapshot. Drawing the bead after the snapshot can put it in the final frame while leaving it out of the glass. Writing depth early enough for opaque rejection still does not describe the bead to a light ray. The author has to know when buffers were derived, which writes are legal and which materials a DrawRenderers pass accepts. These are real dependencies that an ordered production pipeline makes manageable across many effects. Asking every new primitive’s author to reconstruct them is the defect. Reject a numbered insertion opportunity as the meaning of “participates in rendering.” Keep the explicit distinction between a writable attachment and a derived, already populated image. [Unity HDRP 17.0 injection points](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Custom-Pass-Injection-Points.html)

**Godot.** SDFGI illustrates the difference between naming a technique and specifying the geometry it sees. Godot’s documented SDFGI can illuminate a moving object while excluding dynamic occluders from generation. Move a large opaque door between a bright room and a dark room. The door can receive indirect light while its changing position fails to close that light path in the cache. Cascades keep the expense of large environments tractable, and a camera-induced cascade shift can visibly change the light while it converges. Meanwhile, screen-space reflections lose information outside the view and behind opaque depth. SDFGI belongs to Forward+, one of three rendering methods alongside Mobile and Compatibility. A project-level method decision thus selects which lighting evidence an author can use. Keep bounded updates and the explicit support matrix. Throw out the coupling between selecting the rest of an engine and choosing how one light query runs. [Godot SDFGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_sdfgi.html) · [Godot rendering architecture](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html)

**Bevy.** Bevy takes replaceability seriously. Its example of drawing a stencil for selected meshes makes the cost legible. The user wants a marked cube and sphere drawn into one additional phase. The example defines a plugin, a phase item, pipeline specialization, a draw command and queueing for views. The phase item carries such facts as the pipeline, draw function and batch range. All serve the engine’s need to extract, sort, batch and schedule ECS data. None describes which pixels belong to the selected objects. There are easier paths for a material change or an item in an existing phase. The revealing case is a change just outside those paths, where an agent has to learn the phase protocol before writing the new rendering law. Reject ECS membership and per-view queue bookkeeping as prerequisites for contributing a pass on somebody else’s device. Preserve the ability to submit arbitrary drawing commands. [Bevy custom render phase](https://bevy.org/examples/shaders/custom-render-phase/) · [Bevy custom phase item](https://bevy.org/examples/shaders/custom-phase-item/)

**three.js.** The ordinary mesh case is a good control for token cost. A geometry, a material and a transform get an imported or generated object into a scene with little ceremony. Change the silhouette by moving vertices in a custom WebGL shader and the ordinary shadow path needs help. Custom depth and custom distance materials cover two different shadow cases, the latter for a point light. The author can now maintain the same displacement in the visible material and two shadow paths. There is a second cost at the backend boundary. The WebGPU renderer’s node materials and TSL enable cross-compilation and automatic pass combination. Its migration guide says that a WebGL material altered with `onBeforeCompile` must be ported. The original hook was cheap because it worked on the generated shader in place. The replacement system needs structure to translate it. Discard the bargain in which the shortest customization becomes the least portable one. Keep easy asset admission and the ability to compose a post chain without writing intermediate passes by hand. [three.js Object3D](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Object3D.html) · [WebGPU renderer migration](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/webgpurenderer)

**Babylon.js.** A leaf with holes makes the same issue measurable without judging a whole framework. Consider a binary mask with exactly half the texels cut out, seen normal to a parallel light, with texel-aligned samples. Its shadow covers half the interior projected area. A solid shadow caster covers all of it. Babylon provides a ShadowDepthWrapper to carry custom material behaviour into the shadow pass. Its documentation also demonstrates supplying a separate, lighter material that implements only fragment discard. Wrapping saves duplicate author code. A separate minimal shader avoids dragging unused lighting through depth generation, at the cost of another version of the leaf. This tension arose because the built-in shadow path serves cheap, ordinary casters and the custom shader can do arbitrary work. The wrapper is a valuable repair. Discard the expectation that authors should have to choose between sharing their definition and paying only for the portion a query uses. [Babylon shadow shader integration](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/lights/shadows)

**Filament.** A physically coherent, compact material vocabulary is valuable on a phone. Filament offers more control than a table of sliders. Its custom surface shading callback receives the material, shading data and a particular light’s data. It can implement a toon response. The manual restricts this facility to the lit shading model. The light has already been chosen and visibility is an input. Suppose the author’s innovation is to select one emissive triangle using a new importance distribution. No expression of the response to the already selected light can select a different one. Likewise, a prefiltered environment contains incident light indexed by direction and roughness but lacks the position of a newly moved local occluder. Keep the material units, preintegration and explicit separation of material parameters from lights. Discard the per-light callback as the deepest promised level of control. Otherwise a one-function experiment in sampling becomes work on renderer internals. [Filament materials](https://google.github.io/filament/main/materials.html) · [Filament environment prefilter](https://google.github.io/filament/dup/cmgen.html)

**Frostbite.** The PBR course notes give an unusually candid explanation. Artists separate material and lighting so assets travel between environments. Implementation couples them tightly to achieve performance. Under Loft’s criterion the consumer of the implementation is also an author. A team changing a distribution now owns the corresponding assumptions in area lighting and environment preintegration. Agreement on nearly diffuse plastic can conceal disagreement on a brushed highlight. Frostbite’s volumetric work offers a better inheritance. Injecting different sources of extinction into a shared volume lets particles cast volumetric shadows and avoids independently compositing each effect. The cost is spatial sampling. In a cell of depth h, a density spike of width h/8 can lie entirely between two point samples a distance h apart. Filtering preserves some integrated signal at the cost of putting it elsewhere. This is a sampling example, not a reported Frostbite artifact. Keep its common physical quantities. Discard a uniform requirement that an analytic bank of fog must spend spatial resolution to recover an optical depth already known in closed form. [Frostbite PBR course notes](https://media.contentapi.ea.com/content/dam/eacom/frostbite/files/course-notes-moving-frostbite-to-pbr-v2.pdf) · [Frostbite volumetric lighting presentation](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/index.html)

**id Tech.** id Tech 5’s virtual texture gave artists a vast unique painted surface through demand paging. Its documented example is 128K × 128K texels, 1,024 pages along one side. Only the working set must be resident. That is an excellent answer for unique marks made by a person. For stripes defined by a short periodic function, committing the marks to pages exchanges a few operations for UVs, residency decisions and a filter footprint tied to the sampled data. A cold camera cut can change the working set without changing the stripes. id Tech 6 gives the complementary frame-time lesson. Its published DOOM frame allocated about 6.5 ms to opaque forward passes, 3 ms to shadow caching and 1.5 ms to transparency. A faster opaque shader can leave the dominant *incremental* cost of a new caster untouched. Keep residency control, clustering and whole-frame accounting. Discard the assumption that the production asset representation or one title’s pass order defines a new author’s exact surface. [id Tech 5 virtual texturing](https://mrelusive.com/publications/presentations/2009_siggraph/05-JP_id_Tech_5_Challenges.pdf) · [id Tech 6 frame breakdown](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2016/Siggraph2016_idTech6.pdf)

## When one representation really does own the world

**Quilez, Shadertoy and the 4K intro.** Writing the surface as mathematics and carrying a repeated cell’s identity into its paint are genuine advantages. A fullscreen shader can specify a world with two triangles. A 4,096-byte executable rewards a fold that generates an entire forest from one motif. The executable size measures the price of distribution. Each invocation of a fragment shader still pays for the computation. If a naïve field takes the minimum of 64 primitive distances, 48 march steps at 512² cost up to 805,306,368 primitive distance evaluations before shadows, normals or refinement, assuming every pixel takes every step. A camera tight on the canopy changes that bill even when the source stays identical. The insight worth inheriting is that one short formula can specify many objects. Discard the assumption that it must be evaluated from the root at each step on every ray. [Quilez on procedural graphics in four kilobytes](https://iquilezles.org/articles/proceduralgfx/inspire2008.pdf) · [Rendering Worlds with Two Triangles](https://iquilezles.org/articles/nvscene2008/rwwtt.pdf)

Shadertoy makes the image function itself editable. That is closer to Loft’s stance than a material inspector. A self-contained image shader, however, can bury its ray convention, field bounds, surface identifiers, exposure and display transfer inside the final colour calculation. A host asking for the first hit ID cannot reconstruct it from an RGB pixel. Multipass buffers allow the author to export extra information, but the two authors composing shaders must agree what the channels mean. There is a geometric cost to clever compression too. Folding a point into the nearest repeated cell is valid only when the neighbouring motif cannot be closer under the chosen construction. Loft’s own sampled check found a slope of 98 for a polar-repeat coral whose crowns crossed sector walls. A march using distance as a safe step can jump over the very branch the author wanted. Keep raw image shaders as a complete artistic medium. Throw out hidden geometry inside final colour as a default unit of reuse, and sampled success as a certificate that a field is safe. [Shadertoy competition and examples](https://blog.siggraph.org/2015/07/shadertoy-competition-at-siggraph-2015.html/) · [Loft’s fold measurements](../../DESIGN.md)

**Media Molecule’s Dreams.** Evans’s 2015 development account traces the path from evaluating shapes to drawing point clouds and strokes. The evaluator could serve the art director’s demand for marks in space. The splatter culled and refined clusters and packed roughly 64 splats together. At one setting the points were subpixel. At another each seed made a visible textured stroke. The distinction matters. On a painted creature a hole between marks might be the art. On an opaque polished sphere it leaks background. The prototype used stochastic alpha and temporal accumulation, and Evans describes noise and ghosting when history validation met stochastic coverage. That evidence pertains to the prototype. The deeper limitation is independent of its eventual shipping refinements. The mathematical solid, visible marks and opacity to a distant light can be three intentionally different things. A point cloud alone cannot tell a second author which one should be picked or reflected. Keep spatial brush marks as an authored choice. Reject splatting every implicit surface as the cost of getting those marks on the ones that need them. [Media Molecule’s account](https://www.mediamolecule.com/blog/article/siggraph_2015) · [Evans’s SIGGRAPH slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)

**Teardown.** A breakable voxel wall has a decisive advantage. Removing one occupied element changes what a subsequent ray traverses, and the same edit makes sense to gameplay. Tuxedo Labs built the engine around that property. Its tools explicitly convert meshes to voxels and repair such things as connectivity. For a diagonal cable or a curved lens the conversion loses information before the ray tracer starts. At a fixed physical extent, halving the cell edge costs 8× as many cells in a dense three-dimensional representation. Sparse storage can avoid buying empty space. It cannot restore the original cable radius from two identical occupancy patterns. A ray can interrogate the grid perfectly and still see the wrong silhouette. The adjustment cost falls on whoever imports the cable, picks scale and checks that it survives. Keep voxels where an occupancy edit is the authored event. Discard their authority over a lens whose surface and refractive normal the author has specified continuously. [Teardown engine FAQ](https://www.teardowngame.com/faq.html) · [Tuxedo Labs’ mesh-to-voxel tools](https://teardowngame.com/voxtool/)

## Transport, reference renderers and GPU tools

**ReSTIR and RTXDI.** Direct lighting asks for a weighted integral over lights and visibility. Sampling every light is wasteful when a few matter. Bitterli and colleagues reused candidate samples across pixels and time. Their 2020 paper reports 6–60× faster equal-error estimates for its unbiased variant against its comparison methods, and 35–65× for a biased variant with some energy loss. Its carousel example had 3.4 million animated lights and took 42 ms at 1920×1080 before denoising. Those numbers describe direct illumination in specific scenes. A scene with one directional light has no light-selection uncertainty to eliminate. Reserving history, fetching neighbours and updating weights solely to discover that light buys nothing. This is a workload argument, not a forecast of the cost of RTXDI on Loft’s GPU. [Original ReSTIR paper](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2020-07_Spatiotemporal-reservoir-resampling/ReSTIR.pdf)

RTXDI’s application bridge exposes the real integration cost. It asks the host how to read a surface in current or previous buffers, sample a light, evaluate a target density and test visibility. For temporal conservative visibility its documentation explains the bias and noise incurred when previous acceleration structures are unavailable and current data is substituted. Consider a lamp occluded by a newly closed door. A stable light ID says nothing about whether the segment was clear at the relevant time. Many-light reuse is valuable precisely because it carries information that took work to obtain. Discard treating the history as free, a denoised output as evidence of unbiasedness, or a hard-wired reservoir pass as a universal lighting model. Keep the distinction between proposal, weight and final visibility. An author changing a material must be able to tell which of those depends on it. [RTXDI application bridge](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI/blob/main/Doc/RtxdiApplicationBridge.md)

**DDGI.** Majercik and colleagues cache diffuse irradiance in a volume of probes and use directional distance moments to improve interpolation visibility. Diffuse transport is smooth enough in many rooms to repay that compression over thousands of receivers. A probe count of P and R update rays per probe implies PR ray queries for a full update. Doubling spatial resolution in all three dimensions multiplies that bill by eight at fixed R. More rays per probe reduce sampling noise, while interpolation across a thin wall can remain wrong at fixed spacing. Two different incoming radiance distributions can give the same diffuse irradiance for a given normal and very different values in a mirror. A sharper denoiser cannot invert that loss. Keep visibility-aware interpolation for diffuse receivers. Reject a diffuse cache as the reference for glossy transport or as proof that an unseen blocker casts the right shadow. Its error changes with geometry, probe spacing and motion as well as sample count. [DDGI paper](https://www.jcgt.org/published/0008/02/01/paper-lowres.pdf)

**PBRT.** PBRT makes the most important competing claim. A camera, a BSDF, a light, an intersection and a medium can describe one transport problem precisely enough to compare estimators. Its GPU implementation groups work into queues, including queues for material evaluation, so divergent paths regain some coherence. That costs memory traffic and dispatches. A queue of 262,144 surviving paths carrying even a hypothetical 32-byte record entails 8 MiB written and 8 MiB read per handoff, before other state. The record size here illustrates the cost model. It is not PBRT’s measured layout. A predominantly opaque raster frame or a single analytically lit plane has no obligation to move that data to answer a primary pixel. Conversely, a one-sample interactive preview of a difficult indirect path can be unbiased and aesthetically unusable. Keep PBRT’s explicit sampling semantics, analytic shapes and independently converged pictures. Throw out equating the reference algorithm’s scheduling costs with the minimum cost of drawing the same scene. [PBRT’s GPU path mapping](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Wavefront_Rendering_on_GPUs/Mapping_Path_Tracing_to_the_GPU) · [PBRT v4 guide](https://www.pbrt.org/users-guide-v4)

**Mitsuba.** Mitsuba comes close to Loft’s promise of replaceable parts. Its Python custom-shape example sphere-traces a Mandelbulb and makes it available by name to the integrator. The example still supplies a primitive count of one, bounds, a preliminary intersection and a surface interaction. That division pays when a shadow ray needs less work than a camera hit. Its variants let the researcher choose such concerns as RGB versus spectra, backend and differentiation. Those are genuine changes in the computation. They also make a poor admission price for someone who has a borrowed render target, a triangle asset and one new hit equation. A BSDF plugin that works in several integrators establishes transport reach. It establishes neither a cheap raster schedule nor the ability to put a pass between a game’s existing draws. Keep the ability to test the same shape under several integrators. Discard the assumption that every interactive shading change deserves the lifecycle of a research renderer plugin or the costs of differentiation. [Mitsuba custom shape](https://mitsuba.readthedocs.io/en/latest/src/others/custom_shape.html) · [Mitsuba compilation variants](https://mitsuba.readthedocs.io/en/stable/src/developer_guide/compiling.html)

**Falcor and Slang.** Falcor is designed for researchers to replace whole techniques. Its graph can infer resource allocation from pass inputs and outputs. A resource flagged persistent survives frames and restricts reuse by the allocator. The cost of expressing too much as passes is just as concrete. Two pointwise effects separated by a materialized RGBA16F image at 1920×1080 exchange 31.64 MiB for one logical write and read. Cache, compression or tile memory can reduce physical DRAM traffic. If that image also feeds a second consumer, materializing it can win. A standalone Falcor pass can involve a plugin class, registration, reflection and execution, all quite reasonable for comparing two denoisers. Those are excessive tokens for exchanging one scalar shading term. Keep resource lifetimes explicit. Reject pass granularity as a minimum size of replaceable mathematics. [Falcor render passes](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Falcor/blob/master/docs/usage/render-passes.md)

Slang tackles the other side with typed modules, generics, module precompilation and link-time specialization. It can preserve the author’s functions while removing dispatch. Specialization still needs a reason at each use. With 12 independent binary choices, eagerly enumerating every combination yields 4,096 programs before formats or sample counts enter. This is an upper-bound construction, not a count from Slang or Falcor. Slang can compile only requested compositions. What a shader compiler cannot infer from a type argument alone is whether this month’s thousand nearly identical objects justify another pipeline on a particular driver. Keep typed linkage and readable generated code. Discard specialization keyed to every author-visible distinction, and the claim that compiling a shader proves a scene’s bounds, material energy or p95 frame time. [Slang link-time specialization](https://shader-slang.org/slang/user-guide/link-time-specialization)

**Hardware ray tracing and portable WebGPU.** Hardware acceleration answers a narrower question than “draw my world.” Vulkan can traverse an acceleration structure over triangles and procedural bounding boxes. The built-in triangle test produces a primitive intersection. A procedural box candidate still needs the consumer’s intersection law. A long thin object in a loose box can produce many candidates and few hits. A moving assembly can demand acceleration structure updates before any ray pays back the investment. If an update costs U and traversal saves d per query for that frame, break-even is more than U/d queries for positive d. One shadow over a few pixels and millions of reflection rays have different answers. Discard equating hardware availability with lowest whole-frame cost, or charging tree construction to someone else’s benchmark. [Khronos ray tracing guide](https://docs.vulkan.org/guide/latest/extensions/ray_tracing.html) · [Vulkan traversal specification](https://docs.vulkan.org/spec/latest/chapters/raytraversal.html)

WebGPU offers a portable execution contract. Its standard baseline limits include four bind groups and eight storage buffers per shader stage. Giving each independently written shading part its own group would exhaust the baseline at the fifth simultaneously used part. Loft pins wgpu 30.0.1, which exposes experimental ray capabilities separately from the web baseline. Timestamp queries are optional, and opaque native pipeline-cache data has implementation and device constraints. A browser promising portable WGSL cannot promise the application a portable precompiled ray pipeline. Equally, forcing software traversal everywhere charges native users for a restriction of the browser. Keep explicit capabilities and the right to use a device owned by a caller. Discard a public per-part binding layout, a universally writable disk pipeline cache, and any per-adapter timing claim made when the timer is unavailable. [WebGPU specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/CRD-webgpu-20260521/) · [Pinned wgpu features](https://docs.rs/wgpu/30.0.1/wgpu/struct.Features.html) · [wgpu pipeline cache](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.PipelineCache.html)

## Loft judged by the same standard

**Loft already loses an object between the eye and the light.** The TS renderer writes projected hit depth for fields and analytic tracers. Water can put foam at that depth where a triangle or field meets it. That is real interoperability. But the general `castRay` queries skeleton capsules and a plane. It can return an object’s mean paint, flatten a sail to a round chain, miss the swell or miss a thing with no skeleton. A traced field’s fitted shadow can come from a 22³ grid sampled at time zero. A moving field can leave its fitted shadow behind. These approximations arose to put legible shadows onto stylised water at low cost. They make good stylistic decisions available to the library. They make poor answers to a request for a reflected hit or the ID of the painted surface under a cursor. Discard the implicit promotion from a decorative stand-in to a scene query. The bead’s axial-ray failure is the complementary warning about exact queries. A public equation and an independent geometric counterexample are both necessary. [Scene cast](../../src/draw/cast.ts) · [Field shadow fit](../../src/draw/field_shadow.ts) · [Drawing schedules and depth](../../DESIGN.md)

**The fastest measured draw can change the picture.** Loft’s 3,069-branch coral is a better decision test than an abstract debate about raster and rays. At 1280×800 the listed analytic branches cost 3.08 ms in close-up against 25.89 ms for the bricked march. At the far view the pair cost 0.72 and 1.90 ms. These are isolated draw times, best of 40 interleaved GPU rounds. Listing brought compilation from 7.2 s to 31 ms and static tables from 4.04 MB to 0.14 MB. Yet 1.5–9.5% of pixels, depending on view, differed by more than 8/255. The fold clips branches that cross a mirror, the list keeps them whole, and near-miss ink and shadow fitting differ. A 406,000-triangle mesh still gave the tips knobs. Today the author writes the list. There is no general spatial lookup for many blended parts, although the TS path already accelerates particular fields and lattices. Reject both an N-way minimum in the inner loop and a speed verdict that conceals a different silhouette. The relevant question is which portions of this win survive the original artist’s clipping and ink. [Measured coral comparison, §7.7–7.8](../../DESIGN.md) · [Marching analysis](../../docs/tracing_sota.md)

**A shared material name hides different lighting.** Today’s GGX distribution, Smith visibility, Fresnel and environment terms are public functions. Swapping a visibility function once exposed a stale environment fit, shifting a furnace check by 0.0755. Loft now refits or refuses an unsuitable fit. This earns its place because a changed law has observable consequences in two computations. The remaining single-scattering lobe has directional albedo 1 − ln 2 ≈ 0.307 at normal incidence, unit roughness and Fresnel one. A white dielectric furnace result supported by a diffuse component says little about that missing multiple-scattered energy in a rough perfect reflector. Scalar roughness also cannot set two different widths along a brushed surface’s tangents. More fundamentally, the default reflected sky cannot show an off-camera red hull, and tinting glass cannot reveal its refracted background. Clearcoat, sheen, subsurface and thin-film effects deserve the same scrutiny under changed illumination. A convincing still of lacquer alone cannot distinguish layered transport from a well-painted highlight. [PBR parts](../../src/style/pbr_parts.ts) · [Measured lobe integral](../2026-11-18_pbr_style/NOTE.md) · [Environment fit correction](../2026-11-18_fix_pbr.md)

The existing field AO samples four offsets along the geometric normal. It darkened a groove’s display luma by 23.2% in the documented patch and left a planar control unchanged. Meshed fields and copies take the unoccluded variant. Choosing raster for the same shape can therefore brighten its grooves. That coupling defeats automatic method selection even with identical depth. Keep the measured local approximation where the style wants it. Discard using a representation-dependent AO result as evidence for global visibility. Loft’s parity set can tell whether a new build preserves yesterday’s pictures. Judging indirect light needs a converged reference to the same authored emitters, scattering and media. A repeatable old approximation can pass parity indefinitely. [AO study](../2026-11-18_pbrsky/NOTE.md) · [Reference-mode goal](../../docs/design/render.md)

**Opacity and the camera reveal missing information.** The TS volume marcher integrates extinction clipped by opaque depth, then composites each volume once in author order. Put homogeneous red and blue media in exactly the same interval over black. Give each optical depth ln 2 and unit source radiance in its own channel. Red over blue gives linear (0.5, 0, 0.25), reversing them gives (0.25, 0, 0.5), and joint integration gives (0.375, 0, 0.375). All three transmit 0.25 of the background. A transmittance-only check passes while both coloured answers fail. Loft’s Gaussian and marched media, glazed surfaces and spray make this a practical question. Its 100,000-grain veil example measured 120.8 ms marching per grain pixel and 1.56 ms for the grain draw using a 32³ grid, plus pose work. That grid smooths changes below a cell. Depth of field makes a parallel mistake if the accumulated colour of fog is assigned the opaque depth behind it. The existing 32-tap gather caps the blur radius at 16 pixels at 720p.
