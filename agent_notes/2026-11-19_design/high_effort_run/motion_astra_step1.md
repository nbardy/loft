# Why did the heron look down?

*Loft motion design, Step 1 critique · 19 November 2026*

At 0.9 seconds in Loft’s hop comparison, three herons are above the same rail. The first has its bill pointed forward and its legs tucked. The second looks as if it is diving for something behind the rail. The third has almost the silhouette of the first. All three arrive on the deck. The second bird’s mistake is particularly instructive because the mathematics can explain exactly why it looked down. Its lower leg needed clearance. Rotating the torso moved the hip and the knee’s bend direction. The head went along because somebody had attached it to the torso. The optimizer found a way to clear a leg that an animator would reject at a glance. [Three-way comparison](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/heron_hop_3.webp) · [Look-round measurements](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

The motion goal asks for two things at once. An author can decide what the audience should see at a particular instant. A creature can act on information that becomes available only at that instant. A boss can telegraph its own lunge, but its recoil from an unexpected hit must start from wherever the lunge has already taken it. A performance therefore has a past that constrains it, a future the author may direct, and physical limits that can make the direction impossible. An array of joint orientations describes one result of those decisions. It loses which foot was bearing weight, what the eyes were following, and which timing belonged to the shot. Those omissions become expensive at the first edit. [Motion goal](../../docs/design/motion.md) · [Loft’s stance](../../docs/PHILOSOPHY.md)

I judge the work below by the image after an edit. Reverse the crab while a foot is in the air. Move the bird’s fish to the right as its chest turns left. Hit the boss again before it recovers. Inspect the drawn toe, the eye line and the silhouette from the game camera. Then ask how much code, data, GPU time and author attention the edit consumed. Numbers from the Loft look rounds are recorded measurements on their fixtures. Other numbers below are attributed to the original experiments or derived with their assumptions stated. A physics tick, a generated frame, a database query and a presented image pay different bills. [Project priorities and frame budgets](../../CORE.md)

## What Loft already gets right, and where it breaks

**A course can remember a plant only if the course itself stays put.** Loft’s gait takes a home point for each foot and evaluates the course at the midpoint of that foot’s stance. For a known curved path this is economical and geometrically meaningful. The outside foot travels farther than the inside foot, and a stationary target can be reconstructed when the director scrubs backward. In the scramble, however, signed displacement drives the phase. When the body goes left through a coordinate it visited going right, the phase retraces its steps. The foot plays its swing backward. Changing the clock to accumulated absolute distance would keep phase increasing, but two journeys arriving at the same distance and heading can have left a particular foot on opposite sides of a rock. The missing information is the world point at which that foot actually landed. [Gait](../../src/geom/gait.ts) · [Scramble comparison](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/crab_scramble.webp)

The stance transition exposes a second, independent defect. Loft interpolates the standing and walking targets with the walk weight and chooses which planted flag to read at weight 0.5. Imagine two stationary candidate toes 20 cm apart on level ground, both marked planted. A linear transition over 0.2 seconds sends the visible target across the floor at 1 m/s while the flag stays asserted. This is a counterexample derived directly from the blend. The analytic leg can introduce another discrepancy because it clamps reach. A two-link leg of lengths 0.5 and 0.5 m aimed 1.1 m away finishes at least 10 cm short. A particle effect can read “plant,” a target can pass a position check, and daylight can still show under the foot. Discard the use of a phase flag as evidence about the drawn toe. Keep the analytic chain for reachable targets and the course for journeys with a known path. [Stance blending](../../src/geom/stance.ts) · [Limb geometry and its limits](../../src/geom/limb.ts)

**The stateful crab exposes the cost of the right information.** In the look worktree, eight feet remember their plants at 120 Hz. A foot steps when its home projected ahead by velocity has moved far enough from its anchor, subject to a minimum stance time and neighbour exclusions. In four seconds it took 24 steps. Exactly planted consecutive reads had zero displacement, and no tested neighbours swung together. In the final column of the sheet the stateful crab stands with uneven spacing, a visible record of how it arrived. Yet velocity changed sign at 1.792 s and the next foot lifted at 2.150 s, about 43 ticks later. Already-airborne feet could redirect before then. The missed prediction concerned the next lift. Raising the sampling rate would leave almost all of that delay intact because the strain still has to cross the threshold. [Stepper and measured reversal](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

The two panels share their authored lateral trajectory and acceleration-driven roll. Both lean 30.2° at the reversal. Only the feet differ. The root never has to negotiate a missing support, the trajectory contains no yaw, and the stepper uses the height-based capture timescale √(2.5/9.81) ≈ 0.505 s. The reported zero slide measures stored foot positions. Neither this measurement nor the convincing lean establishes that the actual contact forces could have driven the shell. The later variant gave urgent swings less time and height and produced 23 steps. It still uses an explicitly chosen neighbourhood for this eight-foot arrangement. Adding a ninth leg raises an anatomical question about which neighbours may unload together. An index-order convention hides that question until the topology changes. Discard the inference from this successful bake to arbitrary-body balance or live player response. [Scramble variants and their limits](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

The GPU result is more surprising than the timing miss. At 512² and one point in the turn, the best of 60 complete frames was 0.59 ms with baked feet and 0.66 ms with analytic gait. Pose stages took 0.131 and 0.197 ms respectively, a difference of about one timestamp tick. The bake bought that lookup with 480 steps, 3.1 ms of GPU work, 15.7 ms wall time for the bake, 309 ms of pipeline setup and 0.21 MB retained. This is evidence that precomputing the more interesting motion can pay even on a small creature. It leaves p95, five independently controlled creatures and unknown input streams unmeasured. The four-second clip knows the reversal at load. A player can choose to keep going. [Scramble timings](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

**A smooth optimum can remove the only sign of life.** On the crab’s separate 6 m-radius walk, the feet were identical in the two versions. The authored shell had 0.09 m of rhythmic bob and 0.04 rad of roll amplitude. The solve had 56 spline controls for body position and yaw, prescribed endpoint motion and leg reach bounds. No reach bound engaged. The solve spent about 0.2 s finding a trajectory within 3.7 mm of the course at constant height 2.50 m. A level carriage satisfies the stated conditions beautifully. Repeating the solve more accurately offers no reason for the shell to load a leg. Conversely, increasing bob amplitude cannot move the weight onto a different foot after a disturbance. Discard smoothness as a default account of weight. Keep both that cheap authored pulse and the ability to solve a condition that actually binds. [Turn comparison and CPU readout](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

**The heron separates a true claim from a good shot.** The base bird has five cubic spline channels with 15 controls each. Both feet share the same two sagittal channels. Flight acceleration is prescribed between 0.6 and 1.2 s. The solver checks torso and neck stations, bill samples and selected leg segments against the rail. At 0.965 s the lower-leg margin was about 0.2 mm. Torso, neck and bill clearance had over 0.76 m to spare. The resulting 67° nose-down pitch put the base of the bill 0.39 m below the hips, a 1.77 m change from its standing height relative to them. A stronger bill clearance penalty in that region would address the wrong body part. The single torso rotation controls both the solution and its casualty. [Main heron rig and holds](../../examples/second/heron_leap.ts) · [Diagnosis at the active constraint](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

The spline family also directs the acting. Acceleration is continuous across takeoff and landing. With gravity prescribed during flight, the acceleration has to reach free-fall acceleration by the end of the push. On this solution the crouch reaches 0.77 m and the push peaks at 46.8 m/s². The hand-keyed bird keeps its attitude but hangs near the apex. The third version let the head rotate separately, fitted the performance to the keys, added attitude and endpoint conditions, and refined the knot spacing. It used 6 × 27 = 162 controls, reached a 12° peak pitch and a 2.27 g push, and kept head tilt within 0.17° on the reported readout. Its solve took 7.6 s in the look round, beyond Loft’s 2 s CPU training-build target. Several variables changed together. The result establishes the value of preserving the directed pose, but cannot tell us how much improvement to credit to any single change. [Three-way look round](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md) · [Training target](../../CORE.md)

Loft has unusually good tools for saying how far this evidence goes. On the base problem the Rust build returns unproved holds. The separate check produced 24 Holds and two precision-qualified results. The least of nine Rust solve times under load was 117 ms for the base and 221 ms for the moved deck. On the moved problem a tiny numerical perturbation moved the controls appreciably, and the projected gradient showed that the search had stalled before a stationary optimum. A high rail produced a local search refusal. Calling that rail impossible would discard the distinction between failure of a search and failure of geometry. Discard “the smoothest feasible motion” as a description of the delivered answer. Retain the witness, the named precision limit and the proof on the actual returned curves. [Rust solve and check measurements](../2026-11-19_lean_rust_solve2.md)

**The shortest line in the example hides most of the authoring.** I counted nonempty syntax leaves in the TypeScript 5.9.3 parse tree, including punctuation and declarations, excluding comments and whitespace. In the main heron, `holds` consumes 881 such tokens and `leapSpec` another 237, before `rig`, `apart` or the model. On the look branch the crab’s `stepper`, `urgency` and `shapeOf` consume 768, 191 and 131 respectively. These counts measure source a new implementer would have to understand or replace. Reusing an unchanged routine costs far fewer tokens, and model tokenizers give different totals. In particular the author has spelled out clearance stations derived from body geometry. Changing the beak in the visible rig can require a second edit in the safety geometry. The cost is keeping two descriptions true after an edit. [Heron source](../../examples/second/heron_leap.ts) · [Stepper source](../../../_wt/loft-motion/examples/motion/lib/stepper.ts)

The Titan illustrates what deserves to survive subtraction. Its antennae have 14 stations each, with delayed sway, a distinct flick on release and a whip after impact. Its brow reacts differently to attending, straining and letting go. Replacing that performance with indiscriminate damping would erase intentional signals. The weakness is the maintenance cost when the torso performance changes or a new hit arrives halfway through the first response. A prescribed delay faithfully reproduces whatever signal it was given, including an obsolete clock. The mourner’s driven pendulum already shows that Loft can express secondary physics. Reject the premise that Loft lacks secondary motion. The missing test is whether it follows a *changed* parent while leaving a deliberately timed gesture legible. [Titan brow and antennae](../../examples/bosses/titan/face/brow.ts) · [Pendulum](../../src/geom/pendulum.ts) · [Earlier audit](../2026-11-19_audits/motion_audit_astra.md)

**Playback and interchange expose different kinds of state.** TypeScript `recur` bakes a known input history, choosing full storage or checkpoints against a 64 MiB budget, and refuses live host input. Rust’s `Latest` can advance state and restart from initialization on rewind. Its shared-instance draw path refuses live stepped kernels. Two bosses given the same clock and current hit vector can have opposite angular velocities from earlier hits. Rewinding with only the current vector cannot reproduce both. A 120 Hz controller catching up from a 50 ms presentation gap spans six physical ticks. Without an event time, applying one impulse on all six ticks imparts six times its momentum. This is a derived failure case, separate from the single-crab measurements. Reject treating a timestamp as a complete identity for a live performance. [Recurrence](../../src/map/recur.ts) · [Rust GPU host](../../rust/crates/loft-gpu/src/lib.rs) · [Host contract](../../docs/keel.md)

Loft can already export animated glTF. Sweeps have station joints, other eligible surfaces can use morph targets, and the baker compares vertex positions at keys and midpoints. It starts with 32 pilot keys and caps refinement in time at 1,024 keys. This answers whether another renderer can reproduce the surface within a measured tolerance. A model asking where the bird looked or which joint is its load-bearing ankle needs an anatomical correspondence. Refining a swept neck doubles potential station joints without adding a vertebra to the intended rig. A foreign capture brings its own rest axes, scale, root motion and contact timing. Mapping names alone can preserve every local rotation while putting both soles above the floor. Throw out the assumption that skinning channels constitute the whole interop contract. Credit the existing export and charge the cost of recovering semantic joints and contact after retargeting. [Animated bake](../../src/bake/motion.ts) · [glTF writer](../../src/bake/gltf.ts) · [Interop goal](../../docs/design/motion.md)

## What survives a change of body?

**Spore solved a harder problem than scaling a walk cycle.** Hecker and colleagues let an animator pose one creature and describe relationships that survive a change of skeleton. A grasper can take fruit to a mouth even when neither occupies the original joint index. A separate gait system handles user motion across curved paths and uneven ground. Animators provided speed-dependent gait parameters for foot groups of sizes one through six. At seven and above, the system generated parameters procedurally. Footless creatures could acquire pseudo-feet on the spine. This was a considered response to bodies that did not exist when the game shipped. The authors specifically attribute separation of gait authoring from their general retargeting machinery to development history. [Hecker et al., especially §§4.2–4.3](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)

The cost surfaces when a foot or grasper acquires a second job. Suppose a crab braces its large claw on the ground as it climbs, then uses it to present an object. The same endpoint has crossed a boundary between gait and gesture, carrying a world contact through the crossing. The two-phase particle IK in the paper solves the spine, then limbs against the fixed spine. Once in that second phase, a high-priority claw target cannot drive a compensating torso shift through that solve. An earlier stage must have anticipated it. Semantic authoring and the support for asymmetric groups are worth inheriting. Discard the privileged division of labour between locomotor and expressive parts. Its cost is another interface to learn and another coordination rule at precisely the moment the animal looks most alive. [Spore solver and gait design](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)

**Overgrowth gives an animator leverage through a particular animal.** David Rosen builds motion outward from responsive character movement. Pass and reach poses, a stride wheel, tilt about the centre of mass and a spring shared by crouching and landing give a small team control over a large repertoire. Reusing that crouch spring is a better economy than commissioning a landing for every height. The economy depends on facts about the body. A rabbit’s two alternating legs compress under a torso. Four feet planted on one side of a long creature can require quite different joint motions under the same torso rotation. A spring can be perfectly timed while the torso tilts away from every available support. [Rosen, *An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation*](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/Rosen_David_Animation_Bootcamp_An.pdf)

Order alone can destroy a result. If a later pass tilts the whole posed hierarchy by 10° about its centre, a previously locked toe 1 m from the rotation axis moves through a chord of about 17.4 cm. If the tilt precedes the foot correction, a straight leg may run out of reach instead. This calculation illustrates the cost of copying the recipe into a new rig. Rosen’s talk does not report that slip in Overgrowth. A director can tune an overlap stack for a known skeleton and camera. Asking every author to discover the order in which aim, tilt, spring, grip and foot locks survive each other turns saved keyframes into debugging time. Keep the reuse of simple physical responses. Throw out the assumption that the order of corrections can remain implicit in a general library. [Rosen’s movement and ragdoll sequences](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/Rosen_David_Animation_Bootcamp_An.pdf)

**Rain World makes a different bargain.** The slugcat can tumble through its environment as two spherical chunks held a fixed distance apart. Limbs and tail supply a much richer visible animal. In the developers’ animation presentation, a limb can seek a nearby grip, latch on and choose again once the body has moved past it. That is valuable evidence for feet whose next step depends on their current situation. The art also benefits from the separation of a cheap gameplay body from many expressive details. A faithful reproduction of every leg’s dynamics would have spent the simulation budget on features already legible from the silhouette. [Jakobsson and Therrien’s animation talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023475/Animation-Bootcamp-Rainworld-Animation) · [Developer account of the two-chunk body](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)

The limitation becomes visible if the slugcat’s cosmetic grip is asked to arrest a fall. Two chunks connected by a distance do not gain an external force just because the drawn fingers closed on a ledge. In 3D, two points also leave rotation about their connecting axis undetermined. A forearm bearing weight on a twisted ledge and a bill maintaining its roll both expose that missing degree of freedom. A specialist can invent it case by case. The author of a new creature pays for those cases through custom code, especially across a camera change. Keep persistent local choices, reduced bodies and drawn embellishment. Reject promoting a visual attachment to load-bearing status without some account of its effect on the body.

**Control Rig makes the order editable, at the price of encoding it in a rig.** Unreal’s Full Body IK node gives the rigger a root, effectors, per-bone stiffness, preferred angles and limits. Epic’s example changes pelvis rotation and position stiffness to 0.8 to control how a reach propagates. Its node reference lists 20 solver iterations by default. These are appropriate controls for a technical animator adjusting a known asset. Twenty iterations describes compute effort. It gives no bound in millimetres on the toe’s residual. A knee preference meaningful in one bone’s local axes can become an elbow bend in the wrong direction after changing the rest basis. An author then has to inspect the hierarchy, graph order, effector weights and imported axes to work out which setting changed the picture. [Epic’s Full Body IK guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/control-rig-full-body-ik-in-unreal-engine) · [Node defaults](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/node-reference/ControlRig/FullBodyIK)

There is also a time dimension outside the IK node. At one instant the feet can both reach their ground targets, the pelvis can be centred between them and the knees can bend correctly, while the character is moving too fast to stop there. For a constant-height inverted pendulum, a centre 1 m above level ground moving at 2 m/s has a capture point about 0.64 m ahead of it. If the forward support ends 0.15 m ahead, the current pose alone cannot establish recoverability under that model. Discard the practice of treating a successful whole-body pose solve as a locomotion controller. Retain the ability to override an individual joint and inspect the resulting pose. A reusable rule should survive an asset edit without an agent reconstructing solver settings from a screenshot.

## What survives an unexpected event?

**Motion matching buys acting by storing it.** For Honor’s important production decision was to ask gameplay for the intended trajectory and events, then continuously find footage from which to continue. Artists could add a take and see its contribution. Clavet’s presentation explicitly includes procedural warping after matching to meet gameplay and environment requirements. A published Ubisoft follow-up gives a reproducible 27-dimensional locomotion query including foot positions and velocities, hip velocity, and trajectory positions and directions 20, 40 and 60 frames ahead at 60 Hz. That is a one-second forecast. A reversal makes the old forecast wrong immediately. Even an instantaneous search can only offer entrances present in the bank. [Clavet’s For Honor presentation](https://ubm-twvideo01.s3.amazonaws.com/o1/vault/gdc2016/Presentations/Clavet_Simon_MotionMatching.pdf) · [Holden et al., §3](https://staticctf.ubisoft.com/J3yJr34U2pZ2Ieem48Dwy9uqj5PNUQTn/2EVXGPN6ynTrrJaaAUHmZS/060e223cce6a84e47e925105cac5e17f/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)

For scale, ten minutes at 60 Hz gives 36,000 candidates. A flat 27-float index alone occupies 3,888,000 bytes at f32, before animation data. A full scan examines 972,000 feature components per query. Indexing, pruning, sharing one bank among many characters and learned compression all change this bill. None supplies the missing performance of a short-legged crab entering a sharp turn with its heavy claw planted. Three planted points illustrate the geometric problem. One rigid alignment of the chosen take preserves their three pairwise distances. If a new body planted with different spacing, at least one contact must move or the pose must deform. A good match distance cannot tell the director which promise was broken. Keep performances, event labels and retrievable examples. Discard database coverage as the price of responding to a new support arrangement. Charge capture, cleanup and markup to authoring even when a scene calls the bank in three words.

**Euphoria earns its place at the second hit.** NaturalMotion’s active character can try to stay upright, take protective action and recover from configurations nobody captured. Its developers describe parameters for reactive behaviours, extra forces, stronger constraints and supplementary animation for direction. Their production problem was the enormous variety of reactions that a game can induce. On Loft’s boss, two equal impulses applied on opposite sides of the centre of mass can produce the same translation and opposite angular impulses. For a perpendicular 10 N·s impulse at a 0.5 m lever arm the magnitude is 5 N·m·s. A scalar “hurt amount” loses the sign before the motion system even sees it. Physical state preserves an artistically consequential distinction cheaply in the input. [NaturalMotion developer interview](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)

The price of the middleware approach is that changing a response can mean discovering the parameters of a behaviour and layering a compensating force or animation over it. A director asking for the eyes to hold for three frames while the shoulder gives way needs to know which controller currently owns each. Strengthening a drive can stiffen recovery as well as improve the pose on the desired frame. The public account establishes tunability and CPU synthesis. It supplies no comparable per-creature GPU timing or demonstration that a consumer can replace the internal balance law on an invented anatomy. Do not impute either result to Euphoria. Keep evolving state and purposeful recovery. Discard the behaviour package as the smallest unit an author can meaningfully change.

**MuJoCo answers the forward-dynamics question precisely enough to be useful.** Given bodies, masses and inertias, constraints, actuator commands, contacts and a timestep, it computes how the configuration evolves. Soft contact makes force determination and inverse dynamics tractable. The contact model itself exposes a meaningful choice. With contact dimensionality 1 it models the normal only. At 3 it also models tangential friction, and 4 and 6 introduce torsional and rolling effects. Giving the outside foot friction can change the crab’s turn. Giving every piece of visible shell its own frictional collision geometry vastly increases authoring detail without specifying when the crab ought to pick up a foot. [MuJoCo computation and contact model](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/)

In a simple example with two level supports and no other vertical force, the normal loads must sum to the weight in static equilibrium. Equal sharing additionally requires suitable geometry and moment balance. With multiple redundant contacts, posture, compliance and actuation can affect the force distribution. Neither the word “planted” nor a centre of mass inside a polygon uniquely determines how a crab carries its claw. Asking an author to supply a simulator model, motor limits, gains, contact materials *and* an expressive controller for a fixed two-second eyebrow gesture is an extravagant exchange of author time for unused capability. Treat a dynamics simulator as a legitimate source of trajectories or live reactions. Discard the presumption that attaching a body to one produces a motor intention, or that a soft-contact trajectory certifies an exact lock.

**Jakobsen and Müller make correction cheap enough to use everywhere; that invites using it everywhere.** Verlet integration stores current and previous positions. Relaxation projects distance errors away. Müller et al. generalized position-based projection to constraints, a considerable advantage when penetrations or extensions need to be corrected directly. Work roughly follows active constraints times sweeps times substeps. For 48 scalar constraints, eight sweeps and two substeps, that is 768 projections per creature per displayed frame, excluding collision discovery, actuation and drawing. Independent creatures can run in parallel. Within one creature, corrections sharing a hip compete for its position, so iteration order or synchronization matters. [Jakobsen, *Advanced Character Physics*](https://www.gpgstudy.com/gpgiki/GDC%202001%3A%20Advanced%20Character%20Physics) · [Müller et al., *Position Based Dynamics*](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/posBasedDyn.pdf)

Take three equal free particles on a line at 0, 1 and 3 m, joined by two sticks of rest length 1. In a left-to-right sweep the first stick needs no correction. The second moves its endpoints to 1.5 and 2.5 m. One supposedly enforced stick now measures 1.5 m. This exact arithmetic counterexample rules out “one projection per constraint means every length holds.” It says nothing against the look of a loose antenna after that sweep. Classical PBD also couples apparent stiffness to the timestep and iteration count. XPBD introduces compliance and a constraint multiplier, addressing that coupling while leaving finite convergence relevant. More sweeps also consume time on creatures that need only a simple analytic bend. Discard iteration count as a physical material or a hold certificate. Keep fast local projection wherever its residual and silhouette justify the cost. [Macklin, Müller and Chentanez, *XPBD*](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf)

## What survives a change of direction from the animator?

**Spacetime constraints and Disney timing both exploit knowledge of the future.** Witkin and Kass’s jumping lamp is an unusually direct ancestor of the Loft heron. The animator specifies a hurdle and a landing. Doubling the base mass changes anticipation and follow-through. Changing the desired landing force changes the manner of arrival. In their basic jump the foot was nailed to the floor for the first and last five frames. The authors supplied the contact intervals. Each jumping example took under ten minutes on a Symbolics 3640. Hardware has changed dramatically. The enduring cost is that moving the target can require reconsidering the whole push, including frames that the audience may already have seen. Once the character is airborne, an unreachable future foothold does not become reachable because an offline solve can move yesterday’s takeoff. Keep spacetime direction for known shots. Discard its lookahead as an explanation for live recovery. [Witkin and Kass, §6](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/pub_files/pub1/witkin_andrew_1988_1/witkin_andrew_1988_1.pdf)

Disney’s timing, anticipation, arcs, squash, exaggeration and overlap explain why the first bird’s intention reads at camera distance. Staging tells the animator which effect should dominate when several physically possible movements coincide. These principles developed where artists owned the timing and could judge a finished sequence. Their cost in a library comes when an engine turns each principle into a knob, or retimes all channels equally. At 24 fps a three-frame anticipation occupies 125 ms. Before a known jump that pause can sell effort. Before an unforeseen input it would grant the animal foreknowledge, and after the input it introduces a conspicuous delay if applied to every part. An arm can already be recoiling as the other hand finishes a directed point. Keep the ability to stage exceptions, including deliberately holding a pose. Throw out the generic “make it springier” pass and the requirement to rekey every dependent part to change one beat. [Lasseter, *Principles of Traditional Animation Applied to 3D Computer Animation*](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~fussell/courses/cs384g-fall2011/projects/animator/lasseter.pdf)

**Learning shifts where the author pays.** DeepMimic learns a policy that turns state, phase and goals into PD joint targets at 30 Hz. Its human, robot, dinosaur and dragon examples matter. They demonstrate that imitation plus a simulator can acquire skills across very different bodies. The commonality is the training method. A policy’s observed links and output joint targets still depend on the particular body. Adding an actuated joint changes the action interface. A director moving the contact earlier can invalidate the reference phase and the policy that makes the move recoverable. At 30 Hz one policy period is 33.3 ms even if the physical solver handles a collision sooner. Keep demonstration as a cheap way to describe a complex action. Discard trained tracking reward as an author’s guarantee of the event frame. [Peng et al., *DeepMimic*, §§5–7](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/DeepMimic/DeepMimic_2018.pdf)

AMP removes a different burden. A discriminator scores pairs of successive states, allowing style to come from unstructured clips while a separate task reward directs behaviour. This saves explicit clip selection and alignment. A credible succession of local weight shifts can nevertheless miss the one frame at which the boss is meant to open its grip. The style score does not identify the author’s grip obligation, and changing its global weight can alter the walk as well as the hand. ASE amortizes skill acquisition further by learning a reusable latent skill representation, reporting over a decade of *simulated experience* through parallel GPU simulation. A downstream controller can reuse a recovery skill with little new motion data. The remaining author cost is finding or learning controls for a particular edit and establishing that the rest of the performance survived it. Simulated years measure data exposure, not wall time or inference cost. Keep the repertoires and the ability to import their movement. Throw out the use of a reward or latent coordinate as Loft’s only vocabulary for local direction. [Peng et al., *AMP*](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/AMP/AMP_2021.pdf) · [Peng et al., *ASE*](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/ASE/)

SuperTrack attacks the cost of learning the tracker itself. Fussell and colleagues train a differentiable approximation to the simulator’s transitions and backpropagate tracking error through that model to train the policy. Their interactive demonstration used 10–20 minutes of recorded controller interaction. They report roughly 20–40 hours for full training in their setups. The learned world model helps during training, so charging its entire cost to every displayed frame would misstate the result. The shipping controller still needs a reference and a physical character to track it. A dog following previously unseen movement is a real capability. An animator editing one neck limit faces a different question: which reference targets still make sense and over what range does this controller now recover? A tracking error or survival rate averaged over episodes cannot answer whether that particular bird held sight of its fish. Keep the tracker as an optional source of physical performance. Discard retraining as the expected edit loop for a joint law.
