## The audience can see which foot took the weight

At 0.9 seconds in Loft's hop comparison, three herons look across the rail. The bird in the second row points its bill at the ground. Rotating the torso moves its hip and changes the direction in which its knee bends. That gets the lower leg over the rail. At its tightest approach the lower leg has about 0.2 mm to spare. The torso, neck and bill clearance constraints each have more than 0.76 m to spare during flight. Yet the bill base travels from 1.38 m above the hips at rest to 0.39 m below them. A viewer sees an animal suddenly dive at something. The solver has found an economical way to move an ankle. [Four herons, side by side](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/heron_hop_4.webp) · [Diagnosis from the solved curves](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

What did the animal intend? What had already happened when it changed its mind? Which commitments survived to the geometry on screen? A director can answer the first question seconds ahead. A player can change the answer next tick. Either way the left foot may already carry the body, the right foot may be in flight, and an eye may still follow something the torso has turned away from.

Give two crabs the same position, heading, velocity, destination and clock time. In one history the outside foot landed 20 cm farther forward to avoid a stone. Remove the stone. Until another step the feet should still differ by 20 cm. A deterministic function of the five shared inputs must return the same answer twice. Conversely a known hop can be drawn at its apex without replaying takeoff. Discard the assumption that every part of a creature has the same relationship to time. [Loft's stance on state and replaceable laws](../../docs/PHILOSOPHY.md)

## Legs for a creature nobody has seen before

**Spore** had to animate a creature the player could assemble after the animator finished work. Hecker and colleagues recorded the meaning of a selection and its movement frame. A reach can resolve to whichever graspers exist at runtime. The gait system separately groups legs of similar length and approximates their length ratios with small rational numbers to coordinate frequencies. Both techniques allow instructions to precede the body that will perform them. [Hecker et al., especially §§3–5](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)

Two descriptions can produce the same pose on the author's specimen and diverge on the player's. The team reports hours to make an animation on one creature and weeks to learn which constructions generalize. Across testing involving hundreds of creatures and a thousand animations, their production review pass rate was about 90%. Their example of rubbing one's chin exposes the limitation. The movement modes lack the jaw's surface boundary, and the solver omits self-collision. More chin tags could still put the hand inside an unfamiliar jaw. Discard the fixed vocabulary of spatial relationships as the limit of authorship. Preserve late binding and the decision to keep passive jiggle from spoiling an authored pose. [Spore, results and limitations](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)

**Overgrowth** gets much of a rabbit's running silhouette from a handful of authored extremes, interpolation and procedural adjustments. This suited an indie developer who could judge the silhouette and edit its code. The released script's leg list has exactly two entries, `left_leg` and `right_leg`. It names elements of these chains and dispatches foot effects to those two sides. Give that program a crab with eight legs, one short. A rig edit cannot tell a two-entry effects dispatcher which of the other six feet just landed. Keep the expressive extremes. Discard the built-in inventory of limbs as the rig description. Wolfire's open script also makes the cost of fixing that assumption knowable. [Rosen's GDC demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE) · [Wolfire's character script](https://github.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/blob/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as#L660)

**Rain World's** slugcat has two linked collision chunks. They rotate and tumble through tunnels while rendered limbs and tail convey much of the effort. Across over 100 creatures, the developers describe extensive species-specific scripts. That investment is visible in the animals' differences. The revealing edit for Loft is to have one paw catch a ledge and arrest the fall. Drawing the paw on the ledge can sell the catch. Until contact affects the movement of the chunks, releasing the paw cannot change the fall. The developer must write that coupling. Discard the permanent division between decorative limbs and a privileged physical body. Preserve the freedom to spend only two chunks where two suffice. [Videocult developers on the slugcat and creature scripting](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)

Loft's analytic `chain` handles two or three straight segments, a bend side and, for three segments, a preferred final direction. I evaluated a two-link chain on the TypeScript CPU twin with a root at zero, two 1 m links, a perpendicular bend and a target 2.2 m away. The drawn tip is at 2.0 m. An effect placed at the target would appear 20 cm beyond the toe. Clamping gives a sensible pose cheaply. The author still owes the shot its anatomical limits and contact decision. Discard any inference of contact from success in evaluating IK. [Loft limb construction](../../src/geom/limb.ts) · [CPU evaluator](../../src/expr.ts)

## A crab that changes its mind

On a known course, an integer gait cycle identifies the foot's stance. Sampling the course at mid-stance determines a world anchor. A curve gives inner and outer feet different distances for free. The scramble uses signed lateral displacement as its phase input. On reversal all eight feet retrace their earlier phases. Feeding accumulated absolute distance would advance the phase, but reconstructing the old plant would still require the old course. Discard phase as sole authority over which patch of ground supports a live foot. Direct phase evaluation earns its place in a choreographed walk. [Gait law and its documented limits](../../src/geom/gait.ts) · [Scramble comparison](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/crab_scramble_3.webp)

Even two correct stationary targets can yield a false contact when blended. On `main` I held a one-foot straight gait at time zero, with stride 0.8 m, duty 0.5 and phase zero. In the identity ground frame its walk target is 0.2 m ahead and its standing home is zero. With no stamps or replants, I evaluated `footing` twice, changing only the walk weight. At 0.25 the foot is at 0.05 m and `planted(0)` reads 1. At 0.75 it is at 0.15 m and the flag again reads 1. Both component flags are set throughout the transition. The mix therefore moves the foot **10 cm while reporting it planted**. One interpolation and a threshold at 0.5 made stance changes cheap to author. They also made the flag unsuitable as evidence for a footprint, a hard hold or an impulse transmitted through the foot. This failure is reproducible without a GPU or any creature asset. [Foot position and planted flag in the same function](../../src/geom/stance.ts#L397)

The fixed-course walk exposes a different omission. The branch compared an authored bob and roll against four root channels with 14 spline controls each on a 6 m radius turn. The feet were identical. The solved carriage stayed within **3.7 mm** of the course and at height **2.50 m** throughout. None of the reach bounds became active. The solve spent about 0.2 s to remove practically the only indication that the legs were doing work. There is no cause in the objective for a shoulder to sink when a particular foot accepts weight. Adding masses alone would still leave that decision open. For eight coplanar feet taking vertical loads, total weight and two horizontal moment balances supply only three independent scalar equations for eight normal forces. Even the static case can leave five independent ways to shift load, subject to nonnegative forces. A crab carrying a prize in one claw and one quietly sneaking past can make different choices on identical ground. Discard the claim that either smoothness or a mass table determines a performance. [Walk comparison and measurements](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md) · [Earlier motion audit](../2026-11-19_audits/motion_audit_astra.md)

The scramble puts numbers on what remembering a foot buys. A baked recurrence steps eight feet at 120 Hz. It carries a planted position, swing origin and progress, and arbitrates among neighbours that want to lift. In the fixed-swing version velocity changes sign at **1.792 s**. The next fresh liftoff is **2.150 s**, 0.358 s later, against a prediction of at most 0.2 s. An already swinging leg can retarget earlier. Stored positions showed zero slide across pairs of fully planted samples. The crab took exactly three steps per foot and never swung two designated neighbours at once. Allowing duration and lift to depend on urgency changed the total from 24 to 23 and produced low, hurried steps as short as 0.12 s. Those results describe a specific controller following prerecorded motion. Its root leans by a prescribed function of acceleration. The feet cannot make it lose balance. [Scramble results](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md) · [Foot selection and readout](../../../_wt/loft-motion/examples/motion/lib/stepper.ts)

The distinction matters when copying the experiment. Its capture-point approximation uses the height and speed of a fixed-height inverted pendulum. Its urgency measures distance from a centroid. Two support configurations can have the same centroid and completely different capacity to carry a turn. Imagine feet at the corners of two rectangles centred at zero. Give one a lateral half-width of 0.10 m and the other 1.0 m. A pressure point 0.30 m off centre lies outside the first and inside the second. The centroid distance is identical. Likewise the branch's neighbour rule hardcodes two rows of four with opposite index offsets of four. A ninth appendage that occasionally bears weight has no slot in that reasoning. Discard the interpretation of this heuristic as balance or body-independent gait. Keep the observed advantages of placing the next step from the feet's actual positions. [Stepper equations](../../../_wt/loft-motion/examples/motion/lib/stepper.ts)

There is an instructive speed reversal. At 512² and the same instant, the fixed-swing bake gave **0.59 ms** for the whole frame against **0.66 ms** for the gait, least of 60. Pose stages were 0.131 and 0.197 ms. The difference is about one GPU clock tick. The 480-step bake took 3.1 ms GPU, 15.7 ms wall, with another 309 ms of pipeline setup reported separately. Keeping the run consumed 0.21 MB. This demonstrates cheap playback of a known reversal. It says nothing about the p95 cost of five independent crabs changing direction on live input. Selling the smaller number as controller latency would reward doing yesterday's work before the clock starts. [Measured bake, setup and draw costs](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md)

## What an animator means by a beat

Disney's principles concern what the audience understands. Lasseter illustrates timing by turning the same head between the same extremes. Two inbetweens can read as a nervous tic, ten as stretching a sore muscle. The historical tool was a sequence of drawings in which the animator owned every frame and knew what came next. Loft's `anticipated`, `dragged`, `squash` and `echo` are cheap ways to express parts of that craft. The mistake is to apply each principle as an independent, always-on signal. A boss that deliberately holds its face still while an antenna finishes swinging needs precisely zero facial overlap during that hold. A startled boss cannot coil before an unexpected impact. Keep the author's power to withhold movement. Discard a checklist that awards motion for exhibiting every principle everywhere. [Lasseter on timing and staging](https://graphics.cs.cmu.edu/nsp/course/15-464/Fall05/papers/lasseter.pdf) · [Loft's principles](../../docs/animation_principles.md) · [A held expression in Loft](../../src/geom/struck.ts)

Clips preserve a performance at its recorded timing. The cost of blend trees emerges when gameplay interrupts it. Two valid world-space plants 20 cm apart blend to a midpoint 10 cm from either. Local joint interpolation can produce a different error through forward kinematics. Phase synchronization alone fixes neither. A second hit introduces another entering posture and another decision about which limb may yield. Artists made bespoke transitions because they could inspect one splice and make it good. Maintenance grows with the pairs of states requiring continuity. Discard pose weights as a general rule for transferring contact, momentum or ownership of an accent. [Clavet's account of the transition-authoring problem](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Clavet_Simon_MotionMatching.pdf)

**For Honor moved the edit from transitions to search.** Ubisoft compares the current pose and velocity and the desired future trajectory against recorded candidates. An animator can supply correlated movement of the feet, pelvis, shoulders and hands in one take. A small function can call the search, but somebody still performed, imported and marked up the assets. Clavet's slides explicitly give game code control of the simulation point, allow the animated entity a 15 cm displacement around it, and describe corrections after selection. A nearest neighbour answers which performance is available. It cannot produce a missing continuation with a particular foot attached to a particular moving deck. Adjusting feature weights trades trajectory error against pose discontinuity. Neither weight has units of allowable foot slip. Discard nearest-match score as evidence that contact or a beat survived the splice. Keep the performed asymmetry a matching take genuinely carries. [Clavet, motion matching in For Honor](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Clavet_Simon_MotionMatching.pdf)

Ubisoft's later learned motion matching study priced this bargain. In its **bear** scenario, basic matching used **995.6 MB** and averaged **94 μs/frame**. The learned version used **7.1 MB**, averaged **340 μs/frame** and required **29.3 hours** of training. The authors also removed terrain IK and observed sliding and penetration. Restoring IK while training only on flat movement still produced unbalanced-looking terrain traversal. Those are two different missing pieces, geometric correction and a performed response to changing ground. These figures belong to that study's hardware, search cadence and data, with no direct conversion to a Loft GPU draw. They show why compressing a bank may be worthwhile while leaving its coverage and authoring problems intact. [Holden et al., Table 3 and terrain ablation](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/09154101/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)

## A physical reaction and a directed performance

**Euphoria** earns its reputation on the second disturbance. The first shove changes the character's momentum and support. The second acts on the altered state. Authors could never record every starting condition. NaturalMotion describes behaviours directed through parameters, supplementary animations, forces and stronger constraints. Consider two disturbances 0.12 s apart while the boss holds a small prop. Increasing hand drive can preserve grip by hauling the shoulder around. Relaxing recovery can preserve the shoulder's acting while losing the grip. The animator has to discover the interaction by tuning gains and replaying the simulation. The published discussion supplies no comparable per-creature GPU timing. Discard the packaged behaviour as the smallest editable unit. Preserve recovery from the state the first disturbance actually left. [NaturalMotion developers on Euphoria's controls](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)

Loft can already give the boss a reaction. A pendulum accepts signed pivot-velocity impulses, so a known pair of impacts has a cheap closed-form sum. Titan's brow uses 14 stations per antenna, delayed sway, a whip and a distinct release cue. The failure comes when the response to hit two depends on which foot slipped after hit one. Superposition assumes the same linear law and support throughout. A one-slot host input holding only the most recent impulse also discards the first one's unfinished swing. Repeating its values on all catch-up ticks could instead apply a single event several times if the consumer treats a level as an edge. On a 120 Hz clock, a 50 ms presentation gap spans six ticks. Discard both blanket diagnoses, that Loft lacks secondary motion and that secondary motion solves primary recovery. The claw can remain in character even while another part needs history. [Loft pendulum](../../src/geom/pendulum.ts) · [Titan brow and antennae](../../examples/bosses/titan/face/brow.ts) · [Keel event ownership](../../docs/keel.md)

**Unreal Control Rig** lets an artist choose a root, expose end effectors, specify limits and exclude bones from position-based full-body IK. Epic's mannequin walkthrough gives the knee a preferred 45° bend on Z and limits ankle Z to −70° through +70° to repair visible issues. Those choices refer to local frames and require fresh interpretation on another skeleton. More iterations can reconcile competing demands at extra runtime cost. Even exact convergence for the current pose cannot decide when to release the back foot. Every foot can reach its target while the moving centre of mass makes a future step necessary. Keep the editable rig and local exceptions. Discard effector weight one as a contact certificate and a whole-body iterative solve as the default price of pointing one eye. [Epic's Control Rig setup and effector semantics](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/control-rig-full-body-ik-in-unreal-engine) · [Solver iteration settings](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/IKRig/FIKRigFBIKSettings)

**MuJoCo** can answer whether a modeled body can arrest a fall with the forces available. Its robotics and control work favours tractable regularized contact. The author supplies collision shapes, inertias, drives and timestep. Even a tangential load inside the friction cone can yield steady slip. The optional NoSlip pass costs extra and makes inverse dynamics ill-defined. At the default 0.002 s timestep, a second of motion requires 500 simulation steps, averaging 8⅓ per 60 Hz picture. For a claw posed on a railing at a named beat, translating the direction into gains, contact parameters and a controller adds work. Discard a simulator's contact pair as a promise of zero relative displacement. Physical simulation earns its cost when the consequence of losing contact matters. [MuJoCo computation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/) · [Modeling and slip](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html) · [Timestep definition](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#option-timestep)

**Jakobsen and Müller explain why a stable-looking animal can stretch.** Verlet-style particle prediction and iterative stick corrections made interactive bodies practical. Jakobsen reports using one to ten relaxation iterations depending on the object. Müller and colleagues generalized projection to many constraints. In the original PBD, changing the sweep count or timestep can change apparent stiffness. XPBD introduced compliance with explicit timestep scaling to address that defect. Convergence of several coupled constraints at a finite budget is a separate question. [Jakobsen, Advanced Character Physics](https://edelkas.github.io/n/files/bib/jakobsen.pdf) · [Müller et al., Position Based Dynamics](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/posBasedDyn.pdf) · [Macklin et al., XPBD](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf)

Place a fixed root at 0 and equal movable masses at 1 and 2.2 m, joined by two 1 m sticks. Project proximal length first, distal second. The distal correction splits 0.2 m equally, leaving masses at 1.1 and 2.1 m. The last constraint holds while the first stick is 10 cm too long. Further sweeps halve the excess in this example, leaving about 0.78 mm after eight. That softness may suit kelp, but reducing crowd iterations now changes a neck's apparent length. A hypothetical 48-row rig doing eight sweeps and two substeps issues 768 projections per picture before collision detection. Discard visual stability or a sweep count as evidence of an exact length.

## Why solving the whole shot helps, then fails

Witkin and Kass let an animator set conditions across time and optimize the motion between them. Their jumping lamp crouches in anticipation because it must produce takeoff momentum. Doubling the base mass changes its preparation and recovery. A small landing-force constraint changes a stomp into a softer landing. Few authored edits produce a coordinated change across the whole performance. But the authors enabled floor contact for the first and last five frames, and used an inelastic contact with infinite friction. The contact schedule and impact law contain a large part of the direction. The experiments took under ten minutes each on a Symbolics 3640, a historical measure of an offline technique. Unlimited compute today would still give a solver with knowledge of a surprise at frame 30 the opportunity to prepare at frame 29. A live character has no such information. Keep spacetime constraints for shots whose future the author owns. Discard implicitly giving a reactive character tomorrow's inputs. [Witkin and Kass, Spacetime Constraints, §§V–VI](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sequin/CS184/TEXT/p159-witkin.pdf)

Loft's build-time solver varies spline controls under holds and a preference. The original heron has five channels of 15 controls, **75 unknowns, 300 bytes of f32 coefficients** before layout. This makes a solved performance cheap to keep and seek. The Rust lane measured 309 steps and 117 ms for the base and 594 steps and 221 ms for the moved deck, best of nine under load. These are solve times. On the base check, 24 of 26 predicates came back `Holds` and two were undecided within precision. On the moved case the implementations agreed on verdicts while controls differed by as much as about 0.047, and the Rust solution retained a nonzero gradient along feasible directions. Describing the result as the optimum would overstate what the algorithm found. The earlier trial with a 3.6 m rail is even more decisive. The bird passed *under* it, satisfying a clearance condition that never specified the side. Discard a solver return, a proof about a few named stations, and minimum objective value as interchangeable judgments of a shot. [Rust solve and proof results](../2026-11-19_lean_rust_solve2.md) · [Rail experiment](../../examples/second/heron_leap_refused.ts) · [Hold geometry](../../examples/second/heron_leap.ts)

The branch fixed the diving bird by giving the head a degree of freedom, preferring the hand-keyed performance, constraining the attitude and end accelerations, and refining the knot spacing. Its six channels of 27 controls make **162 unknowns**. Peak body pitch fell from 67° to 12°, and peak upward acceleration from 4.8 g to 2.27 g. The solve for that variant took 7.6 s on the branch, above Loft's 2 s training build cap all by itself. The interventions changed together, so assigning the whole improvement to any one would be unwarranted. C² splines also force continuous acceleration into and out of a ballistic interval. That is a choice about contact mechanics hidden in a choice about curve smoothness. A bird with flexible legs can absorb a landing over time. A hard impact can change velocity at contact. A curve family good at one performance need not be physically authoritative about the other. Keep the author's prior and the observed benefits of correcting it. Discard least jerk as the default definition of intent. [Heron comparison and timings](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion.md) · [Loft's training budget](../../CORE.md)

**A heron following a fish needs more than a level bill.** Troje and Frost measured walking pigeons holding the head nearly stationary across several dimensions, with small systematic slip, followed by a thrust. A study of ten pigeons on a moving perch varied translation from 0.25 to 4 Hz and rotation from 0.25 to 2 Hz. Compensation differed by type of perturbation. A heron's bob frequency cannot be read off these pigeon experiments. A look ray can stay on a fish while the head translates. A skull can stay level while the eyes lose the fish. Two birds looking along one ray can differ in roll. A single look-at calculation erases those distinctions. [Troje and Frost](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10667977/) · [Theunissen and Troje](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00551/full)

There is a one-frame failure on `main`. I aimed `lookAt` from the identity frame at two points just behind the head, (−1, 0, −0.001) and (−1, 0, +0.001) m. With yaw limited to 0.5 radians, the CPU twin returned −0.5 and +0.5. The targets differ by about **0.115°** in bearing. The head commands differ by **57.3°**. Clamping a signed angle across its wrap point produced the jump. More smoothing would spread a turn across several frames without settling whether the eyes should release the target or the body should turn. [Look calculation and limits](../../src/geom/motion.ts)

The branch's 1.55 m two-arc neck and critically damped follower gave the head independent motion. The owner still saw a bird looking down too long. The landing spot was below the bird, exactly where the target told it to look. A brief glance instead produced 15° downward pitch at 0.250 s and level gaze from takeoff at 0.6 s. Smoothing the handoff reduced peak sampled jerk over 0.6–1.75 s from 670 to 428 m/s³. The revised readout's 0.17 mm neck-length error comes from 128 chords at selected times. It returns `pass: true` as a diagnostic. A chord longer than 1.55 m remains impossible however smooth the follower. The target also uses the shoulder's future progress through 0.2 s windows. Discard the equation of a successful authored glance with causal tracking of a moving fish. [Revised gaze scene](../../../_wt/loft-motion/examples/motion/heron_hop_gaze.ts) · [Revised comparison](../../../_wt/loft-motion/agent_notes/2026-11-19_motion/heron_hop_gaze.webp)

## What learning buys, and what the author still owes

**DeepMimic** uses a reference motion, imitation rewards and a policy driving physical joints. It can preserve a performer's style through disturbances, and the dragon and T. rex demonstrate that the method extends beyond people. The examples required character models, joint drives, reference performances and training. A typical individual humanoid skill took about **60 million samples and two days on eight CPU cores**. Policies ran at 30 Hz with Bullet at 1.2 kHz, forty physics ticks per decision. The paper describes an instructive training failure in which the character lies on the ground miming the reference. Reference-state initialization and early termination help learning discover the intended behaviour. A beautifully short call to a policy conceals those choices. Changing a forelimb into a supporting wing changes its observations, action meaning and training example. Discard independent training for every such edit as the ordinary cost of making a new creature move. Preserve imitation as a powerful external source of physical performances. [Peng et al., DeepMimic](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/DeepMimic/DeepMimic_2018.pdf)

**AMP** removes the need to track a particular reference frame. A discriminator learns which motion transitions look like the examples, while a task reward says where to go or what to accomplish. That removes manual synchronization and lets the character choose when to step. The published tasks used **100–300 million samples** and roughly **30–140 hours on 16 CPU cores** per policy. The largest example bank in that paper contained 434 seconds from eight actors. Give a director a successful policy and ask for one toe to stay on a chalk mark through 1.2 s. A sequence can be typical of the acting style and have its toe miss by 2 cm. The discriminator's score does not identify the toe, supply the missing distance, or locate the offending frame. More demonstrations can improve likelihood while increasing preparation cost. Discard a style score as a hard acceptance condition for an individual performance. [Peng et al., AMP](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/AMP/AMP_2021.pdf)

**ASE** attacks retraining by learning reusable skills and letting a higher-level policy select among them in a continuous latent space. The experiment used roughly 30 minutes of motion, 4,096 parallel environments and over ten billion samples, about ten days on a V100 for the low-level policy. Reuse across tasks amortizes this work. The low-level control ran at 30 Hz and the higher-level control at 6 Hz. A regular high-level decision interval is therefore about 167 ms. Physical response and the lower-level controller can act inside it. That distinction matters for an author asking *which* hand may interrupt a gesture *this* frame. A latent action that invokes a convincing recovery has no intrinsic interpretation as that single joint-level exception. Discard an anonymous control coordinate as the only way to intervene in a learned repertoire. [Peng et al., ASE, §10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01906)

**SuperTrack** avoids reinforcement-learning reward design for much of the tracking problem. A learned model predicts what the physical character will do, allowing supervised optimization of a policy that supplies motor targets. The authors report roughly 20–40 hours of full training and train one shared policy on a flat-ground subset of the approximately 6.5-hour LaFAN data, retargeted to one character size. Their example of a physically impossible reference is wonderfully specific. One person pulls another to standing by the hand, but the tracking setup lacks the other person's help. No improvement in imitation can supply the missing external force. A survival-rate statistic measures how long episodes avoid failure. It cannot tell an animator whether the supporting hand is still on the other actor at the chosen beat. Discard treating a kinematic reference as a complete description of the forces and partners that made it possible. [Fussell et al., SuperTrack](https://staticctf.ubisoft.com/J3yJr34U2pZ2Ieem48Dwy9uqj5PNUQTn/OXYoOEqVwwtxSMiPzMIva/302e9a76da0b1f95ab62d6c766179868/SuperTrack.pdf)

**MotionBricks makes the incoming performance much cheaper to ask for.** Its modular generative model accepts keyframes and navigation or interaction guidance. The authors report **2 ms latency to generate the motion between two keyframes and 15,000 generated frames/s on an RTX 5090**. These are compatible because one inference generates many future frames. Dividing one second by 15,000 does not give the response time to an unexpected input, still less the time to draw a posed creature. Their dataset contains over 350,000 motions, yet the paper identifies only one example of vaulting at 0.5 m. It reports self-collision as a possible output, uses a separate physical tracker for the robot, and describes months of iteration for accurate offline G1 retargeting. A nonexpert's short authoring session is a real win once that groundwork exists. The groundwork belongs in the cost of changing bodies. Discard the assumption that generating more frames resolves rare contacts, retargeting or the meaning of a hold. Preserve an author's freedom to bring in such a performance and edit it. [Wang et al., MotionBricks, especially §§7–8](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/motionbricks/pdfs/motionbricks_siggraph_2026.pdf) · [Released rigs and runtime](https://github.com/NVlabs/motionbricks)

## The costs hidden by Loft's public examples

An exported animation and an editable body answer different questions. Loft already writes animated glTF, including skinned stations on swept bodies and morph tracks where appropriate. Its public `Skeleton` supplies a joint count and, at each index, a position and radius. That is enough for capsule queries. It cannot distinguish two limbs with identical station centres and radii but different rotation around the limb's own axis. Rotate a forearm 90° about that axis and the palm can turn from facing down to facing sideways while every centre stays put. A consumer importing a gesture or exporting a grasp needs that distinction, a rest frame and a correspondence between anatomical joints. Naming generated stations after the fact cannot recover those data. Likewise, an imported track that places a toe at the origin on two adjacent keys tells us little about whether the toe is carrying a load or just brushing past.
