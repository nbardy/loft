I would use Loft for a procedural short or a game creature whose shape, motion, and contact points must agree. I would choose three.js or Babylon for an asset-driven browser project, and Bevy or raw wgpu for a Rust game. Loft earns its place when changing a claw’s proportions should also move its grip, warning shape, and release point.

The TypeScript renderer delivered. On an Apple M4 at 960 × 540, the lantern took 0.46 ms and the reefback titan took 3.28 ms in their best GPU frames. The titan took another 913 ms to load after compilation. Those numbers make interactive playback credible while showing what an artist pays to see an edit.

The largest adoption risk is the native handoff. The lantern produced five declared TypeScript draws. Its recorded Rust job contained 1,408 expression nodes and zero draw declarations. The installed native addon failed its version check. Typechecking passed, and a shared test lock prevented the focused suite from running.

I would adopt Loft as a specialist asset tool. Native picture parity, installable loaders, sustained performance measurements, reliable scene isolation, and one independently integrated creature would earn it a larger role in a project I expect to maintain.
