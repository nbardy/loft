# What should move when a crab puts its foot down?

Motion design audit · 19 November 2026

Watch the outside claw as the crab turns. The foot should land where it can take the turn, the shell should move over that foot, and the claw should carry on briefly after the shell checks its rotation. Section 3 addresses the last one well. Its effort objective has no way to discover the first two from segment masses alone. I would spend the first primary-motion experiment on constructing the transfer of weight between contacts. The Titan's existing recoil and antenna whip set the visual baseline.

## What the code actually draws

A foot in `gait` has an unusually good anchor. Its integer cycle selects a **world-space plant** from the course frame at the middle of stance. An inside foot and an outside foot therefore travel different distances on an arc for free. Between plants the horizontal interpolation is a smoothstep and the lift a half sine. If lift is 0.12 m, period 0.8 s and duty 0.66, the downward component just before landing on level ground is π × 0.12 / 0.272 = **1.39 m/s**. It becomes zero at contact. That impact sells a stamp and jars on a quiet step. Distance-paced gait also needs a consistent course behind and ahead of the current position. A game supplying only its latest heading cannot reconstruct an old plant through a turn. [Gait and course](../../src/geom/gait.ts)

The body has a separate clock-driven carriage. Idle can unload a scheduled foot, brace can coil before a strike and absorb its landing, and the walk handler adds oscillations. During a transition, footing mixes positions and switches the planted flag at a threshold. A flagged foot can slide. Even a perfectly stationary target can lie beyond the leg. The two-link construction clamps reach, the three-link version aims the last segment, and neither imposes anatomical limits. Measure the **drawn distal joint** in world space. Measure the screen-space silhouette separately. A planted flag, a reachable target and a convincing picture are three different outcomes. The placing layer already provides the root frame and rotations about an arbitrary axis. Keep it as the common endpoint. [Stance](../../src/geom/stance.ts) · [Analytic chains](../../src/geom/limb.ts) · [Frames](../../src/geom/placing.ts)

The lineup turns, draws its homes inward to reduce overstretch and suppresses short steps. The Titan rig shares per-frame foot targets with legs and effects. Its brow antennae each have 14 stations, delayed sway, a whip after impact and a distinct reaction at release. The mourner uses an actual small-angle driven pendulum. There is already a unit-step response, a travelling delay and a signed response to a pivot velocity impulse. The missing capability is cheap overlap **derived from an edited parent performance**, including starting and stopping. Preserve the alert antenna prick and the claw's grip timing. The heron already anticipates, flies with prescribed acceleration and absorbs a landing. Five scalar splines with 15 controls give 75 unknowns, 12 knot intervals per channel and 300 bytes of f32 controls before layout. Takeoff and touchdown coincide with knots at 0.6 and 1.2 s. Clearance covers named torso, bill and neck stations and selected leg segments. It is a claim about that geometry. The starting arch places the feet on the desired side of the rail. The Rust run on the base problem took 309 steps and 117 ms, the **best of nine under load**. The moved deck took 594 steps and 221 ms. Its verdicts agree with TS while its controls can differ by about 0.047. On the base clip, 24 of 26 verdicts were Holds and two were WithinPrecision. This establishes a build-time baseline. Rust also has stepped kernels retaining the latest state. Rewinding one resets it and advances again. Replaying today's input on yesterday's ticks cannot reproduce yesterday's game. ## Comparison contract

The following are candidate motion call sites, in crab, heron, boss order, all using the existing creature geometry. Fixture names stand for scene data or local adapters. `turn` is a sideways course from straight travel into a quarter turn, `walk` the eight-foot gait specification, `rig` a consumer of body, feet and ornament angles, `leapSpec(deck)` the heron problem with the example's rail and holds. Inputs for a hit include its tick, direction, magnitude and point on the boss. The boss starts braced on level ground. A second hit may arrive during recovery. No design may anticipate an unannounced hit. Aim, grip and damage timing belong to the game. Candidate-only functions are described below their code.

Costs below are estimates for f32, one pose sample per channel per frame, eight feet, and four secondary channels unless specified. A multiply-add counts as two scalar operations. Special functions, fetches, dispatches and synchronization are listed separately where material. Common mesh drawing, IK and frame construction remain on every bill. A two-link IK alone costs roughly 50–90 scalar operations and several length, divide or square-root operations per leg, before mesh evaluation. Compiler sharing and the GPU receiving the work determine the frame time. None of the new designs has a measured GPU time here.

For author cost I count visible lexical tokens per snippet (identifiers, numbers, strings, operators and delimiters), with shared geometry and fixture definitions excluded. This is a reproducible proxy for model tokens. Asset creation, hidden rigging and controller tuning are charged separately. Names unique to the scene do not become engine vocabulary. ## §3. Follow the parent analytically and optimise effort at build time

```ts
const feet = gait({ ...walk, course: turn })
const roll = bspline(time, [0, 2], shellRoll)
rig(feet, roll, spring(roll, { f: 2, zeta: 0.65 }), spring(roll, { f: 4, zeta: 0.3 }))

const leap = leapSpec(deck)
const bird = solve({ ...leap, prefer: { jerk: 1, effort: hindLegs } })
heronPose(bird.curves)

const kick = pendulum({ length: 0.8, gravity: 9.81, damping: 0.35 })
boss.lean(kick.answer(time, hits.map(h => jolt(sub(time, h.at), h.speed))))
```

The claw and antenna channels are applied to their respective mirrored pivots by the fixture. The roll here is a keyed artistic choice through the turn. A scalar spring follows an unwrapped angle in radians and returns the *absolute* followed angle. A child already carried by the roll needs the relative correction, followed angle minus parent angle, in its local frame. Aiming and grip can override it. Applying both angles in full doubles the rotation. The boss excerpt uses the existing impulse response and a fixed number of host event slots. Signed speed is the hit projected onto the chosen pivot axis, zero for an unused slot. For this design the two new public concepts are `spring` and `prefer.effort`. Visible tokens are **65 / 35 / 53 (crab / heron / boss)**. The author must also supply the mechanical model behind `hindLegs`.

For a constant frequency ω = 2πf and damping ζ, choose a position-target law, x″ + 2ζωx′ + ω²x = ω²u. It differs physically from a damper attached to a moving base, which adds a base-velocity term. On one polynomial span find the cubic particular solution by coefficient matching, then add the homogeneous response matching incoming x and x′. Carry those two values to the next span during the build. Underdamping needs a decaying sine and cosine, critical damping a linear factor times one exponential, overdamping two decaying real modes. Near repeated roots use the limiting expression or a stable series with a bounded error. For a finite clip start at the first target with zero velocity and let the final constant target settle. A loop requires a periodic initial state from the one-cycle affine map; refuse a singular or nonunique periodic solve. At reset, decide which of these performances the author requested.

A critical qualification is the source curve. `keys` constructs a monotone cubic or linear interpolant, samples **64 vec4s**, then returns a row lookup. The driver actually drawn is linearly interpolated between row samples. Filtering the artist's original Hermite curve would follow something different. Treat the 63 linear spans as given. A row edit invalidates all subsequent spring boundary states. Likewise `bspline` accepts moving controls, `through` accepts moving points, and the Titan's body includes trigonometry, time warps and host inputs. Admit fixed spans under an affine clock at the boundary. A changing parent, changing frequency or non-affine clock requires an explicit sampled bake with its rate, a live stepped implementation, or a named refusal. The proposed generic fallback to `recur` cannot follow Keel. A finite nonlinear build driver can be sampled with a declared approximation error if available. Sampling alone supplies no error bound.

The resulting lookup needs a segment index, four particular coefficients and two homogeneous constants per scalar span. With uniform spans and shared ω,ζ, budget 24 packed bytes or 32 bytes in two vec4s, 15–30 scalar operations and up to one exp, one sin and one cos per underdamped channel. Four channels × 12 spans × 32 B = 1,536 B. The state at the boundary is static data. Build work is linear in spans, roughly 48 small coefficient and transition evaluations here. Add no dispatch if evaluated in an existing pose stage. Hoist the four angles there before drawing the many antenna stations. Uniform lookup is a proposed lowering. The existing spline builds a tree of selects over polynomials, and the compiler may evaluate multiple sides. Inspect emitted WGSL before quoting indexed costs. Identical clock and inputs permit direct seek; small f32 and transcendental differences across devices still require picture parity.

Effort changes the solver much more than the spelling suggests. Given coordinates q, forces λ at contacts and actuated joints selected by S, inverse dynamics requires M(q)q″ + C(q,q′) + g(q) = Sᵀτ + Jᵀλ. A bird described by hip position, pitch and foot targets has no declared inertias, centres of mass, joint axes, support-force split or torque scale. Multiplying linear acceleration by mass yields force. Even the static hip torque changes when a claw reaches farther out. Supply masses, centres, inertias, contact schedule, admissible friction and a rule for distributing load. Normalise torque by a stated capacity before blending its squared integral with time-scaled jerk. Squared torque can reward straight locked legs or introduce a sudden load transfer. It does not model muscle activation or co-contraction.

Today's jerk/acceleration integral gives a constant quadratic over controls, assembled once by Gauss–Legendre integration. For torque the objective and derivatives change each iterate. Twelve gaps × four quadrature points give 48 inverse-dynamics evaluations **per objective sweep**, at least 14,832 for 309 sweeps before line searches, derivatives or load allocation. That quadrature is approximate for trigonometric articulated dynamics. Preserve the recorded holds, work budget and f32 rounding. Solve in Rust, record an objective declaration and its model on the internal wire, and keep V1 playback as ordinary coefficients or pose expressions. The checker must inspect those same final curves. A runtime table fetch currently outside its proof vocabulary needs an equivalent checked expression with a rounding enclosure, or a named unsupported/undecided outcome. Cost decrease, even at all quadrature points, proves neither equilibrium nor global optimality. For overlapping live hits, supply the history in bounded slots or let the host fold old linear responses into displacement and velocity at the latest event. Eviction of a still-ringing hit changes the pose.

**Decision experiment.** First compare hand gait against a minimum-jerk crab using identical plant windows, turn and duration. Compare the solved heron with a hand-posed hop at its takeoff and landing times. Then run a two-by-two ablation on the solved clips, toggling spring and effort independently. Before adding an objective, evaluate inverse dynamics on the existing heron and one deliberately stiff alternative under the same load model. If the proxy prefers the stiff landing, revise the model before running any search. A unit step with ζ = 0.5 and f = 2 Hz should overshoot 16.3% at 0.289 s. Compare the generated angle there and on both sides of edited knots. My prediction is a visible gain on the crab stop from the continuous driver and a smaller gain from effort on the base bird. Ship the two decisions independently.

## Alternative 1. Construct the motion between changes of support

```ts
const crab = support(gait({ ...walk, course: turn }), { height: 1, lean: 0.7 })
rig(crab, crab.roll, claw.answer(time, crab.drives), antenna.answer(time, crab.drives))

const bird = support({ stand: [0, 1.9], off: [0.6, 0.85, 1.92],
  land: [1.2, 2.45, 2.48], rest: [1.8, deck.land, deck.top + 1.9], gravity: 9.81 })
heronPose(bird, tuckOver(rail, bird, deck))

const recovery = support({ at: hit.at, from: hit.pose, velocity: hit.afterVelocity,
  feet: hit.anchors, rest: boss.rest, duration: 0.45 })
boss.pose(recovery)
```

Start with what is fixed in the picture. During stance a particular patch of ground supports a particular foot. During flight nothing supports the body. The new capability is to construct a root trajectory with velocity carried across those boundaries, then recover the legs with the existing chain. The example uses one proposed author name, `support`, distinguished by gait, timed launch/landing, or a captured landing/recovery state. Keep the three constructions as scene functions until a common return value and repeated uses justify an export. Visible tokens are **57 / 68 / 47 (crab / heron / boss)**. `claw` and `antenna` are existing pendulums with different lengths or stiffnesses. `tuckOver` is heron-specific foot choreography, charged to this design. It must specify the toe path, pitch and start/end anchors. A root parabola cannot clear the rail on the bird's behalf.

For the turn, begin with the gait's existing world anchors. Split the timeline at footfall boundaries. Within double or multiple support, transfer a normalised share of weight to the receiving feet with a smoothstep. The weighted anchors give a support centre. For a reduced level-body model at fixed height h, the centre of pressure implied by the desired horizontal acceleration is approximately c − h a/g, where c is the horizontal centre of mass. On a turn of radius 2 m at 1.4 m/s and height 1 m, this point is about **0.10 m outside** the mass centre. Lean the shell inward by a fraction of atan(0.98/9.81), about four degrees for the shown gain. Fit a short cubic lateral transfer between support centres, biased inward by that offset, and carry its endpoint velocity into the next segment. Check the pressure point of the *constructed* curve against the active polygon and check the friction demand. The formula alone cannot guarantee either. If the support is only a line or point, test that geometry directly. An impossible turn should identify the missed foot or required slower speed.

For the ornaments, take the change in velocity across each loading pulse and approximate it by a jolt at its centre. Two neighbouring pulses of opposite sign give overlap into the stop. Put their known prehistory into the clip or its periodic boundary. For an unlimited walk with damping, either precompute the periodic contribution or carry aggregate state at the last change. Cutting the pulse list to the previous cycle leaves a residual. The approach inherits the pendulum, the course and analytic IK. Its proposed contribution is to have the plant determine a body transfer and the body transfer determine the input to the loose parts. This removes independently phased bob, roll and whip from this walk. The crab's idle, communicative antenna flick and claw closure remain artistic channels.

For the hop, the sample endpoints completely determine flight. In 0.6 s, vx = 2.667 m/s, vy at takeoff = 3.876 m/s, touchdown vy = −2.010 m/s and apex y ≈ 2.686 m at t ≈ 0.995 s. Construct contact on either side by integrating two consecutive half-duration constant accelerations. They satisfy displacement and end velocity through a two-by-two linear system per axis. In the first 0.6 s, rise only 0.02 m from rest and leave at 3.876 m/s. The vertical accelerations are roughly −6.24 and +19.16 m/s². At their change the bird has crouched 0.281 m and is still descending. It reaches its lowest point around 0.398 s, about 0.372 m below standing. Upward contact forces for the two halves are approximately 0.36 and 2.95 body weights. Round the force changes over a specified duration and refit the two amplitudes to preserve the endpoint moments. Keep the force nonnegative, bound friction, check reach and rail clearance, and account for the difference between a visible hip and an articulated centre of mass. Vary the foot tuck or use the existing solver to fit the free channels when they collide.

For the boss, Keel supplies the pre-hit visual displacement and velocity (evaluated from the previous finite segment), world anchors, and post-hit velocity including the projected impulse J/m. Start from the same position, then absorb momentum over a specified time. A second hit evaluates the active segment at its tick and starts a new one there. The host can reconstruct this small segment from its own logged inputs with shared pure maths; it never asks the GPU for a pose. A recovery with fixed feet has a finite reach envelope. A larger hit requires a new support foot placed by the game or a named out-of-range result.

**Cost and ownership.** Sort at most 16 foot boundaries per eight-foot cycle and build one small record per interval. A clip with 16 transfers storing xyz cubic coefficients costs 16 × 3 × 16 = 768 B, plus times, anchors, and orientation. Building the hop requires three scalar linear systems per contact phase; even including a clearance scan at 128 times this is thousands of operations plus IK. For uniform phase or binary lookup expect 1–4 index comparisons, about 18 flops for xyz cubic evaluation, another 20–50 for orientation and two to four exp/sin pairs per ornament if using recent pulses. A cached 8-foot anchor block adds 128 B per block at vec4 stride. One pose stage, zero extra dispatches if fused. Memory and work are bounded by the authored segments. A live instance needs the initial conditions, anchors and tick of its active segment, roughly 192 B. Clip seeks index time directly. Live seeks need an event log or the segment at that time. Replays with quantised ticks and versioned coefficients are stable within declared numeric tolerance.

Record comparisons and polynomial expressions today, lower fixed tables and construction in Rust after the port. No new persisted format is needed for ordinary constants, graph functions and frame inputs. Verify each domain including the limits from both sides of contact; acceleration may jump. Keep contact checks on drawn feet after IK. A force balance on a point mass supports a claim about that model only. Test the quarter-turn and both decks. Predict the 0.37 m crouch, inward lean and <1 px planted-foot travel in the fixed game camera. If the moved bird requires manually repositioning the launch or an extra pose for every rail, count those edits. If viewers still prefer the existing heron, keep the support construction for walking and seed the existing bird solver with its parabola.

## Alternative 2. Retrieve a performed phrase and preserve its contacts

```ts
const crab = match(crabTakes, { course: turn, feet: walk.feet, action: "walk" })
rig(crab.feet, crab.roll, crab.claw, crab.antenna)

const bird = match(heronTakes, { from: ground, to: deck, over: rail, landAt: 1.2 })
heronPose(bird)

const flinch = match(bossTakes, { from: hit.pose, support: hit.anchors,
  direction: hit.direction, strength: hit.strength, at: hit.at })
boss.pose(flinch)
```

Here the picture comes from a performance. A heavy outside claw can stay high for two beats because that particular crab is showing off. No law of minimum effort or spring constant contains that decision. Store root-relative task coordinates for foot, claw and antenna targets, body pose and contact labels in a small rig-specific library. Search short windows at support changes or hits, align the incoming root to the world anchors, and blend only free channels. Carry planted foot positions until their recorded release. Play back with the existing chain. Motion matching inherits the search by current pose and desired trajectory. The proposed restriction to tagged, creature-specific phrases makes a small database and inspectable failure possible. [Holden et al., Learned Motion Matching, §3](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/09154101/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)

The new name is `match`. Visible tokens are **41 / 29 / 45 (crab / heron / boss)**, conditional on owning the takes. The library author pays for channel correspondences, root frame, timing, contact intervals, allowed time stretch, per-feature scales and transition masks. For a first bank, an artist supplies a straight walk, left and right turns with asymmetric claws, a hop for each of two decks, and four signed hit responses. That is nine short performances for three rigs. A mirrored fiddler changes handedness, which is visible and may change the gameplay-facing claw. Count separate takes or an explicit retarget edit. Existing authored Titan and heron performances are useful seeds, but retrieving their own samples cannot improve their acting. Author one distinctly better pose sequence or this direction has no source of improvement.

At a transition reject candidates with incompatible planted feet. Rank the rest by scaled pose and velocity differences, desired root displacements, direction and impact phase. Deterministic ties go to the lowest stable asset/frame index. Align two anchors in the ground plane when possible. Three incompatible anchors cannot generally be aligned by a rigid transform. Preserve the individual world targets and check actual reach. During flight, retime the free legs but reconstruct the ballistic root from boundary positions and duration, then recheck the rail. At impact sample the currently blended pose and match the next phrase from it. Reject a bank lacking the appropriate hit side or support phase, or have the author explicitly choose their fallback performance. Return the chosen source range, alignment and measured errors.

**Cost and ownership.** A deliberately uncompressed bank of 16 two-second takes at 60 Hz and 24 vec4 lanes costs 737,280 B before labels, about 720 KiB per rig. With 12 f32 query features per candidate frame the index adds 92,160 B. Brute force over 1,920 frames costs about 69k scalar operations for squared distances (subtract, square, accumulate), plus filters and tie breaking. At four decisions/second this is about 0.28M arithmetic operations/s per active boss on the decision CPU. Building features is linear in samples; fitting 16 assets or commissioning them sits outside the 2 s training build and must be accounted for separately. Playback per interpolated lane uses two vec4 fetches and about 12 flops. At 24 lanes this is 768 B read and roughly 288 flops per pose before alignment, IK or a two-phrase transition, which can double the reads. No new GPU pass when sampled in the pose stage. For an authored clip, preselect phrases in Rust and store a schedule. Any time can be sought with the schedule, including its entrance anchors. Keel can instead send the chosen phrase IDs, start ticks, blend and locked anchors, about 160–224 B per instance. Record decisions and bank hash in replays so a later index rebuild cannot change an old hit.

Use ordinary f32 tables in the bundle. The TS recorder references versioned source samples. The Rust build validates correspondence, labels and ranges and packs the existing resources. A game that chooses phrases needs the feature index on its side and a small deterministic matcher, independent of the renderer/compiler. Charge that extra distributed asset to deployment; do not conceal it inside an artist's `match` call. Persisted V1 scenes can continue to draw using their own tables. For a fixed scheduled clip prove holds on the final interpolation, warp and IK, including transition intervals. A validated source take or a nearest-neighbour distance does not certify the result. Live checks are conditional on a declared input domain or a specific logged trace. If proof cannot read the table, report the limitation.

**Decision experiment.** Author one expressive outside-claw turn and two hit phrases on opposite sides. Hold out a tighter radius and request two hits 0.12 s apart. Predict the chosen turn is preferred at its recorded radius and preserves visible asymmetry. Expect a larger problem at the second hit, when the library lacks the entering pose. Score anchor travel and silhouette discontinuity at the splice, and time the author's edit for the held-out turn. If the expressive advantage disappears after alignment or every new turn requires another take, stop at reusable, directly indexed scene clips. A learned prior would first need a bank that already clears this test.

## Alternative 3. Give each creature a small simulated body

```ts
const crab = simulate(crabRig, { targets: gait({ ...walk, course: turn }), ground, dt: 1/120 })
rig(crab.feet, crab.roll, crab.claw, crab.antenna)

const bird = simulate(heronRig, { targets: leapTargets(deck, rail, 0.6, 1.2), ground: platforms,
  dt: 1/120 })
heronPose(bird)

const body = simulate(bossRig, { targets: boss.guard, ground, dt: 1/120,
  impulses: hits.map(h => [h.tick, h.point, h.impulse]) })
boss.pose(body)
```

The appeal here is the second hit. An actual off-centre impulse changes translation and angular momentum from whatever state the first hit left behind. The near shoulder recoils first, the far claw keeps moving and a slipping foot finds a new plant. Simulation earns its place if it reproduces a *family* of such shots under changing contacts. Use driven position constraints for joints and posture, unilateral ground constraints and limited tangential correction. Give each antenna two or three light links. Clamp muscle-like drives to a documented maximum and relax them briefly at impact. A high-gain controller that instantly restores the target defeats the entire exercise. The inherited numerical method can be XPBD with compliance scaled by the squared substep. Its multiplier is a numerical constraint variable; the bank of drives is an artistic controller. [Macklin, Müller and Chentanez, XPBD](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf)

One new scene verb, `simulate`, costs **49 / 36 / 52 (crab / heron / boss)** visible tokens. The rig contract is the largest of the four: body masses and inertias, joint rest frames, permitted axes and limits, contact geometry, friction, target attachments, gain or compliance, drive limits and event units. State explicitly whether positions are in metres and whether the supplied impulse is world-space N·s. `leapTargets` must provide a timed push, flight posture and landing target; a floor constraint cannot tell a heron to jump a rail. Reuse the present gait as a controller and the drawn geometry as its skin. Delete bespoke shell recoil and delayed appendage curves only for rigs whose simulated performance wins. A badly weighted rig can take more author time than all of those curves together.

**Cost and ownership.** Size the smallest credible test before considering a physics dependency. Budget 24 short rigid links, 48 scalar constraints including active contacts, two 1/120 s substeps per 60 Hz display frame and eight projection sweeps. That gives 768 scalar constraint projections/frame. Allow 80–200 scalar operations per projection including rotational Jacobians and writes. Add 100–200 operations/link/substep for prediction, velocity recovery and motor targets. The resulting **66k–163k scalar operations/boss/frame**, or about 2.8k–6.8k per link, exclude broad-phase collision; the test has a fixed floor and a small fixed list of obstacle proxies. A six-axis joint consumes several scalar rows. Count actual active rows and sweeps before extrapolating to the full eight-legged Titan. Two arrays of 24 × 64 B for aligned positions/orientations and linear/angular velocities take 3 KiB. Add multipliers, anchor/lock state, inverse masses, rest frames and contacts, roughly another 2–5 KiB plus controller data.

Target one workgroup per creature, with a fixed ordering of conflict-free constraint colours and barriers between colours. Four colours × eight sweeps × two substeps = 64 colour barriers, in addition to integration and state loads/stores. Colours depend on the joint graph; verify four suffices before using that estimate. A dedicated kernel can keep a whole island local and run both ticks in one dispatch for the normal frame. Using the existing Steps scheduler naïvely costs two dispatches, potentially more for constraints spanning groups. Time the barriers on a mostly idle group. A bad frame at 20 Hz needs six ticks and roughly triple the projection work. Cap work or delay presentation by an explicit host policy, recording which ticks were presented. Dropping physical ticks silently changes the outcome.

Building a rig validates positive inertias, connectivity, rest frames and colour assignments in roughly linear work for bounded degree. It also compiles a compute pipeline and warms it at load. A two-second baked take costs 240 substeps × 8 × 48 = 92,160 projections before sampling; do it outside the draw or use the existing bake schedule. Rendering can read latest state directly or consume derived frames after stepping. Today's Rust host dispatches frame kernels before advancing Latest. A frame kernel reading new joints there would see the previous pose. Express the dependency in the Rust schedule, or read final state directly in the draw. The current multi-instance path refuses live Latest kernels. The first test uses a single separately owned instance; joining five bosses in one pass requires individual mutable resources and step counters. Record rig/controller data on the internal wire. Existing V1 compute kernels, bindings and buffers cover the small island if the required order can be expressed. Otherwise version the schedule, with a reader or migration for persisted bundles. The Keel process continues to own authoritative collisions and only forwards impulses.

Fixed tick, stable ordering, duplicate-event suppression and no cross-thread floating-point atomic accumulation make replay tractable. They do not guarantee bitwise equivalence of GPU backends over thousands of ticks. A 4 KiB checkpoint each half-second costs about 480 KiB/minute per boss and bounds seek cost to 59 intervening ticks plus interpolation at 120 Hz if tick 60 has a checkpoint. Include velocities, active contacts, persistent multipliers if warm-started, controller phase and input cursor. Checkpoint indices and ordered input logs belong to the session, shared rig data to the bundle. Rewind-and-resimulate must consume historical inputs at each tick. A one-frame input spike read by every catch-up substep can apply one hit six times. Use sequence IDs and tick windows.

A proof about finite sampled projections is not a continuous contact guarantee. Fixed-step collision can tunnel through a thin rail, an iterative contact can penetrate, and a motor can miss the deck. Report penetration and tracking error on the actual tick/trace as diagnostics with units. For a released, baked heron, fit or interpolate the output, then independently prove the existing holds on exactly what is drawn. Keep undecidable margins named. For live author WGSL without a checkable twin, a request for an inter-frame clearance guarantee gets a typed refusal. The cheapest experiment needs just the torso, two feet, one claw and two antenna links against a plane. Hit the shoulder with two equal impulses in opposite directions 0.12 s apart, vary support and replay at 30/60/120 Hz. Predict a visibly different angular reaction for equal impulses on opposite sides of the centre of mass, no repeated hit during catch-up, and a resolvable extra GPU stage. If the existing analytic hit reads as well or the controller cannot recover its guard within the authored window, keep the experiment local.

## Ranking and phased decision

| Rank | Direction | First scene that could justify the cost | What would reverse the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
