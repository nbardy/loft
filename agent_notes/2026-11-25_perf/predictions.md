# Predictions before scoring the candidate

The inputs are the five fixed-camera poses in the perf brief. All cameras set eye and at literally. The final Strand and Rod already have closed-form visible queries. Whole-frame time, fresh visibility and repeated visibility answer different questions.

1. On a newly rendered view, 62.5 mm proof cells and at least 1 mm padding should dominate analytic strand proxy size. A tiny primitive straddling a cell junction can pick up 125 mm per axis before rotation. At 1 m with a 38 degree vertical FOV, 125 mm spans about 131 pixels vertically at 720p. A 3 mm fibre spans about three. Diagnostic overdraw clips at 16 layers. Count all fragments and trace steps, including cap exits, before assigning milliseconds.
2. For a fixed view whose draw list and visibility shaders depend only on immutable geometry, a changed frame clock cannot change Vis64 or D32. Reusing these attachments should execute zero Vis commands after frame one and remove the entire Vis pass. From the lead's measurements the predicted warmed p50s are 3.0 ms for ball, 1.5 ms for base otter, 2.5 ms for walrus, 2.3 ms for heron and 2.7 ms for final otter at 960×720. At 1280×800 the predicted character p50s are 3.7, 3.4 and 4.0 ms (pixel-area extrapolation). Predict all three under 16.7 ms p95 in both sizes. New cameras and poses pay for fresh visibility. Require bit-identical RGBA for reuse.
3. Independently calibrate each field at a camera rotated 7 degrees, distance multiplied by 1.1, 672×504, four overlapping draws. For a fresh 960×720 view scale each isolated cost (subtract the empty attachment pass, divide by four) by the ratio of target to calibration field evaluations, or proxy fragments for analytic kinds. Add the clear/store once. No fit to target times. The goal is ±30% for each scene. The model assumes similar SIMD occupancy and frequency between cameras. It can fail for tiny triangles because partial quads and per-row vertex cost do not scale with shaded area.
4. Fresh-view experiment in forked Loft source. Both rod solvers already rebase the ray to the midpoint; test the enclosing sphere before computing the roots of the caps and side. This retains the old proxy triangles, ray interpolation and hit point calculation. For analytic miss-dominated layers predict a 1.5–2× speedup of the cone fragment and 1.1–1.3× for capsules. Predict no change for ball and heron, 0–1 ms for base, 1–4 ms for walrus and 15–35 ms for final otter from the brief. Hash differences disqualify the fork from the exact-picture result.
5. The all-ray counter changed the fresh-view ranking. HeronHead alone executes 59.15 million field evaluations on misses and 0.14 million on hits. Its proof box is 128 m wide. Walrus Hide and Cap alone execute 50.16 million miss evaluations inside 512 m and 192 m boxes. Starter Otter spends 48.95 million evaluations on misses in a 128 m box. A second, smaller ray/box test should reject most of those rays or stop them shortly after the character, while leaving original march entry and steps intact. A pose-specific Loft ablation should save 28–40 ms for heron, 10–18 ms for walrus, 7–11 ms for starter otter, 25–45 ms for final otter, zero for ball. Compare first-frame and moving-clock RGBA before calling it equivalent. These hand-sized boxes are an experiment for a compiler proof.
6. The independent isolated-draw model predicts 1.24, 23.52, 47.76 and 31.74 ms for ball, starter, walrus and heron. The first three native attempts put starter at 14.26–15.03 and walrus at 23.11–26.53 ms. Before further engine edits, revise the rate instrument. Four consecutive overlapping isolated draws have a different execution history from 30 warmed, 120 timestamped frames of the complete renderer. Profile the rotated 672×504 calibration cameras with the native CLI. Use that calibration Vis to normalize the sum of isolated work at the calibration camera, then scale only by counted work from calibration to the target view. Report the original errors and the normalized target predictions separately. This is a shader/scene-family calibration, not a universal primitive throughput constant.
7. Also fit two shared nonnegative rate multipliers to the five independent 672×504 native calibration views, one for analytic proxy fragments and one for marched field evaluations. Subtract attachment cost first. Use ordinary least squares in relative calibration time, with no intercept. Freeze those two multipliers for all five 960×720 target views. This scores whether per-scene normalization is actually necessary and avoids calling five camera-specific normalizations a universal M4 rate.
8. The sun-only integrator ignores AO. Metal is free to remove all five AO field samples when it inlines that integrator, so attributing the ball's entire roughly 1.7 ms shade difference to the twelve sky directions is ambiguous. At 960×720 compare the original sun and sky against the same 14 evaluations with AO fixed to one, and sun-only against sun-only using AO on the direct term. Predict 0.1–0.5 ms for keeping AO live and 1.2–1.8 ms for the twelve-direction lighting loop at the original view. These illumination controls change the image by design.
9. The final counter gives 69.75M miss evaluations for Head4, 48.68M for Torso and 109.30M analytic Strand invocations for 8,247 hits. Torso is about 40 ms of the observed Vis at the isolated rate, larger than either Head4 or Strand. The head-only ablation leaves Torso untouched. Predict another 24–36 ms of fresh Vis savings from bounding Torso as well. A 45–60 ms aggregate saving still leaves the strands above a 16.7 ms fresh-frame budget.
10. The otter isolated empty pass read 0.431 ms, higher than four draws of Eye or Water. Subtracting it implies negative work. Keep the original 158.20 ms forecast, but use the median empty-pass measurement across all five identical-size calibration targets for revised rates and the additive attribution. Report the outlier instead of allocating negative milliseconds to useful hits.
11. The normalized 672×504 instrument overshoots the first four 960×720 native Vis measurements by 51–96%. At half the pixels the native renderer spends almost as many milliseconds on the same kinds. A model that transfers a fixed rate across resolution and system load has failed. Test a held-out camera at the actual 960×720 raster. Count all rays at the rotated calibration view, keep the per-kind rates, and scale the rotated native time by the ratio of weighted work. Alternate each independent calibration with its original view in a single GPU slot. Write the prediction to disk before launching the original camera. Score against both the original series and this untrained paired run. Only the camera changes in the pair.
12. Apply the same two-coefficient nonnegative fit to the five full-resolution rotated views as a second test of a shared rate. Use the independently collected analytic-fragment and trace-step counts at those views. This fit never sees an original-camera timing. Keep the per-camera forecast as a separate result. A zero class coefficient signals that the five views do not identify its hardware rate.
13. The independent guard counters preserved every geometrical hit and reduced Head4 work by 62.08 million evaluations and Torso by 40.28 million. At the original per-kind shares those imply 21.65 ms saved for Head4 and another 30.80 ms for Torso. The first fork timings were taken in different operating windows and the combined score was slower than the head-only score. Measure original / head / both in forward and reverse order at 960×720 within the final otter GPU reservation, after its fixed-camera controls. Keep both runs and their flags.
