# Predictions before scoring the candidate

The inputs are the five fixed-camera poses in the perf brief. All cameras set eye and at literally. The final Strand and Rod already have closed-form visible queries. Whole-frame time, fresh visibility and repeated visibility answer different questions.

1. On a newly rendered view, 62.5 mm proof cells and at least 1 mm padding should dominate analytic strand proxy size. A tiny primitive straddling a cell junction can pick up 125 mm per axis before rotation. At 1 m with a 38 degree vertical FOV, 125 mm spans about 131 pixels vertically at 720p. A 3 mm fibre spans about three. Diagnostic overdraw clips at 16 layers. Count all fragments and trace steps, including cap exits, before assigning milliseconds.
2. For a fixed view whose draw list and visibility shaders depend only on immutable geometry, a changed frame clock cannot change Vis64 or D32. Reusing these attachments should execute zero Vis commands after frame one and remove the entire Vis pass. From the lead's measurements the predicted warmed p50s are 3.0 ms for ball, 1.5 ms for base otter, 2.5 ms for walrus, 2.3 ms for heron and 2.7 ms for final otter at 960×720. At 1280×800 the predicted character p50s are 3.7, 3.4 and 4.0 ms (pixel-area extrapolation). Predict all three under 16.7 ms p95 in both sizes. New cameras and poses pay for fresh visibility. Require bit-identical RGBA for reuse.
3. Independently calibrate each field at a camera rotated 7 degrees, distance multiplied by 1.1, 672×504, four overlapping draws. For a fresh 960×720 view scale each isolated cost (subtract the empty attachment pass, divide by four) by the ratio of target to calibration field evaluations, or proxy fragments for analytic kinds. Add the clear/store once. No fit to target times. The goal is ±30% for each scene. The model assumes similar SIMD occupancy and frequency between cameras. It can fail for tiny triangles because partial quads and per-row vertex cost do not scale with shaded area.
4. Fresh-view experiment in forked Loft source. Both rod solvers already rebase the ray to the midpoint; test the enclosing sphere before computing the roots of the caps and side. This retains the old proxy triangles, ray interpolation and hit point calculation. For analytic miss-dominated layers predict a 1.5–2× speedup of the cone fragment and 1.1–1.3× for capsules. Predict no change for ball and heron, 0–1 ms for base, 1–4 ms for walrus and 15–35 ms for final otter from the brief. Hash differences disqualify the fork from the exact-picture result.
