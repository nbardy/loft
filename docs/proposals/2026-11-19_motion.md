# Motion in Loft: what we have, what we want, a first proposal (19 Nov 2026)

Status: proposal for audit. Nothing here is built. Read `CORE.md` first: the picture is the product, GPU frame time is
what speed means, the author writes the least code, nothing Loft reports may be false.

## 1. What exists

All motion today is **kinematic**: closed-form functions of time (or of host inputs) evaluated where they are read,
mostly in the vertex and pose stages on the GPU. Nothing has mass or momentum unless a hold states it.

| Piece | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `keys`, `bspline`, `spline` | `src/curve.ts`, `src/geom/solve.ts` | Keyed and B-spline curves of time. |
| `gait` | `src/geom/gait.ts` | A walk clock: each foot lifts and plants on a phase (duty ~0.66), body bob/sway/roll are sines of that clock. |
| stance | `src/geom/stance.ts`, `docs/legacy/stance.md` | A sum type over carriages (`idle`, `brace`, `dance`, `walk`), blended by envelopes; feet planted at homes and moved only by named events (re-plants, stamps, the walk). Every term a closed form of the clock. |
| `limb`, `chain`, `poseFrame`, `turnedAbout` | `src/geom/limb.ts`, `src/geom/placing.ts` | Analytic IK (three-segment limb), frames and chains. Knees fold from IK when the body moves over planted feet. |
| `solve` | `src/geom/solve.ts`, Rust `loft-core/src/solve.rs` | Motion from constraints. Unknowns are uniform cubic B-splines (C2, n numbers each); holds are conditions on position, velocity or acceleration (`equal`, `ge`, `always([time,a,b], …)`); objective is the integral of squared jerk or acceleration (exact Gauss-Legendre quadratures). Augmented Lagrangian, Newton inner steps, exact symbolic Hessians, LM damping. Runs once at build; output is spline coefficients (numbers) in the bundle. Holds are `Unproved` in a build, proved by `loft check`. heron_leap: 309 steps, 117 ms (loaded). |
| `iterate`, `recur`, `tabulate`, compute(steps) | `src/expr.ts`, `src/map/recur.ts`, `src/map/table.ts` | Loops in the graph; stepped recurrences with kept tables and checkpoints; tables filled at build, load, frame or steps; raw WGSL compute at any of those times. The escape hatch for anything stateful (simulation). |

Frame cost of motion today: evaluating splines and closed forms in the pose/vertex stage, a few hundred flops per
part. Solve costs nothing per frame.

Known limits:
- Hand gait and stance read rhythmic and procedural; nothing carries weight or overshoots.
- Solve finds the smoothest curve meeting the holds (minimum jerk is the classic model of human reaching), but it is
  local (picks a side only via `spline(n, from)`), knows no mass, and heron "moved" stops before the true optimum.
- No secondary motion: claws, antennae, tails, feathers and flesh move rigidly with their parent.
- No reaction to the game at run time beyond host inputs feeding closed forms; anything reactive needs `recur`/compute.

## 2. What we want

Natural motion that reads as alive and weighted: anticipation, follow-through, overlap, settle, muscle tension and
release, contact that holds. Authorable in a few lines, cheap per frame on the GPU, deterministic, editable after the
fact, and honest (a hold Loft reports as met is met). Usable both in authored clips (bosses, creatures) and live in a
game (Keel), where some motion must react to the player.

## 3. A first proposal (the thing to audit)

1. **Analytic secondary motion, `spring`.** A damped second-order spring driven by a parent signal (stiffness and
   damping ratio, or frequency and ratio). Because the driver is piecewise cubic (keys, splines, solve output), the
   response of the linear ODE to polynomial forcing is closed form per segment: evaluate at any `t` with a few
   `exp`/`sin`, no stepping, no per-frame state. A non-polynomial driver falls back to a `recur` (stated, not silent).
2. **Muscle-like primary motion: `prefer: { effort }` in solve.** Minimise the integral of squared joint torque
   (mass times acceleration plus gravity torque on a per-segment mass model) instead of, or blended with, jerk.
   Still a build-time precompute.
3. **A look round first.** Hand gait vs solve (minimum jerk) side by side on the crab walk with a turn and the heron
   hop, then the same with springs and effort, before anything is ported to Rust.

4. **Interop with motion from outside, not running models.** Loft does not run motion models. Users who use them
   (generators like NVIDIA MotionBricks, mocap, other tools) must be able to bring motion in and take poses out
   cleanly:
   - **In, baked:** a clip of joint tracks sampled at a rate (quaternions or angles plus a root path; from BVH,
     glTF animation, SMPL/SOMA-style parameters or plain arrays) becomes curves (fitted splines or a table), read on
     the GPU like any curve. One import, not one per format family.
   - **In, live:** a host streams poses into a per-frame input table; the model runs wherever the user runs it.
   - **Retarget:** a skeleton map (joint names, rest-pose alignment, scale) from their rig to the Loft rig;
     non-humanoid maps are the user's to state.
   - **Correct:** an imported clip can be projected onto holds with `solve` (`prefer: { near: imported }`): feet stay
     planted, contacts and timing hold, and checks still prove what they claim. A generated clip is never reported as
     meeting a hold it has not been checked against.
   - **Out:** read back the posed skeleton (joint frames per frame or over a span) so a user's model can take Loft
     motion as its context and targets.

Open questions the proposal does not answer:
- Is a damped linear spring enough, or do we want nonlinear (muscle activation, tendons, contact)?
- Live, reactive motion in a game: per-event re-solve, a learned or tabulated controller, or full simulation on GPU?
- Who owns state: pure functions of time (scrubbable, deterministic, cacheable) vs stepped state (reactive, not
  scrubbable without checkpoints)?
- How does an author say "this part follows that one with weight" in one line, and how does a model learn it?
