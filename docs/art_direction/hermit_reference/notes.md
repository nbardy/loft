# Hermit crab: how the shell and the body line up (owner's reference, 2026-11-12)

Two photographs the owner gave as the target for how the hermit crab (`islander`) wears its spiral shell: "study the
way the shell and body line up; ours should line up better from multiple views". `hermit_front.webp` (three-quarter
front) and `hermit_side.webp` (side). Reference only: not to be published or redrawn closely.

What they show:

- **The crab comes out of the shell.** The shell's mouth is at the front. The head, eyestalks, claws and legs emerge
  from it; the rear of the body is inside and never seen. The shell is not a thing set on top of a whole crab.
- **The lip is a hood over the back.** From the front the lip is one clean arc that starts low on one side of the body,
  passes over the carapace just behind the eyes, and comes down the other side. The body's top surface runs in under it
  with a dark gap of shadow between.
- **The shell is behind and above, and as big as the crab.** From the side the shell is about half the animal's length
  and taller than the body; its bulk hangs behind the legs and its underside is down at the ground.
- **Everything leaves through the mouth.** All the legs fan forward and to the sides from under the lip. No leg comes
  out of the shell's wall or from behind it.
- **The shell's axis points back and to one side**, a little up: the spire is at the rear, the coil's face on one
  flank. The body's axis and the mouth's are the same line.
- **The claws fold in front of the mouth** like doors: the great claw rests across the opening.
